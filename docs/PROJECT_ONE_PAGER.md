# YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED (PC Simulator)
**A browser and WebXR narrative work about how organised "conversion" networks adapted to the internet.**
*SurvivingSOGICE · University of Bergen, Center for Digital Narrative · led by Sérgio Roxo*

## The premise
You sit at one computer, in one room, and you never leave the chair. Over roughly thirty years the room
ages around you and the software keeps updating. In the piece's own words: *"You will follow different
lives through thirty years of one machine… What the software is for is the thing you are here to see."*

## The argument
**The apparatus is never defeated in this story; it is upgraded.** Every era change arrives as a software
update — a notification, a licence agreement, an install, a restart — and the changelog is the thesis in
four lines: *same instrument, new casing.* The vocabulary softens from prayer to therapy to wellness to
machine learning, the interface gets friendlier, and the function does not change. That is why the work
is a *simulator* of a personal computer rather than a story told on one: the update is the plot.

## What is in it
**Four eras, three lives, one building.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **1997** | a gay teenager | a shareware kit, a chat room, a residential placement |
| **2003** | the same man, older | an accountability platform, a friendship the system reads as evidence |
| **2016** | a lesbian moderator | a content-correction queue: she is now inside the machine, applying it to others |
| **now** | a trans woman | an AI assistant, a "restoration" photo filter, and a record holding a name she does not use |

Between them: a **helper character** that softens across the decades (Helpy → Aski → Sol → Ami™) and can
always be dismissed; a **dossier** of sourced cards behind the fiction; and a closing constellation where
the rooms are finally seen as one building among many.

## How you interact
**The only thing your body does is turn.** There is no walking and nothing is timed. You click on an
in-world screen, and you turn to look at the room the screen is talking about — the piece's one bodily
demand, and the moment its argument lands: the machine describes a person, and you have to look at where
that person lives. It runs three ways from one build — a headset, a desktop browser, or a phone or tablet
you physically turn like a 360° video.

## The rules it is built under
These are ethics constraints, not style preferences, and they are enforced in the build:
- **Satire only inside the perpetrators' self-presentation, and it must collapse.** The people targeted
  are never the joke.
- Every scene is marked **operable** (system surfaces, which may charm), **felt** (the person: bare, no
  assistant, no mechanics), or **respite** (real queer joy — never a trap, never revealed as fake).
- **No real people, likenesses or survivor testimony.** Invented marks throughout; real organisations are
  named only where the sources document them, and only on dossier cards carrying a status of
  *documentary*, *contested* or *speculative*.
- **Nothing you type is stored, and nothing leaves the machine.** No network calls after load, no
  cookies, no analytics — a piece about surveillance that collects nothing, verified by its own tests.
- The photo "restoration" filter never touches a real camera or a real file.

## Why it is made this way
Conversion practices are usually depicted as a historical abuse or a foreign one. This work argues they
are a **contemporary, networked, and increasingly automated** phenomenon, and that the softening of their
language is a feature of their persistence rather than evidence of their decline. Putting the audience in
the seat — as the target, then as the person applying it to others — is a claim that the machinery works
because it is ordinary, patient and well-designed.

## Status
Playable end to end in a browser, in active development. Its most sensitive passage is gated on review by
a trans reader before it ships, and all display text awaits the project lead's final pass. Built with
substantial AI assistance, disclosed as part of the work rather than hidden.
