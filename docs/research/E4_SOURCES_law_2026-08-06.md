# E4 Sources — the legal counter-current (conversion-practice bans, Malta 2016 → 2026-08-06)

Research pass for Era 4 / the ending's "bans that DID pass" beat. Scope: source-verification only, no story text. Compiled 2026-08-06. Every row below is a claim with a URL attached to it; where I could not resolve a date or a scope question against a primary source, I've said so explicitly rather than rounding it up — per the task's own rule, "could not verify" is a finding, not a gap to paper over.

A general caution about the whole file: most of what follows was retrieved via web search + fetch, not by holding the original PDF/gazette text in hand for every entry. Where I quote a primary source directly (legislation databases, parliamentary records, official gazettes) I say so; where I'm relying on a news outlet's or tracker's summary of that primary text, I say that instead. Treat trackers (Stonewall, ILGA-Europe, Equaldex, Global Equality Caucus, Wikipedia's "Legality of conversion therapy") as secondary and cross-check anything load-bearing for the piece against the primary cite before it goes on screen.

## 2. Sub-national bans (summarised, not exhaustive)

- **Australia**: no federal ban; state/territory bans in Queensland (2020), the ACT (2020, reported in force 2021), Victoria (2021, reported in force 2022), and — much more recently — **South Australia** (Conversion Practices Prohibition Act 2024, reported in force 1 Apr 2025) and **New South Wales** (Conversion Practices Ban Act 2024, reported in force 4 Apr 2025). All secondary-sourced in this pass.
- **United States**: no federal ban. A secondary tracker count (as of Sept 2024) lists 23 states plus DC and Puerto Rico with bans, nearly all restricted to licensed professionals treating minors — this is a materially different (narrower) instrument than the criminal, all-ages bans in e.g. Belgium or Iceland. See §3 for the 2026 Supreme Court complication.
- **Mexico**: federal ban (2024, §1) sits on top of a patchwork of state-level bans reported to number roughly 22 of 32 states, enacted across 2020–2025 — I did not verify this state-by-state.
- **Switzerland**: no federal ban. Reported cantonal bans in Neuchâtel, Valais and Vaud (2023–2024), secondary-sourced only.
- **United Kingdom (Crown Dependency)**: the **Isle of Man** reportedly banned conversion practices in 2024 (effective March), ahead of and separate from Westminster — see §3 for the England & Wales situation, which is not law.

## 3. The failures, delays, repeals, and weakenings — equal prominence

This section is the one the anchor claim depends on most: a list of only victories would misrepresent the record.

**United Kingdom — repeatedly promised, never delivered (as of 2026-08-06)**
- 2018: a ban first promised, under Theresa May's government, as part of the LGBT Action Plan.
- Dec 2021 / early 2022: reported delay "over criminalisation fears."
- April 2022: the government announced it would **not** proceed with a ban at all.
- April 2022 (same month, reversed under pressure): decision reversed, but the reworked plan **excluded transgender people** from its scope.
- Jan 2023: coverage for trans people reportedly added back in.
- Early 2024: reports the plan had effectively stalled again ahead of the general election.
- July 2024: the incoming Labour government's King's Speech renewed a commitment to legislate, covering both England & Wales.
- June 2026: the government published a **draft** Conversion Practices Bill — the criminal threshold proposed is conduct that aims to change someone's sexual orientation or transgender identity through abusive acts that seriously harm the victim, up to 5 years / unlimited fine. This is explicitly at the **pre-legislative scrutiny** stage as of this research pass — parliamentarians, survivors and legal experts are still reviewing it. **No ban is in force in England or Wales as of 2026-08-06.** This is an eight-year gap between promise and (still incomplete) delivery, and it is the clearest single example in this research of a promised ban not being delivered. Sources: House of Commons Library briefing (page exists at https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9972/ but returned 403 to automated fetch in this pass — read it directly, don't rely on my paraphrase alone), plus contemporaneous news coverage (Stonewall UK, gov.uk announcements, The Conversation, Scene Magazine).

**Portugal — a passed ban now under organised repeal pressure**
- Lei 15/2024 (§1) is in force, but a petition seeking its repeal, reportedly carrying close to 17,000 signatures, was delivered to the Assembleia da República on 18 Mar 2026, arguing the law's definitions are "vague and contradictory" and that the banned practices are "natural care relationships," not criminal conduct.
- This has **not** resulted in a repeal bill being formally tabled or passed as of the sources reached (early April 2026) — a large counter-petition (reported ~50,000 signatures) opposing repeal followed.
- Separately — and importantly, **not the same law** — a package of three anti-trans bills (repealing gender self-ID, requiring medical diagnosis for name/gender changes, banning gender-affirming care for minors, re-permitting non-consensual intersex surgery) passed a first preliminary reading in the Assembleia 20 Mar 2026 (151–79, PSD/Chega/CDS-PP in favour), and is still moving through committee. Do not conflate this package with the conversion-therapy law itself — they are politically adjacent but legally distinct instruments, and only the anti-trans package has actually advanced in parliament; the conversion-therapy repeal has not.

**European Union — a resolution, not a law**
- A European Citizens' Initiative on banning conversion therapy gathered over 1.2 million signatures (collected since 2024).
- The European Parliament voted 29 Apr 2026 (405 MEPs in favour) to back an EU-wide ban and referred the matter to the European Commission — the only EU body that can propose binding legislation.
- The Commission's formal response, document C(2026) 3333 final, dated 13 May 2026, **declined to propose a binding directive**. It acknowledged conversion therapy as harmful and "based on a medically false premise," but chose to commit instead to a non-binding **recommendation**, expected in 2027 — a directive would bind member states to transpose it into national law; a recommendation does not. One outlet framed this explicitly as a political choice rather than a legal necessity, citing the Council's unanimity requirement as the stated obstacle. **There is currently no EU-wide binding ban, and the Commission has explicitly chosen not to pursue one before 2027.**

**United States — a Supreme Court ruling that narrows, not necessarily kills, state bans on minors**
- *Chiles v. Salazar*, decided 31 Mar 2026, 8–1 (Gorsuch for the majority; Kagan concurring, joined by Sotomayor; Jackson dissenting). The Court held that Colorado's ban on conversion therapy for minors, as applied to a licensed counsellor's talk therapy, regulates speech based on viewpoint and must be reviewed under **strict scrutiny** — the most demanding First Amendment standard.
- This is **not** an outright strike-down: the case was remanded, and the ruling explicitly did not invalidate bans in other states outright. But it forces every state-level talk-therapy ban to be re-litigated (or preemptively rewritten) under strict scrutiny.
- In direct response, **Colorado itself amended its law in May 2026**, narrowing the definition of prohibited conversion therapy to conduct that "directs a patient toward a predetermined sexual orientation or gender identity outcome" — a real-time example of a ban being weakened in reaction to a legal challenge, not repealed outright but narrowed under pressure.

**Israel — legislative failure, then a regulatory workaround**
- Knesset bills to ban conversion therapy were voted down in both 2016 (37–45) and 2017 (26–38), reportedly blocked by ultra-Orthodox parties.
- What exists today (from 2022) is a **Health Ministry directive** restricting the practice among licensed professionals — a regulatory instrument issued by the executive branch after the legislature twice declined to pass a law, not a Knesset-passed ban. Worth being precise about that distinction if the piece uses Israel as an example.

**Brazil — an attempt to go backward**
- Brazil's 1999 Federal Council of Psychology resolution against "curing" homosexuality is widely cited as the first professional-conduct ban of its kind anywhere. In 2013, a bill was introduced that would have effectively **legalised** conversion practices for psychologists again; it was withdrawn/voted down after public objection. Treated here as a documented attempt to reverse existing protection, not merely a failure to advance one.

## 4. International bodies and professional associations

- **World Health Organization**: removed homosexuality from the ICD in 1990 (pre-dates this file's window but underlies everything after it). ICD-11, adopted by the World Health Assembly 25 May 2019, moved "gender incongruence" out of the mental-and-behavioural-disorders chapter entirely (into a new chapter on sexual health) — a direct rebuttal of the premise conversion therapy relies on. https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/17-05-2019-moving-one-step-closer-to-better-health-and-rights-for-transgender-people
- **PAHO (WHO's Americas regional office)**: position statement "'Cures' that Harm," 17 May 2012 — no rigorous scientific evidence such therapies work, and documented association with depression, anxiety, and suicide risk. https://www.paho.org/en/news/17-5-2012-therapies-change-sexual-orientation-lack-medical-justification-and-threaten-health
- **UN Independent Expert on SOGI** (Victor Madrigal-Borloz): report to the Human Rights Council, A/HRC/44/53 (2020), based on roughly 130 submissions, documenting practices including beatings, rape, electrocution, forced medication, isolation, forced nudity; called such practices "inherently discriminatory," capable of amounting to torture depending on severity, and called for a coordinated global ban. https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2020/07/un-expert-calls-global-ban-practices-so-called-conversion-therapy
- **Council of Europe**: Commissioner for Human Rights issue paper "Nothing to Cure" pre-dates this file's core window but frames later action. More recent and squarely inside the window: **PACE Resolution 2534 (2026)**, "For a ban on conversion practices," adopted 29 January 2026, calling on member states to adopt legislation with **criminal** sanctions and a clear, comprehensive definition, plus monitoring/reporting mechanisms and survivor-support resourcing. https://pace.coe.int/en/files/35742/html — note PACE is a parliamentary assembly, not the Council of Europe's executive; its resolutions are recommendatory to member states, not self-executing law.
- **European Parliament**: named the EU an "LGBTIQ Freedom Zone" in 2021, at which point it noted conversion practices reported in member states and recalled its position that they should be criminalised; escalated in the 29 Apr 2026 vote (405 MEPs) described in §3 — again, a resolution referred to the Commission, not a binding instrument, and the Commission has since declined to make it one before 2027.
- **Professional associations**: American Psychological Association reaffirmed opposition to conversion therapy May 2024; in August 2025 the APA (joined by the American Psychiatric Association and 12 further mental-health/medical bodies) filed an amicus brief in the Chiles v. Salazar litigation presenting evidence that SOGICE practices are not legitimate treatment, are potentially harmful, and lack credible scientific support. WPATH's Standards of Care is the most widely cross-referenced clinical-guidance document opposing "change" efforts for gender identity specifically. The American Medical Association has also published an issue brief opposing SOGICE (https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/conversion-therapy-issue-brief.pdf).

## 5. Cross-border relocation: documented vs. asserted

This is the section the task flagged as resting on thin sourcing, so I've kept the "what's actually shown" and "what's merely stated" separate rather than blending them.

**Genuinely documented (investigative, named actors, reproducible)**
- **CBC Marketplace (Canada), investigative report, ongoing coverage from 2022**: an undercover CBC producer, posing as a client, was offered online conversion-practice coaching by a named U.S.-based life coach (Rich Wyler, Virginia) despite Canada's federal ban being in force. The investigation is explicit that online delivery from a U.S. provider to a Canadian client sits outside the reach of Canada's Criminal Code amendment, because the provider operates outside Canadian jurisdiction. This is the strongest single piece of documented evidence in this file for a ban being circumvented via cross-border online delivery — a real investigation, a named practice, a specific mechanism (jurisdiction arbitrage via teletherapy). https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/marketplace-life-coach-conversion-therapy-1.6369104
- **The Progressive / Finbarr Toesland, Feb 2025** (republished by 76crimes.com Mar 2025): documents specific organisational activity — the Core Issues Trust's "Africa Project," attendance and resourcing at GAFCON IV (Rwanda, April 2023) by delegates from Uganda, Rwanda and Nigeria, the International Foundation for Therapeutic and Counselling Choice's 2,000+-signature declaration, and the Bethel Church (Redding, CA)-linked "Changed Movement." This documents Western-based conversion-practice organisations actively building capacity and resourcing in African countries with weaker or no protections — a real, sourced pattern of practice/resource export, though it is about **organisational expansion**, not literally people crossing a border to receive treatment abroad after a ban closed off their home country. https://76crimes.com/2025/03/24/exporting-conversion-therapy/

**Asserted, plausible, but not tied to a specific documented instance in what I could reach**
- A recurring line across several secondary sources ("a patchwork of gaps has allowed the practice to persist and relocate across borders") — I could not trace this to a specific documented relocation case beyond the two items above.
- A claim, found only via search-result snippets and not confirmed against the original article text, that organisations began operating in **Switzerland** specifically **after** Germany and France passed their bans. I was blocked (403) from the source (worldcrunch.com) that carried this claim and could not verify its sourcing or specificity. **Do not use this as a confirmed fact** — it's exactly the kind of claim the task warned about resting on one journalistic assertion, and here I can't even confirm the assertion held up under its own citation.
- Rainbow Railroad's framing of *Chiles v. Salazar* as "conversion therapy as a tool of displacement" is real organisational commentary, and Rainbow Railroad does cite its own caseload data (nearly one in three of its assistance requests reportedly mention conversion therapy, disproportionately from the U.S.) — but the specific "displacement" example given in that piece is **proposed Ghanaian legislation** that would mandate conversion therapy as an alternative to imprisonment, which is a threat of state-mandated conversion driving people to flee, not documentation of practices relocating in response to a ban elsewhere. Different mechanism from what Era 4 is claiming; don't conflate the two.
- A University of Alberta "Folio" piece, "Conversion practices continuing despite federal ban" (title only reached, not the body) — likely relevant to the Canada picture, worth a dedicated follow-up read since I did not get past the headline in this pass. https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2024/03/conversion-practices-continuing-despite-federal-ban.html

**Bottom line for this section**: the CBC Marketplace investigation is real, specific, and directly on-point for "online delivery evading a ban." The Toesland/Progressive piece is real and specific for "organisational export to a country with weaker protections," though it's about capacity-building rather than an individual crossing a border. Everything beyond those two is pattern-level assertion in secondary sources, and the Switzerland claim specifically could not be verified at all in this pass. If Era 4's ending needs one clean, defensible cross-border example, the CBC investigation is the sturdiest foundation available; treat anything more sweeping ("bans just push it elsewhere," stated as a general law) as the piece's own interpretive claim, not a documented fact.
