# ERA 4 — THE DEVICE, AND WHETHER SHE PUTS IT ON
*Sérgio, 2026-08-05: "we can even make a play with a VR headset aesthetic, creating the room as if it
is an XR mixed-reality device — or better, her putting it on? Or is this against the whole narrative
of what we are doing?"*

**Short answer: it is not against the narrative. It is the terminal state of the narrative — and it
is the answer to the one question Stage 0 could not resolve.** But only in a bounded form, and the
reason is the piece's oldest law.

---

## 1 · ⚑ Why it fits, and it is not because the piece is in VR

Start with his other note from the same message, because it is the stronger one:

> *"it's symbolic of our own existence as audience — this is happening in front of us, so news,
> videos, stuff, is all offered to us."*

**A headset is the exact object for that sentence.** It is the only piece of hardware whose entire
purpose is that content arrives at your face and you do not go to it. Three eras have shown a machine
you sit at, a program you open, a job you work. **The fourth shows a thing you wear**, and the
apparatus has finally closed the last distance.

And it resolves things the design was already reaching for:
- *"the room itself speaks"* and *"the room rewrites you — objects re-captioned, spoken now"* is
  literally what a passthrough overlay does: **it labels reality.**
- **`REINTERP_E4_AUDIO_FIRST_DESIGN` already asks for an "all-in-one" that does not exist in the data.
  This is what it is.** Stage 0's first question is answered.
- Gaze and pinch is **almost touchless** — his own note about E4, arrived at independently.

## 2 · ⚑ THE REAL ARGUMENT — the device is how the apparatus finally gets past the turn

The piece has one bodily law: **you never walk, you turn.** For three eras, turning away has always
worked. The machine is on a desk. The queue is on a laptop. **You can always look at something else.**

**A thing on your face comes with you when you turn.**

> E1 the apparatus is in the room · E2 it is in the machine · E3 it is in the work ·
> **E4 it is in the direction you are looking.**

That is not a betrayal of the mechanic — **it is the mechanic's last move**, and the only escalation
left after "you are obsolete." The player will discover it without being told: they will do the thing
they have done for thirty years of story, and it will not help.

### And then the ball gives it back
**TRANSCENDANCE is where turning works again.** Whether it comes off, or fails, or simply has nothing
to say — at the ball there is nothing between you and the room, and the one gesture the piece has
ever asked for returns in the only place that wants you.

**The input budget bottoms out, the turn stops working, and both come back once.** That is the
cleanest arc E4 has, and it came out of two of Sérgio's notes meeting.

---

## 3 · ⚑ THE COST, stated plainly — this collides with a decided law

CLAUDE.md, under *do not re-litigate*: **one UI surface.** All interaction lives on the offscreen 2D
canvas, textured onto a screen mesh. *"The 3D room is staging, not UI."*

**A full mixed-reality overlay breaks that**, and the law is not decoration — it is why the piece is
legible at Quest resolution, why `?flat=1` works as a universal fallback, and why every era has
shipped.

### So: two versions, and I recommend the first without hesitation

**⚑ OPTION A — the visor IS the screen. (Recommended.)**
The headset is a real object in the room. She picks it up and puts it on. **And the 2D canvas the
piece has textured onto a monitor for three eras is now textured onto the visor.**

- **The one-surface law is untouched.** Same canvas, same `FILTER_NEAREST`, same pixel discipline,
  same `?flat=1` fallback. Only the mount point changed — which is exactly the argument the piece has
  been making all along: *same machine, new form.*
- The overlay in the room stays **sparse and diegetic**: a few object captions, large, anchored, and
  never a UI. Everything with structure stays on the surface.
- **Cheap.** It is a prop, a mount change, and a small caption layer.

**❌ OPTION B — the player is inside her view, full passthrough UI.**
Breaks the one-surface law, breaks `?flat=1`, puts small text in world space at 72 Hz, and — the real
problem — **makes the piece about VR.** Its subject is a conversion apparatus that now has these
tools. If a beat only works because the audience is in a headset, it is off-target.

---

## 4 · The three ways this goes wrong, and the guards

1. **⚑ The device must never be the villain.** This is not a critique of headsets. The apparatus would
   use whatever was on your face — it used mail, then a CD-ROM, then a moderation queue. **If a beat
   would read as "VR bad," cut it.** The horror is continuity, not novelty.
2. **It must not become clever about its own medium.** *The frame never plays.* A headset inside a
   headset is one step from the piece winking at the audience. **Guard: nothing in the work ever
   remarks on the coincidence.** No line, no visual gag, no nod. If a player notices, that is theirs.
3. **`?flat=1` and the browser build must lose nothing.** Most people will meet this piece on a
   desktop, and Option A survives that intact because the canvas is still a canvas.

## 5 · ⚑ And one thing to decide with your eyes open
The piece seats the player *as* the person of each era — E4's design says *"Maya answers by chip."*
So **her putting it on is you putting it on.** That is what makes the beat land, and it is also why
the deadname advisory and the trans reader pass matter more, not less: the moment you accept the
device, the piece has you wearing the apparatus.

**My read: that is the right amount of uncomfortable, and it is honest** — the piece never pretends
you are outside this. But it belongs on the same page as the reader pass, and it is yours to confirm.

## 5 · The touchless budget, made concrete
"Almost touchless" is a number, not a mood. **E4's entire era ships with roughly a third of E3's
interactions**, and the shape matters more than the count:

| | who advances the beat |
|---|---|
| **E1–E3** | the player, almost always. Insert, press, apply, reply, publish. |
| **⚑ E4** | **the system, almost always.** L begins the next thing while you are still in the last one. |

**The rule for the build:** *if a beat can advance itself, it does.* The player's presses are reserved
for three things only — **answering L by chip** (which is not navigation, it is being talked to),
**the memories undo** (the dismissal law, which must always work), and **the turn**.

**Nothing else. No menus, no confirmations, no "continue."** The era is the only one where the piece
moves without you, and that is what obsolete feels like from the inside.

## ⚑ And TRANSCENDANCE has no screen at all
The ball is not watched through the device — decided above. **So it is not watched through anything.**

Four eras have been surfaces: a CRT, a program, three device screens, a visor. **The last thing in the
era has no screen in it.** The ball arrives as sound and light *in the room*, and the only thing you
do is turn.

That costs no new geometry, it is the strongest possible statement of the contrast, and it is why the
one input comes back there: **there is no interface left to come between you and it.**
