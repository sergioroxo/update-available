# PULL 1 — Peer accountability groups in ex-gay / ex-lesbian networks

## FINDINGS

### 1. Documented form of peer accountability groups

* [DOCUMENTED] **Closed membership is a genuine feature of ex-gay ministry groups, rather than an invented “secret cell” device.** Desert Stream/Living Waters describes its programme as a closed-group format, explicitly justifying closure through confidentiality and the development of trust. Its twenty-week programme combines a larger mixed-gender gathering with smaller same-gender groups. — Desert Stream/Living Waters, “Who We Are” and FAQ. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The small group is normally embedded inside a structured programme rather than existing autonomously.** Living Waters meetings combine worship, teaching, prayer, and same-sex small groups; Portland Fellowship similarly describes “Christ-centered accountability groups,” trained peer-to-peer support, testimonies, and counselling referrals as components of its ministry. ([desertstream.org][2])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Facilitation/leadership is normal.** Living Waters explicitly names coordinators, small-group leaders, assistant small-group leaders, intercessors and pastoral overseers, and requires leaders to be trained. This is stronger evidence for a facilitator structure than for a completely leaderless circle of peers. ([desertstream.org][3])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Accountability can also be peer-to-peer rather than leader-to-member.** Portland Fellowship explicitly describes “trained peer-to-peer support”; wider evangelical sexual-purity practice uses a designated “accountability partner” or several trusted people who receive reports and ask questions. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][4])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Weekly structured meetings are well evidenced.** Living Waters is a twenty-week programme; Portland Fellowship's Taking Back Ground programme met weekly and ran for months; Harvest USA describes women's support groups as combining sharing, biblical discussion, prayer, mutual encouragement and accountability. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I cannot currently substantiate a standard ex-lesbian ministry form of “four to six women in a continuous phone thread checking in every day” in 2010–2018.** Daily accountability is documented in the adjacent Christian sexual-purity ecology, but I did not locate an organisational ex-lesbian source establishing daily group messaging as a standard practice.

* [DOCUMENTED] **Very small accountability relationships could be substantially more continuous than the weekly programme.** Christian accountability materials from the same ecology describe Internet-use reports going to selected friends/partners, with discussions triggered by questionable activity; Covenant Eyes advertised reporting from computers, smartphones and tablets to one or more trusted people by 2014. This is sexual-purity evidence, not proof of an ex-lesbian messaging-group norm. ([Covenant Eyes][5])

### 2. Mechanics of accountability

* [DOCUMENTED] **Disclosure is explicitly part of the programme.** Living Waters asks participants to be willing to be “open and honest” about sexual feelings and practices, identity, emotional immaturity, hurts involving parents and other forms of what the programme calls “brokenness”; confession is an explicit curriculum theme. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The accountability field extends beyond sexual acts.** Desert Stream's leadership application separately asks about homosexual feelings, significant homosexual relationships, romantic/sexual thought life, emotional dependency, codependency, pornography, masturbation and gender identity. That makes a historically plausible fictional check-in broader than simply “did you have sex?” ([desertstream.org][3])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Adjacent Christian sexual-purity accountability interrogates temptation and anticipatory risk as well as behaviour.** Covenant Eyes' accountability guide asks members to assess pornography, sexualised material, “second glances,” sexual thoughts, known triggers, seeking sexual attention and potentially compromising interpersonal situations. ([Covenant Eyes][6])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Disclosure can be directed to a designated accountability person rather than broadcast to the entire group.** Covenant Eyes allowed activity reports to be sent to chosen spouses, friends, siblings or multiple partners; its own case materials describe trusted friends questioning the monitored person about reports. ([Covenant Eyes][5])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Group disclosure also occurs.** Living Waters combines confession/healing prayer in small groups, while Harvest describes women's group meetings as including “a time for sharing,” prayer, mutual encouragement and accountability. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [DOCUMENTED] **A reported lapse is framed primarily as something to confess, pray through, understand and continue working on, not automatically as grounds for expulsion.** The core Living Waters repertoire is honesty, confession, forgiveness, healing prayer, boundaries and “walking out” of patterns. ([desertstream.org][7])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I have not found an ex-lesbian organisational rulebook from this period specifying a formal escalation ladder—e.g. first lapse → warning, second lapse → sponsor call, third lapse → removal.** Do not invent such a disciplinary ladder without another source.

* [DOCUMENTED] **Progress vocabulary is processual rather than simply “cured/not cured”:** “healing,” “wholeness,” “freedom,” “walking out,” “transformation,” “maturity,” “healthy relationships,” and identity “in Christ” recur across Desert Stream, Portland Fellowship and Restored Hope material. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **A consistent movement-wide term for someone who leaves a group is not established.** “Prodigal” is common in family ministry for an LGBTQ-identifying loved one, but I would not use it automatically for a woman merely leaving an accountability group. ([Harvest USA][8])

### 3. Register — how members address one another

* [DOCUMENTED] **The prevailing register combines intimacy with process-language:** members are people who “struggle,” are “walking” a “journey,” seek “healing,” “freedom,” “wholeness,” “truth” and “grace,” and receive “prayer,” “encouragement,” “support” and “accountability.” This wording appears directly in organisational descriptions. ([desertstream.org][2])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The person is often linguistically separated from the sexual identity.** Movement materials favour “same-sex attraction,” “struggling with homosexuality,” “unwanted same-sex attraction,” “lesbian-identified,” or a person “affected by” lesbianism rather than accepting “lesbian” as a stable identity category. Robinson and Spivey's analysis identifies precisely this euphemistic pattern in ex-gay discourse about women. ([MDPI][9])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Warmth and behavioural scrutiny are not opposites in the accountability model.** Covenant Eyes describes surveillance reports as prompts for conversation with a trusted person, while Harvest explicitly puts “mutual encouragement” alongside “accountability.” ([Covenant Eyes][5])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Correction is characteristically framed as accompaniment rather than punishment:** “walk with,” “come alongside,” “encourage,” “care,” “pray,” “speak truth,” and “grace and truth” are recurrent organisational formulations. ([desertstream.org][2])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Scripture can appear either as direct quotation or as an embedded reference system.** Organisational materials frequently cite chapter-and-verse directly, but support-group descriptions also describe “discussion of God's Word” without implying that every interpersonal exchange consists of pasted Bible quotations. ([Harvest USA][10])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I cannot establish distinctive, standard ex-lesbian terms of direct address such as “sister,” “sweet friend,” “girl,” etc. from sufficiently strong 2010–2018 group-message evidence.** “Sister” would be culturally plausible in some Christian environments but should not be presented as documented ex-lesbian-group protocol on the evidence I found.

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I also cannot source fixed greeting formulas for private ex-lesbian phone groups.** The organisational register is well documented; actual private-chat pragmatics are not.

### 4. Specifically lesbian / women's strand

* [DOCUMENTED] **Women's ex-gay discourse did develop a recognisably different explanatory repertoire from the heavily masculinised “gender deficit/manhood wound” literature aimed at men.** A major recurring term was **“emotional dependency.”** Restored Hope Network still lists Lori Rentzel's *Emotional Dependency* alongside women-specific resources by Anne Paulk and Janelle Hallman. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][11])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The women's strand frequently interpreted lesbian relationships through emotional intensity, unmet relational need, dependency, enmeshment and wounded attachment.** Robinson and Spivey's scholarly analysis of ex-gay literature identifies “emotional dependency” as one of the movement's characteristic euphemisms for female homosexuality and documents the recurrent construction of women's same-sex relationships as dependent or excessively emotionally fused. ([MDPI][9])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Desert Stream material specifically links a woman's lesbian relationship with “emotional dependency,” femininity, insecurity, absent paternal affirmation, unsafe relations with men and attempts to secure identity through relationships.** This is evidence for the ministry's explanatory narrative, not evidence that these factors cause lesbian orientation. ([desertstream.org][12])

* [CONTESTED] **Father/mother wounds and deficiencies in gender development were treated by these ministries as causal or formative explanations for same-sex attraction, but these etiological claims are not established psychological science.** Living Waters nevertheless teaches “healing of father, mother and foundational wounds” and “roots of gender brokenness,” and asks participants to explore parental hurts. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Women's materials particularly emphasised learning or recovering an allegedly authentic femininity.** Anne Paulk's *Restoring Sexual Identity* is explicitly a women-specific Exodus-era text; movement bibliographies pair it with Hallman's *The Heart of Female Same-Sex Attraction*. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][13])

* [DOCUMENTED] **A contemporaneous critical analysis identifies this as more than generic Christian morality: the female ex-gay discourse connected lesbianism with improper femininity and treated heterosexual femininity as restorative.** Robinson & Spivey, “Putting Lesbians in Their Place,” *Social Sciences* 4(3), 2015. ([MDPI][9])

* [DOCUMENTED] **There was not one single women's theory.** Relevant strands include Desert Stream/Living Waters' sexual-and-relational “brokenness” model; Exodus/RHN women's ministry represented by Anne Paulk; Lori Rentzel's emotional-dependency framework; and Janelle Hallman's counselling model. RHN's resource taxonomy puts all of these in the same wider ecosystem. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][11])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Religious/private SOCE was especially important in people's actual experience, not only professional psychotherapy.** In a 1,612-person study of current/former LDS members published in 2015, private and religious change efforts were more common and longer-lasting than therapist/group approaches; 43% of women in that sample reported attempting orientation change. This is LDS evidence and should not be universalised to evangelical ministries. ([PubMed][14])

### 5. Movement onto phones and messaging apps, c. 2010–2018

* [DOCUMENTED] **By the middle of your period, evangelical sexual-accountability infrastructure had unquestionably moved onto smartphones.** Covenant Eyes' 2014 materials explicitly covered Android phones/tablets and iPhones/iPads and transmitted browsing/activity reports to selected accountability partners. ([Covenant Eyes][5])

* [DOCUMENTED] **This changed accountability from episodic self-report to potentially persistent observation.** The system could report sites visited, search terms, videos and—with Android implementations—app usage, turning online behaviour into material another person could later question. ([cleanheartinitiative.org][15])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Multiple observers were technologically possible.** Covenant Eyes explicitly advertised “unlimited partners,” and its own materials describe activity reports going to a small group of trusted friends. ([Covenant Eyes][5])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The accountability logic therefore became asynchronous:** the accountable person did not have to confess an event at the moment it happened for someone else to know that flagged activity had occurred. That follows directly from periodic activity-report architecture. ([cleanheartinitiative.org][15])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Online support provision also became normal within ex-gay successor networks.** Portland Fellowship now documents online programmes for adults and family/friends; Harvest documents online support groups. This demonstrates the institutional migration online, though these current pages do not prove the precise platform used in 2016. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][4])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **WhatsApp/Facebook Messenger as a standard ex-lesbian accountability technology in 2016 remains unverified from the sources I located.** I found scattered contemporary/near-contemporary Christian accountability practice using calls, DMs and online groups, but nothing strong enough to establish a movement-wide ex-lesbian WhatsApp norm.

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I cannot document from an ex-gay organisational source whether a departing member's exit notification from a WhatsApp/Facebook group was itself used pastorally or disciplinarily.** The UI behaviour—visible departure, retained backlog for remaining members, screenshots—is platform functionality, but its specific conversion-ministry use needs a separate source before becoming dossier fact.

### 6. Caricature risks

* [DOCUMENTED] **The record does *not* support treating every meeting as openly punitive humiliation.** Official programmes explicitly foreground confidentiality, prayer, mutual support, trust, encouragement and healing even while operating from a conversion-oriented ideology. ([desertstream.org][16])

* [DOCUMENTED] **It would also be inaccurate to make every interaction obsessed with sex acts.** Accountability encompassed feelings, fantasies, triggers, attachment, identity, relationships, family wounds, prayer and perceived spiritual condition. ([desertstream.org][3])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Do not portray participants as unaware that they are being accountable.** In the documented voluntary adult programmes, disclosure/accountability is explicitly presented as part of what participants sign up for. Criticism belongs in the coercive social context and consequences, not in inventing covert technology where sources show explicit consent. ([desertstream.org][1])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Do not flatten women's programmes into men's reparative-therapy vocabulary with pronouns changed.** The women's literature has its own strong cluster around emotional dependency, intimacy, relational wounds, femininity and attachment. ([MDPI][9])

## REGISTER NOTES

The safest base register is **pastoral-process language rather than drill-sergeant language**. The person is “struggling,” “walking,” “seeking,” “healing,” “growing,” “learning,” or “working through” something. The collective vocabulary is “journey,” “community,” “support,” “prayer,” “encouragement,” “accountability,” “grace,” “truth,” “freedom,” “wholeness,” and “identity in Christ.” ([desertstream.org][2])

For women, add the historically specific lexicon **relationship / attachment / dependency / boundaries / femininity / emotional need / wounds / healthy relationships**. “Emotional dependency” is especially useful because it is not a retrospective parody term: it is genuinely embedded in the literature and resource networks. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][11])

Surveillance does not normally announce itself linguistically as surveillance. It is made legible as **care**. A person is asked how she is doing because somebody “cares”; hard questions are an expression of “accountability”; disclosure means refusing isolation; intervention becomes “walking with” someone; scrutiny becomes helping someone identify “temptation” or “triggers.” That fusion is important. Covenant Eyes literally marketed automated behavioural reporting as a means of initiating trusted conversation. ([Covenant Eyes][5])

Likewise, correction need not sound angry. The structurally authentic shape is closer to **affirmation → naming the concern → spiritual interpretation → invitation to disclose/pray/continue**. That is an inference from the documented programme architecture, not a documented fixed script, so the exact sentence form should remain fictional.

Scripture is omnipresent at the organisational level, but the sources do **not** justify making every peer response a Bible verse. Group descriptions alternate direct scripture, prayer, informal sharing and discussion of “God's Word.” A believable fictional group can therefore know scripture almost by shorthand: sometimes quotation, sometimes chapter/verse reference, sometimes simply Christian vocabulary derived from it. ([Harvest USA][10])

I would avoid giving them a specialised cult argot unsupported by the record. Ordinary terms—*friend, group, prayer, struggle, journey, difficult day, temptation, accountability*—are safer than invented organisational jargon. Distinctive direct-address terms for women's ex-gay messaging groups are **[VERIFY SOURCE]**.

## WHAT WOULD BE A CARICATURE

A caricature would make five women behave like junior prison guards: numbered confessions, instant denunciations, robotic Bible quotations, formal “infractions,” a leader who speaks entirely in threats, and automatic expulsion after a sexual or emotional lapse. I found no documentary basis for that as the ordinary form of the ministries above.

Another caricature is **constant cruelty**. The troubling quality of these systems is precisely that warmth, friendship, vulnerability and surveillance can be materially genuine at the same time. Participants may be giving each other actual companionship while simultaneously reinforcing a framework in which lesbian desire is interpreted as temptation, injury, dependency or brokenness. Living Waters and Harvest explicitly organise accountability together with confidentiality, prayer and encouragement. ([desertstream.org][16])

A third is portraying the women as dupes who mechanically believe a theory fed to them. Qualitative research on SOCE shows complicated motives rooted in religion, belonging, identity conflict and community; religious/private efforts often lasted for years. ([PubMed][14])

Finally, avoid generic macho reparative-therapy clichés. A women-centred group obsessed with “building masculinity” would miss the documented female discourse around intimacy, emotional dependency, relational wounds, femininity and boundaries. ([MDPI][9])

## WHAT THE RECORD DOES NOT SHOW

There is **little accessible published evidence of the ordinary text-message traffic of private ex-lesbian accountability groups around 2016**. Organisational manuals describe structure; books describe doctrine; testimony describes experience; none gives me a defensible corpus of everyday WhatsApp-style conversation.

I therefore cannot verify group size for your exact fictional form, standard greetings, emoji practice, response times, whether silence triggered a direct message, who retained message histories, screenshot norms, or the exact linguistic ritual following a reported lesbian encounter. Those details remain **[VERIFY SOURCE]**.

The technological transition is much better documented for evangelical **sexual-purity/accountability software** than specifically for lesbian SOCE. Using that ecology to inform affordances is defensible if the piece labels it as adjacent evidence; claiming that an ex-lesbian organisation used Covenant Eyes or WhatsApp in exactly this fashion without a source would not be.

# PULL 2 — Family-contact practice

## FINDINGS

### 1. How organisations solicit and handle family contact

* [DOCUMENTED] **Family members are often treated as a distinct ministry constituency, not merely as collateral informants about the LGBTQ person.** Harvest USA maintains a dedicated Parents & Family Ministry; Portland Fellowship maintains a Hope Group specifically for family and friends; Restored Hope affiliates advertise analogous groups. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The family can therefore become a client in its own right even when the LGBTQ person has never contacted the organisation.** Harvest invites parents/family to make an appointment, join an eight-week group and receive one-to-one help; Portland Fellowship invites people whose loved one has “embraced” an LGBTQ identity into a separate discipleship programme. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Historically, parental demand helped create a separate family-support branch of ex-gay ministry.** Restored Hope's account of early Love In Action describes a mother contacting the ministry because the existing ex-gay ministry serving her son did not provide ministry to parents; the account presents this as a catalyst for networking around family needs. This is the successor movement's own historical narrative. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][19])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Incoming contact can begin simply as a distressed parent seeking advice.** A Southern Baptist ministry account from 2009 describes repeated calls from mothers, fathers, spouses and friends following a disclosure of same-sex attraction, including parents who had only just learned of it. ([Baptist Press][20])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Organisations actively solicit this contact by offering phone consultations, appointment forms, online groups and resources specifically labelled for parents/families.** Harvest and Focus on the Family both explicitly direct parents to staff/counsellors. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Referral works in both directions:** families can be referred to ministries by counsellors/churches, while ministries refer families or the LGBTQ person onward to pastors, Christian counsellors or affiliate programmes. ([Harvest USA][21])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I have not found evidence of a standard 2016 organisational CRM in which mothers are formally attached as “referral sources” to adult children's treatment records.** Your queue-of-family-messages interface is plausible as administrative fiction, but do not label that exact data relationship documentary yet.

### 2. What families are told and told to do

* [DOCUMENTED] **Parents are commonly told first to preserve the relationship rather than stage an immediate confrontation.** Focus on the Family's guidance explicitly prioritises protecting the relationship, assuring unconditional love, listening and avoiding pressure for an immediate resolution. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Parents are simultaneously told not to affirm a gay/lesbian identity or same-sex sexual relationship.** Focus frames homosexuality as behaviour/temptation rather than identity and advises parents to retain a conservative Christian sexual ethic while maintaining connection. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **A standard instruction is therefore “love without approval,” or its equivalent.** Focus explicitly distinguishes unconditional love/acceptance of the person from approval of homosexual behaviour; Portland Fellowship describes its family curriculum as learning to relate with “grace and truth.” ([Focus on the Family][23])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Parents are encouraged to listen, ask questions and avoid excessive intrusion.** Focus advises open-ended questions about attractions and identity but also says to step back if questions feel invasive. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Parents can be directed toward a third-party Christian counsellor or ministry even when the child has not independently sought one.** Focus explicitly recommends asking whether the child would speak with an experienced Christian counsellor and offers its own counselling department as a referral point. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Parents are told that they cannot personally force transformation.** Harvest material tells parents that change is attributed to God/Holy Spirit and should occur over time within Christian community rather than through parental argument alone. ([Harvest USA][24])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The parent is nevertheless cast as an ongoing influence or ministry presence.** Harvest's Parents & Family Ministry says it teaches family members “Christ-like ways to minister to the struggler”; Restored Hope affiliates describe equipping family members to “disciple” their loved one. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Prayer is not peripheral but operationalised as something the parent should continuously do.** Harvest explicitly positions prayer and the parent's own Christian practice as principal means of “ministering” to the loved one. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Family estrangement is recognised as a recurrent circumstance rather than automatically taken as proof that the family should intensify contact.** Contemporary Restored Hope-affiliated programming includes material explicitly titled “Walking through Estrangement.” ([Mid-Valley Fellowship][25])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I found no basis for a family worker routinely telling a parent how an independently enrolled adult child is “progressing” in confidential treatment.** Living Waters explicitly stresses confidentiality; family programmes are generally presented as parallel support for families, not windows into another adult's disclosures. ([desertstream.org][16])

### 3. Documented instruction to restrict or condition contact

This part needs the sharpest distinction.

* [DOCUMENTED] **There is clear documentation of organisations encouraging *conditions on behaviour within the parents' home*.** Focus on the Family advises conservative Christian parents of a gay-identified child living at home to establish rules against same-sex sexual/romantic expression in the home, including restrictions on displays of affection and expectations that household beliefs be respected. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **This “boundary” framework predates your 2016 setting.** A 2014 doctoral dissertation reviewing evangelical family guidance quotes Richard Cohen and Joe Dallas/Nancy Heche materials recommending equal heterosexual/homosexual rules regarding partners sleeping together and negotiating limits on same-sex physical affection at family gatherings. The dissertation gives page-specific citations to the underlying books. ([covenantlibrary.org][26])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Richard Cohen's family manual itself had an explicit programme of parental behaviour modification: improve communication, repair the relationship, alter same-/opposite-sex parental dynamics, create a welcoming home and address boyfriends/girlfriends/ceremonies/sleepovers.** This documents family coaching as a distinct intervention target. ([Open Library][27])

* [DOCUMENTED] **But influential conservative Christian ministry sources also explicitly warned *against breaking off the relationship*.** A Southern Baptist ministry article reports parents being advised elsewhere to break off contact, then rejects that advice and insists parents should reassure the child of continuing love and availability. ([Baptist Press][20])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Focus likewise says withholding love worsens the situation and encourages ongoing relational connection.** ([Focus on the Family][23])

* [ALLEGED] **Survivor/interview evidence does document conversion-practice providers advising people undergoing SOCE to distance themselves from LGBT or “liberal” friends and discouraging disclosure of their sexuality.** The UK government's qualitative study records multiple participants reporting such instructions. Because this comes through participant testimony rather than the providers' written protocols, the provider action is best dossier-labelled ALLEGED even though the fact that participants reported it is documented. ([GOV.UK][17])

* [ALLEGED] **The same UK evidence records people losing their faith-community support network after ceasing change efforts or accepting an LGBT identity.** Again, the reported exclusion is well documented as testimony; whether a particular organisation maintained a written expulsion policy requires organisation-specific evidence. ([GOV.UK][17])

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I did not locate a solid organisational document from the 2010–2018 ex-gay network instructing mothers generally to withdraw money, housing or all contact until an adult child renounced homosexuality.** Do **not** turn that into a routine staff script on the present evidence.

* [VERIFY SOURCE] **I also did not locate a reliable source for organisations routinely instructing families to route all communication with an adult child through the conversion organisation.** Such control may occur in particular residential/institutional settings, but it is not established here as a general family-ministry practice.

* [DOCUMENTED] **The more defensible pressure mechanism is conditional participation in family space rather than total no-contact:** maintain the relationship, continue influence, but impose Christian behavioural boundaries on home, partners, ceremonies and sexual expression. ([Focus on the Family][22])

### 4. Organisation's register when writing to families

* [DOCUMENTED] **The dominant register is pastoral with counselling vocabulary, not cold clinical case-management language.** Harvest speaks of “coming alongside,” “a difficult and long journey,” prayer, fellowship, God's timing, heart change and caring for the “struggler.” ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The opening emotional move is frequently reassurance of the parent.** Materials stress that the family is not alone, acknowledge shock/grief/confusion and present the organisation as a place of support. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The next move is often to reduce parental urgency:** do not force immediate resolution; listen; preserve connection; recognise limits on what the parent can control. ([Focus on the Family][22])

* [DOCUMENTED] **This is followed by theological reframing:** identity in Christ rather than gay identity; “heart change” rather than surface compliance; God/Holy Spirit as the agent of transformation; prayer and Christian example as the parent's role. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Administrative language enters mainly around logistics—appointments, confidential groups, curriculum, group dates, referrals and contact with staff—rather than around medical charts or diagnoses.** ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **“Grief” is a recognised organising concept in parent ministry.** Portland Fellowship explicitly lists “Dealing with Grief” alongside restoring relationships, communication and responding biblically to LGBTQ loved ones. ([portlandfellowship.com][28])

### 5. Who performs the work and what it is called

* [DOCUMENTED] **“Parents and Family Ministry” is an actual organisational label.** Harvest USA uses exactly that name. ([Harvest USA][18])

* [DOCUMENTED] **The worker need not be a licensed psychotherapist.** Harvest lists dedicated “Parents and Family Ministry Staff”; its recruitment material describes duties including one-to-one short-term care/discipleship, facilitating parent groups, producing resources and church teaching, with theological/counselling familiarity preferred but professional clinical licensure not required. ([Harvest USA][29])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Other organisations call the role ministry director, co-director, pastoral counsellor, spiritual mentor or peer/support-group leader.** Restored Hope's affiliate taxonomy distinguishes licensed therapist affiliates, pastoral affiliates, spiritual mentors and pastoral counsellors. ([restoredhopenetwork.org][30])

* [DOCUMENTED] **Portland Fellowship's analogue is the “Hope Group” / family-and-friends ministry.** The work includes teaching, facilitating discussion, receiving questions, prayer and accountability. ([portlandfellowship.com][28])

* [DOCUMENTED] **A worker answering a queue of mothers is therefore most defensible as a *family-ministry worker / parents-and-family ministry staff member / family support or discipleship worker*, not as the mother's child's therapist.** That distinction follows directly from how Harvest and Portland divide services. ([Harvest USA][18])

## REGISTER NOTES

For this scene, the strongest documented register is **pastoral counselling wrapped around administrative service work**.

A mother's message enters as a crisis—her daughter has stopped talking to her, will not answer, has moved in with a woman, does not want to discuss faith—and the organisation's language does not need to answer that urgency with equal urgency. The institutional voice repeatedly slows things down: *journey, pray, listen, remain connected, don't force, trust God's timing, show grace, hold to truth*. ([Harvest USA][18])

The family member is generally treated as hurting. Parent-facing materials speak to **shock, grief, fear, confusion, heartbreak, isolation** and the need to realise that other parents are going through the same thing. ([Harvest USA][21])

The queer relative, however, is linguistically processed differently: “your loved one,” “your child,” “the struggler,” someone “embracing an LGBTQ identity,” someone who has “adopted” an identity, or someone dealing with same-sex attraction. “Gay” and “lesbian” are often placed at semantic distance from the person's deepest identity. ([Harvest USA][18])

The core tension appears in pairings such as **love/truth, grace/truth, relationship/conviction, acceptance/approval**. Those binaries are extremely characteristic. The organisation does not generally tell the mother “reject her”; it tells her to remain available while refusing to alter the organisation's moral classification of the daughter's relationship. ([Focus on the Family][23])

Correction is also displaced upward: the mother is told she cannot *make* her child change; God changes hearts. This creates an interesting institutional voice because it can sound non-controlling while still orienting every relationship toward the hoped-for renunciation of the LGBTQ identity. Harvest explicitly assigns parents the role of reflecting Christ while describing the outcome as the Holy Spirit's work. ([Harvest USA][18])

For the staff interface, **“case,” “compliance,” “patient,” “treatment outcome,” “percentage recovered”** would sound too clinical for the ministries I found. **“Parent,” “family member,” “loved one,” “appointment,” “support,” “prayer request,” “resources,” “follow-up,” “group,” “referral,” “discipleship,” “journey”** are much closer to the documentary register. ([Harvest USA][18])

Crucially, a family worker plausibly knows **the mother's story because the mother told the ministry**. She does *not* automatically know the estranged daughter's confidential disclosures. Making that asymmetry visible would actually make your interface more faithful to the record.

## WHAT WOULD BE A CARICATURE

The easiest caricature is a worker gleefully instructing every mother to “cut them off until they repent.” That is too simple. Some conservative Christian sources explicitly rejected family estrangement as a strategy and stressed unconditional love and continued relationship. ([Baptist Press][20])

Another would be a quasi-medical call centre issuing progress percentages about absent adult children. The family ministries I found are better understood as **parallel pastoral systems**: the mother becomes the person receiving care, instruction and accountability. Confidential adult participant groups are separately described as confidential. ([Harvest USA][18])

A third caricature is making every reply overtly hostile toward the queer child. The documentary register is much more unsettlingly double: remain loving, listen, protect the relationship—but continue treating same-sex identity/relationships as spiritually erroneous and retain “biblical boundaries.” ([Focus on the Family][22])

And a fourth is portraying mothers simply as agents deployed by the organisation. The family-facing record repeatedly shows parents arriving frightened, grieving and desperate for a way to preserve their family and faith simultaneously. That vulnerability is part of why the ministry has leverage. ([Harvest USA][21])

## WHAT THE RECORD DOES NOT SHOW

I do **not** have strong evidence for a standard ex-gay organisation operating a formal queue where an employee receives updates from mothers **about named adult participants and sends those updates into their treatment files**.

I do **not** have evidence that family workers routinely disclose an adult participant's progress to relatives without consent.

I do **not** have sufficient evidence for a movement-wide rule instructing parents to terminate financial support, housing or all communication.

I do **not** have sufficient evidence for a standard requirement that estranged adult children communicate with parents only through the organisation.

There **is** evidence for conditional household boundaries, disapproval of same-sex relationships, parental coaching, referral, prayer campaigns, counselling, parent support groups, and survivor reports of pressure to distance oneself from affirming/LGBT people. Those are stronger and safer foundations for the fiction. ([Focus on the Family][22])

For **Europe circa 2016**, the detailed organisational mechanics in this pull remain disproportionately North American. The UK government evidence confirms analogous religious SOCE and isolation dynamics in Britain, while Clucas documents the UK-based Core Issues Trust's conversion-oriented framework, but I did not find a comparably rich 2016 European parent-ministry archive. ([GOV.UK][17])
