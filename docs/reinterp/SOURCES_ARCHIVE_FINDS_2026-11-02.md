## 4 What it adds to the research

- Visual culture of the ex-gay websites, 2001 to 2007: the home pages use a plain-text "WE BELIEVE THAT" credo, menu images, family and children photographs and a visible visit counter ("20775 page views" across roughly nineteen months from March 2001, about 1,100 a month on average by my arithmetic). The rainbow banding the lead reports is not visible in captured text and needs the screenshot to confirm.
- The credo as a numbered or bulleted doctrine: the institute page sets out a three-part definition, and the home page carries a shorter "WE BELIEVE" list in the same register. The lead's "5 points" graphic is unverified. This is the register the piece's 2003 era can draw on.
- CD-ROM programmes: "the Map" is a twenty-lesson curriculum on a CD with printable journals (row 6). It shows the conversion product as a self-directed course, not only a church programme.
- Testimony pages: the stories section frames video testimonials as "hope and deliverance" with downloadable samples (row 5), and the teen question page (row 10) uses first-person prompts. Both are structure references only.
- Worship songs as recruitment: the song page (row 7) pairs a parable retelling with a downloadable MP3 and a church copyright line. This is a documented case of the song-as-outreach form.
- Slideshow and "movie" sites: the Interview With Jesus page (row 9) and the Flash demo of the Map (row 3) show the form of the movie and the screensaver as tools of outreach.
