## ⚑ Q-D50 — his answer, and the better idea inside it
> *"I think it goes dark until called for if the narrative needs. Unless we can think of these time
> to make the space for the Provotypes to exist, that way if the person wants they can jump to
> there? that way creates a more narrative structure and we can indicate that during the experience."*

**The second half is the stronger idea, and the build is already asking for it.**
`data/paths.json` says of the intake provotype: *"FULL-cut beat, **staged on the one desktop that
exists**"* — the provotypes are squeezed onto Daniel's machine because they have nowhere else to be.
They are authored as `cuts: ["side-quest"]` content with **no home**.

**So: Daniel's abandoned machine becomes where the provotypes live.** Why it works —
- It answers a real, logged compromise rather than inventing a feature.
- It is thematically exact: **the apparatus's instruments remain examinable after you have left it.**
  The room you escaped is where its tools are still on the desk.
- It fits the **infrastructure spine** — the apparatus doesn't stop when you leave. A machine still
  running in an empty room *is* that argument, physically.
- It makes the rooms-that-age model do more work: each abandoned room becomes revisitable.
- It gives real agency inside the conducted-movement law — the node already exists and is unlocked.

**⚑ The risk to design against:** it must not become a **level select**. A menu of vignettes would
break the frame law (the frame never plays) and cheapen the beats into collectibles. It should read
as *going back to a room you lived in*, never as choosing a stage. **Sérgio's "we can indicate that
during the experience" is the right instinct** — the indication should be diegetic (the machine is
still on; the door is still there), never a quest marker.

**Combining both halves of his answer:** the machine goes **dark on departure** — and can be
**returned to**, later, when the narrative has given you a reason. Dark first, then found.
