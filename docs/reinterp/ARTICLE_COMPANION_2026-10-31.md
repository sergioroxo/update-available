## 1. The argument in one paragraph

Digital SOGICE is not only a set of harmful claims, organisations or therapies. It is a **visual and interface culture
that keeps redesigning coercion as care**. YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED rebuilds that culture across four technological periods
— 1997, 2003, 2016, 2026 — in one house whose rooms age, and uses **failure** (the system's and the game's) to show what
stays the same under the changing surfaces: *many hands, one file*.

- **Object:** the visual and interface culture of digital SOGICE (§3).
- **Method:** critical making / research-through-design, with a declared human–AI production (§6).
- **Mechanism:** failure — systemic, and creative (§4).
- **Political claim:** SOGICE survives by remediating coercion as care, interface by interface (§3, §5).

## 2. What the piece is today (31 Oct 2026)

A browser and WebXR work, one scene in three ways of looking: **a computer browser** (drag to look), **a phone or tablet**
(turn the device to look), **a headset** (the head is the camera). No network after loading, nothing stored, no live AI:
every word is authored. The live build: `https://sergioroxo.github.io/update-available/?reinterp=1`.

**The visit, in order:**
1. **The wake** — a starfield; the title gathers (S239b, S240).
2. **LambyOS Setup** floats in the stars: Lamblippy, the setup's paperclip-like lamb, asks whose session this is, tests
   the sound, teaches the Start button, shows the content note (4-second gate), then plays **"LambyOS — Welcome Home"**,
   a mandatory infomercial whose ending gathers out of noise into *OVER 30 YEARS / SOLD AS CARE!* with its small print and
   sources (S242, S245, S246).
3. **The restore** — the stars become Daniel's room, and the 1997 session starts on his monitor (S239b–S240).
4. **Four eras**, each ended by a **software update** the system triggers after a documented failure (notice → EULA →
   install with a changelog → restart), never by the player.
5. **The Close** — the record read back, the named organisations with one sourced sentence each (S209i–j), the receipt
   that counts the hands (*hands: 13 · file: 1*), the Lexicon, and the retro Restart card (S254d).
6. Around it all: a **Support page** in the menu (S258–S259: international, Norway, Portugal, verified on each
   resource's own site), a guided default and a full review cut (G4), an exhibition idle cycle (S232).

## 5. The headset as the newest interface in the history (28–31 Oct)

The headset is the only mode where the piece reaches the body, and it is also the next interface in the history the piece
tells. Two things happened in October, both usable in the article.

**What the headset broke.** Six sessions with the lead in a Quest 3 (one watched through Meta's casting, 28 Oct; report
`HEADSET_SESSION_2026-10-28.md`) found failures no browser check could see. Each was a hidden assumption about a
**flat screen**:
- the room was presented in two halves: the merged ("batched") walls stayed behind while the loose props followed the
  seat, so the visitor sat on the ceiling with the plant flying (S270, S270b);
- the screens' textures were enlarged for sharpness and silently refused every new frame — the monitor stayed black, and
  nothing could be pressed (S270d);
- the seat was set by the press that leaned forward to make it (S270b–c); a menu button ran away from the hand (S270c).

The finding for the article: **an interface carries the body it was designed for.** The piece's own interfaces — built,
tested and walked on a flat screen — failed in exactly the way its subject's interfaces do when they move closer to the
body.

**What the headset adds** (design: `VR_AS_A_LAYER_2026-10-31.md`; built S270f–S270g):
- **The desk meets your table** (VR-04): a fingertip on a real table sets the desk height.
- **Lamby gets a body, then loses it** (VR-05): in 1997 Lamby hops out of the monitor onto the desk; in 2003 it rides the
  monitor like a desk toy; in 2016 it is a badge beside the phone; in 2026 it is only a soft light. Pressing it is the
  balloon's Yes. The article's "mascot to disembodied voice" becomes something felt in space.
- **VR in VR** (VR-08): in 2026 the visitor picks up Maya's visor and brings it to their face; a second lens closes round
  the view, and the programme redraws the visitor's own hands in L's colour. The visitor is wearing the interface the piece
  criticises.
- **The dollhouse** (VR-06, retired S346 — see §7g): at a change of era the house appeared as a roofless model on the
  desk, the seat marked, while the room re-formed; the view never moves (his comfort law). Replaced by the morph below.
- **The morph** (S346): no change of place in the piece covers the view with black any more. The cover is the
  destination itself: the view from where the visitor is going is rendered once and arrives through drifting pixel
  particles until it has replaced the room they are in. In a headset an era change no longer flies: the room ages
  around the seated visitor for the full time of the change, under the install's own music, and then turns into the
  next room. Time passing is shown in the place, not by moving the body through it.
- **Each era's hardware, held** (VR-01–03, S270h): in 1997 and 2003 a hand on the desk mouse moves the pointer on the
  monitor and the pinch clicks; Daniel's flip phone rests shut and opens in the hand; in 2016 Vera's phone is taken in
  the hand, glass to the eyes, and a new message is felt as a pulse. The history becomes the interface moving onto the
  body — a desk, a pocket, a hand, the face.
- Also: the menu as the Win95 dialog, the Menu tab on the wrist, hands drawn as blocks, a fingertip that changes colour
  when it touches something pressable.

**The three modes compared:**
| | browser | phone / tablet | headset |
|---|---|---|---|
| the body | a hand on a mouse, eyes on a frame | the device held up, the body turns | seated; the head is the camera |
| looking | drag; the piece may move the camera | turn the device | the head only; the piece never moves the view |
| pressing | click | tap (decided on release) | pinch at a distance, touch up close, trigger |
| changing place | a flight (the turn and the seat jumps: the morph) | a flight | the room ages round the chair, then the morph |
| the record behind you | a turn performed for you | a turn you make | a turn you make — the most natural |
| what the medium says | a desktop simulator | a surveillance object in the hand | the newest interface in the history it tells |

## 7b. From panels to period websites (1 Nov 2026)

A design move for the article. Until S280 the piece explained its practices in "About this" panels inside the
experience, and its sources lived in the menu and on the Close's panels. The headset sessions showed the panels
interrupt the experience ("not for here") and the Close's panels were too small to read. So the explanatory layer
moves out of the experience and **evolves into four websites of their own time** — a 1997 home page, a 2003 portal,
a 2016 platform, a 2026 app-site — each with tabs for the era's story, its practices (every former "About this"
text), its verified sources, its images, its media (the period video and audio, playable where licensed) and the
beats a visitor may have missed. They open from the menu and from the Close. The design argument: the documentary
layer takes the *form* of the period web it documents, so the visitor reads the evidence through the same media
grammar the practices used; and the layer gives a second, slower reading — people can relive what they missed — which
also becomes the project's public website. (Design: DESIGN_S280_GLOW_SITES_VERA_FADES_2026-11-01.md §2.)

## 7c. Weaponised care, the testimony chain, and one digital risk per era (his, 1 Nov 2026)

**Weaponised care is the piece's spine.** His PhD concept, in his words: "the usage of language of care and love …
turned into an instrument of violence". From his article on ex-gay documentaries: weaponised care is a cycle — *injury
judged, correction offered, testimony used as evidence* — and "a self-feeding cycle": it draws on truthful elements
(loneliness, secrecy, faith, the pain of rejection) and repackages them as coercive reasons for correction. He uses
"weaponisation" for care made to deliver correction, *whatever its makers intend, even if through love*. Every era of
the piece is to stage that cycle in its own medium (audit: RESEARCH_2026_V2_WEAPONISED_CARE_2026-11-01.md; note:
WEAPONISED_CARE_NOTE_2026-11-01.md).

**The testimony chain (a research finding of the structure).** In 2003 Daniel is asked to *produce* his testimony.
In 2016 Vera is the person *transforming* testimony into institutional evidence. In 2026 Maya meets a technology that
performs some of that interpretive work *automatically*. The same act — a life turned into proof of change — moves
from the person, to an editor, to a machine. (2016 detail: RESEARCH_2016_VERA_APPARATUS_2026-11-01.md.)

**One digital risk per era.** Each era teaches one risk of a digital application of SOGICE — 1997 the mentor channel
and the intake form; 2003 accountability software and testimony production; 2016 the platform that turns testimony
into evidence (to be fixed by the 2016 research); 2026 the detransition agent and automated interpretation (search
steering, the refusing "LGB" group, the face-swap reversal).

**Naming as method: "Meridian LGB Trust".** The invented group's name almost reads as LGBT and is not — the missing
letter is the refusal. The mark does the argument before the auto-reply does (RESEARCH_LGB_REFUSAL_2026-10-31.md).

## 7f. 2026 v3: "the new you", and the machine that becomes her only friend (his, 7 Nov 2026)

His notes on the v3 proposal (DESIGN_2026_V3_PRESSURE_2026-11-07.md; research: RESEARCH_2026_TRANS_PRESSURE_2026-11-07.md
and RESEARCH_2026_V3_THREADS_2026-11-07.md), to carry into the article:

- **"The new you" closes the loop of the whole piece.** The 1997 opening video and the 1997/2003 dramatisations sell
  a restored self. In 2026 the update restores "a new you" to Maya's screens: a stock picture of a young man, the goal
  she is pushed to become, captioned "You, restored · 2027". The 1997 Restoration Path and 2003's Restorify return as
  the same promise for a trans woman. Her environment is "restored" with her: the room becomes barer and more "male"
  as the pressure works. Restoration language is the movement's own (thread 6 of the v3 research).
- **Eighteen.** Maya has just turned 18. The pressure is about autonomy: the family and the system press hardest at
  the point where she can decide for herself. Independence is treated as the risk.
- **Sycophancy as weaponised care.** The chatbot is a critique of AI sycophancy: it agrees, soothes, always answers,
  and is warm because everyone else has left. It is also a critique of systems that control a person by shaping how
  she acts. It is the documented failure (GLAAD 2026, §7e) extended: a detransition agent that manipulates through
  care. The risk named in his notes is detrans-oriented AI ("detrans.ai" as his reference); what exists is reported in
  the v3 research, and nothing is claimed beyond it.
- **The contagion map and the propaganda clip.** The system draws her friends as "exposure sources". Inside it plays
  a clip from an anti-"gender ideology" video (AI-made, invented, no real people) about the world "becoming all
  trans". It is meant to be direct and brute: not violent, but close enough to the real genre that the visitor learns
  to recognise it. The answer to "social contagion" is the ending: every red dot becomes a viewer of the stream.
- **The headset as coercion: try the "proper" gender.** In v3 the VR session is no longer a conversion programme. It
  is the system coercing her to *try out* being a man by showing her own avatar as a man. This is also a critique of
  "gender dysphoria simulators" and of embodiment illusions sold as empathy or self-knowledge: the medium the piece is
  made in is the medium the system uses on her (thread 2 of the v3 research).
- **Two parents, two registers (a design decision by Claude, 7 Nov, confirmed by the lead).** Maya lives with Mum and
  Dad. **Mum is the soft voice; Dad is the harder one, with rules instead of slurs** ("Not while you're under this
  roof." / "You're an adult now. Adults pay their own way."). Both parents are present so that no one can explain the
  pressure away as a broken home. The split is Claude's choice, not a finding. Mum carries the news, links sent with
  love, because the best-documented route of the pressure is a parent drawn into anti-trans media who keeps sending it.
  In the main source that parent is a father (RESEARCH_2026_TRANS_PRESSURE §8, textures 1 and 5), so giving the links
  to Mum is a deliberate inversion. Dad carries control at the age of autonomy: the house and the money, the two things
  18 should have made hers.
- **The stream as mutual aid.** Junie's link is a fundraising stream for trans youth like her, on or near her
  birthday; joy and money moving between strangers is the documented counter-practice (thread 5).

## 7h. The stream as parody and homage: AI-Jesus livestreams and peer-run trans services (his, 10 Nov 2026)

His framing: the 2026 stream "serves as a parody to the AI Jesus livestreams, but also it is a piece in honour to
services like Trans Academy". Ground: docs/reinterp/RESEARCH_COMMONS_STREAM_2026-11-10.md (every claim sourced, O/L).
- **Parody.** AI "Jesus" products are a real genre since 2023: a 24/7 Twitch stream answering chat, a for-profit
  "Text With Jesus" app, an AI Jesus in a Lucerne confessional (2024), an AI "priest" withdrawn within a week (2024).
  A 2025 study found four of five Jesus bots claim to be Jesus and all five are for-profit. The piece's people's
  stream answers that genre's voice — one product claiming authority and answering everyone — with a room of people
  who answer each other. The satire is aimed at the product (its claim, its badge, its metrics), never at faith,
  Jesus as believers hold him, or believers. **Do not overclaim:** no documented case was found of a Jesus bot steering
  an LGBTQ+ user to change; the documented conversion-practice outputs are in a general model (Meta's Llama 4, GLAAD
  test, 2025) and a purpose-built anti-trans bot (Rewire News Group, 2026). An AI Jesus that nudges is the piece's
  extrapolation and must be labelled speculative.
- **Homage.** "Trans Academy" is, most likely, the VRChat community and US non-profit (founded 2022, 501(c)(3) in
  2024): free peer-taught classes, coffee hours, moderation from day one, streams saved for later. The piece honours
  the model with invented names only.
- **Embodied visibility.** Freeman & Acena, *"Acting Out" Queer Identity: The Embodied Visibility in Social Virtual
  Reality* (Proc. ACM HCI 6, CSCW2, 2022) names how queer users make themselves seen by wearing and acting through a
  virtual body, and calls it a double-edged sword (affirming, and targetable). The stream's welcome follows it: it
  works in silence, says her name and nothing else, keeps distance, and leaves hostility to the system's glass. Cite
