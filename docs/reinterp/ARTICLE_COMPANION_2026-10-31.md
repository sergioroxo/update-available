## 1. The argument in one paragraph

Digital SOGICE is not only a set of harmful claims, organisations or therapies. It is a **visual and interface culture
that keeps redesigning coercion as care**. YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED rebuilds that culture across four technological periods
— 1997, 2003, 2016, 2026 — in one house whose rooms age, and uses **failure** (the system's and the game's) to show what
stays the same under the changing surfaces: *many hands, one file*.

- **Object:** the visual and interface culture of digital SOGICE (§3).
- **Method:** critical making / research-through-design, with a declared human–AI production (§6).
- **Mechanism:** failure — systemic, and creative (§4).
- **Political claim:** SOGICE survives by remediating coercion as care, interface by interface (§3, §5).

## 2. What the piece is today (31 Oct 2026)

A browser and WebXR work, one scene in three ways of looking: **a computer browser** (drag to look), **a phone or tablet**
(turn the device to look), **a headset** (the head is the camera). No network after loading, nothing stored, no live AI:
every word is authored. The live build: `https://sergioroxo.github.io/update-available/?reinterp=1`.

**The visit, in order:**
1. **The wake** — a starfield; the title gathers (S239b, S240).
2. **LambyOS Setup** floats in the stars: Lamblippy, the setup's paperclip-like lamb, asks whose session this is, tests
   the sound, teaches the Start button, shows the content note (4-second gate), then plays **"LambyOS — Welcome Home"**,
   a mandatory infomercial whose ending gathers out of noise into *OVER 30 YEARS / SOLD AS CARE!* with its small print and
   sources (S242, S245, S246).
3. **The restore** — the stars become Daniel's room, and the 1997 session starts on his monitor (S239b–S240).
4. **Four eras**, each ended by a **software update** the system triggers after a documented failure (notice → EULA →
   install with a changelog → restart), never by the player.
5. **The Close** — the record read back, the named organisations with one sourced sentence each (S209i–j), the receipt
   that counts the hands (*hands: 13 · file: 1*), the Lexicon, and the retro Restart card (S254d).
6. Around it all: a **Support page** in the menu (S258–S259: international, Norway, Portugal, verified on each
   resource's own site), a guided default and a full review cut (G4), an exhibition idle cycle (S232).

## 4. Failure as the mechanism

| failure | where | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Systemic: the update** | every era ends when the system fails at something documented, never because the player won | the apparatus is the plot; it survives by renaming and migrating "your file" |
| **Creative: games that cannot be won** | FIT IN's heap, ROOTCAUSE's bar stuck at 99 (NOT FOUND), CLEAR, FloppySheep, MATCH MADE SIMPLE | his law: "a rhetoric of failure… the queer side always wins" |
| **The refusal to let go** | Shut Down → *"You are not authorised to leave your treatment."* (S220); the diary that will not delete | the interface as enclosure |
| **The glitch** | the Enter glitch (S239b), the 2026 visor failing on her face | "she wasn't supposed to see it" |
| **The diary that forces the update** (his, 31 Oct; built S272) | 1997 now ends on DIARY.TXT: Daniel writes the truth the night before New Morning, the programme flags it and tries to erase it, the erase fails, and *that* failure is the update to 2003 | his words: the refusal to go "forced the system", and it is "a poetic connection to the fact that it is not possible to change". The system's next version is born from what it could not delete. |
| **The production's own failures** | content a player could not reach though every check was green (see the 3 Oct compilation §4); in October, a whole headset build that rendered but could not be used (§5) | critical making as finding out what the system you build does, not what you meant |

## 5. The headset as the newest interface in the history (28–31 Oct)

The headset is the only mode where the piece reaches the body, and it is also the next interface in the history the piece
tells. Two things happened in October, both usable in the article.

**What the headset broke.** Six sessions with the lead in a Quest 3 (one watched through Meta's casting, 28 Oct; report
`HEADSET_SESSION_2026-10-28.md`) found failures no browser check could see. Each was a hidden assumption about a
**flat screen**:
- the room was presented in two halves: the merged ("batched") walls stayed behind while the loose props followed the
  seat, so the visitor sat on the ceiling with the plant flying (S270, S270b);
- the screens' textures were enlarged for sharpness and silently refused every new frame — the monitor stayed black, and
  nothing could be pressed (S270d);
- the seat was set by the press that leaned forward to make it (S270b–c); a menu button ran away from the hand (S270c).

The finding for the article: **an interface carries the body it was designed for.** The piece's own interfaces — built,
tested and walked on a flat screen — failed in exactly the way its subject's interfaces do when they move closer to the
body.

**What the headset adds** (design: `VR_AS_A_LAYER_2026-10-31.md`; built S270f–S270g):
- **The desk meets your table** (VR-04): a fingertip on a real table sets the desk height.
- **Lamby gets a body, then loses it** (VR-05): in 1997 Lamby hops out of the monitor onto the desk; in 2003 it rides the
  monitor like a desk toy; in 2016 it is a badge beside the phone; in 2026 it is only a soft light. Pressing it is the
  balloon's Yes. The article's "mascot to disembodied voice" becomes something felt in space.
- **VR in VR** (VR-08): in 2026 the visitor picks up Maya's visor and brings it to their face; a second lens closes round
  the view, and the programme redraws the visitor's own hands in L's colour. The visitor is wearing the interface the piece
  criticises.
- **The dollhouse** (VR-06): at a change of era the house appears as a roofless model on the desk, the seat marked,
  while the room re-forms; the view never moves (his comfort law).
- **Each era's hardware, held** (VR-01–03, S270h): in 1997 and 2003 a hand on the desk mouse moves the pointer on the
  monitor and the pinch clicks; Daniel's flip phone rests shut and opens in the hand; in 2016 Vera's phone is taken in
  the hand, glass to the eyes, and a new message is felt as a pulse. The history becomes the interface moving onto the
  body — a desk, a pocket, a hand, the face.
- Also: the menu as the Win95 dialog, the Menu tab on the wrist, hands drawn as blocks, a fingertip that changes colour
  when it touches something pressable.

**The three modes compared:**
| | browser | phone / tablet | headset |
|---|---|---|---|
| the body | a hand on a mouse, eyes on a frame | the device held up, the body turns | seated; the head is the camera |
| looking | drag; the piece may move the camera | turn the device | the head only; the piece never moves the view |
| pressing | click | tap (decided on release) | pinch at a distance, touch up close, trigger |
| changing place | a flight | a flight | the pixel dissolve, the dollhouse |
| the record behind you | a turn performed for you | a turn you make | a turn you make — the most natural |
| what the medium says | a desktop simulator | a surveillance object in the hand | the newest interface in the history it tells |

## 6. The method: critical making with a declared human–AI production

*(The full account to 3 Oct is the compilation's §2–§6. What changed since:)*
- **Several builders, one lead.** The lead's Claude session plans, writes, reviews, merges and walks; **Codex** runs take
  the heavy builds from written prompts (`PROMPTS_CODEX_*`), each in its own branch, merged only after a full walk here
  (CX1–CX5, the entrance, the Library, the advert film); **cloud agents** take reviews and text cuts on their own branches
  (S223, S251); **GPT** made LambyOS Home's art (S231); **Sonnet** watched the headset session through casting
  (`PROMPT_SONNET_CAST_WATCH_2026-10-28.md`).
- **The walk is the gate.** Nothing is done until `tools/walk.mjs` clicks from the front door to the Close; the guided
  version walks in ~251–258 presses (the full review cut ~560). New in October: the walk logs the story step and the
  windows holding it, which found 1997's update unreachable once the browser had been minimised (S269).
- **New instruments for the headset:** `tools/xr-tour.mjs` photographs every era from the VR seat at a real head height;
  `tools/xr-layer-review.mjs` photographs the headset-only layer; `tools/cx5-entry-probe.mjs` drives the real entrance in
  an emulated headset.
- **Verification of what the piece says:** the Close's organisations (S209i–j) and the Support resources (S258–S259)
  were each checked on their own sources, and the Lexicon's sources ticked by the lead (S236b).
- **The lead's rulings move the work.** In October: the Bob-style front door dropped; the advert made mandatory; one guided
  version as the default; "take the viewer there" (no beat may depend on the visitor choosing to look); "no camera
  correction in VR"; the 1997 order packet → diary → Family Form → Root Cause → racket → update (S268–S269).

## 8. What the article must not overclaim

- **Headset and phone timing are not verified by instruments.** Every VR check here is an emulated headset in a desktop
  browser; the lead's Quest sessions are the only real evidence, and they are notes, not measurements.
- **The walk proves reachability, not experience.** It presses what can be pressed; it does not feel, read or hear.
- **2016 and 2026 cost ~170–196 draw calls on a flat screen when looking back through the house**, roughly double in a
  headset, against a budget of 75 (`VR_AS_A_LAYER` §10). Not yet fixed.
- **No survivor review exists**, and the trans reader gate was retired by the lead's ruling (compilation §5, §9).
- **The VR layer (§5) is days old** and has not been in a headset yet.
