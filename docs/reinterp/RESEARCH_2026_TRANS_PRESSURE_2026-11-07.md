## 1 Summary (12 bullets)

1. Conversion pressure on trans people is real, documented and often starts in childhood. In the 2015 US Transgender Survey, 14.0% of 27,715 adults reported gender identity change efforts (GICE). Among those who had talked to a professional about gender, it was 19.6% (S2, O).
2. GICE is linked to harm. Any exposure had 2.27 times the odds of a lifetime suicide attempt. Exposure before age 10 had 4.15 times the odds (S2, O). This is an association, not proof of cause.
3. In the 2024 Trevor Project survey, 16% of trans and nonbinary young people said they were threatened with or subjected to conversion therapy. In the 2025 survey (published 6 May 2026) it was still 16% (S4, S5, O).
4. The news is part of the pressure. In the 2025 Trevor survey (published May 2026), 94% of trans and nonbinary young people said recent anti-LGBTQ+ laws and debates caused them stress or anxiety (S5, O). A US study of 545 trans adults found 97.6% had seen negative trans media in the past year, and more exposure went with worse mental health (S14, O).
5. Relatives are a delivery route. First-person and survey sources describe a parent drawn into anti-trans material who then sends it on (S17, O). TransActual's 2021 survey: 85% of respondents said media transphobia affected how their family treats them (S11, O).
6. The law moved fast. UK: Supreme Court, 16 April 2025, "sex" in the Equality Act means biological sex (S6, O; holding itself seen via L sources). US: executive orders on 20 and 28 January 2025; Skrmetti, 18 June 2025 (S27, L).
7. Abandonment has numbers. In the 2022 US Trans Survey, 70% of trans people rejected by family considered suicide and 52% attempted. Without family rejection it was 56% and 38% (S8, O). Waits for UK adult clinics: over 15 years projected in England (S12, O). FOI-based figures run from 3 to 224 years by clinic (S13, O, advocacy-linked).
8. "Social contagion" / ROGD began with one 2018 parent-report study. The 2019 correction says ROGD is not a formal diagnosis and the data came only from parents, mostly recruited on sites sceptical of transition (S10, O). A 2022 Pediatrics analysis of 16 US states found the sex ratio did not favour assigned-female teens (S3, L).
9. The other side has documented positions too. The Cass Review (2024) and the HHS report (1 May 2025) argue the evidence for youth medical care is weak and favour psychological support first. Critics say the HHS "exploratory therapy" is conversion practice under a new name (S16, O; Cass L). Both positions are real. The piece should not satirise the clinical debate.
10. Detransition: the best-known survey figure is 13.1% of trans adults who had pursued affirmation reported a history of detransition, and 82.5% of those cited at least one external factor, led by parent pressure (35.6%) and stigma (32.5%) (S1, O). Other studies find different numbers and different reasons (S15, O). No single rate is settled.
11. Services that sell "options" to trans people are documented as a policy fight (exploratory therapy, faith programmes). I found NO documented chatbot or app marketed to trans youth for detransition. I found only general chatbot-safety reports and a Grok incident (S38, L). Do not claim an "AI detransition service" exists. [VERIFY SOURCE]
12. Joy and community are documented as protective. Family plus chosen-family support went with lower past-year suicidal ideation in a 4,147-person Trans Lifeline survey (S36, L). Ballroom, dance parties, mutual aid and hormone funds are reported in 2025 local press (S35, L).

## 2 How the pressure is delivered

### 2.1 Who does it
- **Family.** In the USTS data on detransition, "pressure from a parent" was the top external reason (35.6%), then stigma (32.5%), job trouble (26.9%), other family (25.9%), partner (20.2%) (S1, O).
- **Faith leaders and therapists.** The Turban 2020 paper looked at GICE from professionals and religious advisers (S2, O). The 2021 UK coalition research (Stonewall, Ozanne Foundation and others) said many cases happened in a religious setting, and more than half of those who underwent it were under 18 (S9, O). It was a small, self-selected group (64 offered, 39 underwent) and its own monitor called the data "qualitative and illustrative" (S9, O).
- **Government data point.** The 2018 UK LGBT Survey: trans respondents were about twice as likely as cisgender respondents to have been offered or to have undergone conversion therapy (13% vs 7%) (S9, O, quoting that survey). [VERIFY SOURCE against the 2018 survey itself]
- **Young people now.** Trevor 2025: 16% of trans and nonbinary young people were threatened with or subjected to conversion therapy. For all LGBTQ+ youth, 25% of those subjected attempted suicide in the past year (S5, O).

### 2.2 What it does (numbers)
- Turban 2020: recalled GICE exposure went with severe psychological distress in the past month (aOR 1.56), lifetime suicide attempts (aOR 2.27), and higher odds when exposure was before age 10 (aOR 4.15, about 206 people) (S2, O). Cross-sectional. Recall-based.
- A critic (Quillette, 2020) argued the survey data have irregularities and the "affirm vs convert" framing is too simple (S41, L). Report this as a documented objection. [VERIFY SOURCE: I only saw the listing]
- 2021 coalition: the page says harms reported included isolation, forced feeding, beatings, sexual violence (S9, O). Small sample. Treat as illustrative.
- USTS 2022 Health and Wellbeing: rejected by family, 70% considered suicide, 64% made plans, 52% attempted. Not rejected: 56%, 53%, 38% (S8, O).

### 2.3 The language (what the pressure sounds like)
These are recurring phrase types found in the sources. They are paraphrased types, not quotes from any person.
- "It's a phase." "Wait until you are older." (the delay frame; see the father who pushed a teen to delay hormones, S17, O)
- "You are confused / you were influenced online." (the contagion frame; see section 5)
- "You'll regret it." (the regret frame; see section 6)
- Appeals to the body and to God ("trust your body", "God made you ...") are the standard shape of faith-based GICE. I did not find a single dated, citable source that lists these exact phrases. [VERIFY SOURCE] Use as a plausible type only, or pull wording from Ozanne/Stonewall report PDFs, which I did not open.
- Pressure delivered as care: "we just want you safe", "we are only asking questions". In the UK debate, defenders of exploratory work say it is not conversion; critics say the line blurs (S16, S25, O).

### 2.4 Exploratory therapy: both positions
- **Position A (HHS report, 1 May 2025):** psychotherapy including "exploratory therapy" should be first line for pediatric gender dysphoria; calling it conversion therapy is "misguided"; evidence for medical interventions is low quality (S16, O).
- **Position B (AAP and other critics):** the report breaks with major medical associations, hides its authors, and its "exploratory therapy" can include conversion practices (S16, O).
- **UK draft Conversion Practices Bill, 25 June 2026:** covers England and Wales, trans-inclusive, up to 5 years in custody, says it does not ban "explorative conversations", exempts legitimate healthcare and counsellors. Royal College of Psychiatrists wants exploratory conversations protected. Stonewall says "abusive" in the title leaves gaps (S25, O; criticism L).
- Use in the piece: show the blur from the receiving side (what it feels like to be "explored" by someone who already knows the answer). Do not mock clinicians who ask real questions.

## 4 Abandonment

### 4.1 Family and home
- USTS 2022: family rejection and the jump in suicidality (section 2.2) (S8, O).
- USTS 2022 early insights (L): 30% had been homeless in their lifetime; unemployment 18%; 24% avoided doctors for fear of mistreatment; 80% of those out or perceived as trans in school reported mistreatment. These come from a press summary. [VERIFY SOURCE]
- TransActual 2021: 27% had been homeless at some point (S11, O).
- Truthout first-person account (Jan 2025, teen author): parents were supportive at first; a father's anxiety led him into anti-trans material, he pressed the teen to delay hormones, misgendered, and the author now sees him far less (S17, O).

### 4.2 Healthcare and waiting
- NHS England, Levy Review (18 Dec 2025): patients wait over 15 years for a first adult appointment in places; 20 recommendations; a single national waiting list; no official count of how many people present with gender variance (S12, O).
- FOI-based figures (Oct 2025): 48,000 waiting as of March 2025 across 15 UK clinics; four join for every one seen; range 3 years (Nottingham), 41 (Belfast), 224 (Glasgow). The groups behind it are advocacy-linked and the page gives no method. Some patients were told 20 months, not years (S13, O).
- GP refusal: 14% of TransActual 2021 respondents had been refused care by a GP for being trans; 57% avoided the GP when unwell; 98% said NHS transition care was not completely adequate (S11, O).
- US: after the Jan 2025 order, over 20 hospitals nationally stopped youth care in the first months (S21, O); more later. One Massachusetts family describes a 14-year-old two weeks from starting testosterone when it was cancelled (S21, O). Paraphrase only.

### 4.3 Institutions, names and records
- Name records are the repeated thread: university systems keep the old name in a second database; scholarships and aid can sit under the birth name; credit reports can still show the old name (S44, L: Xtra, Inside Higher Ed, LGBTQ economics call for stories). These are student-press and advocacy pieces, not prevalence studies.
- USTS 2015 via Healthline: only 11% had their affirmed name on all government IDs; 35% of people wanting a legal name change had not done it because of cost (S44, L). [VERIFY SOURCE: figures are from the 2015 survey and second-hand]
- UK Code of Practice (in force 5 Aug 2026): questions about birth sex should usually seek confirmation, not evidence (S20, O).

### 4.4 Spaces and groups
- Rejected by "LGB" groups: I did not find a citable, dated source for a specific LGB group refusing trans people in 2025-26. TransActual's Aug 2025 report speaks of "trans segregation in practice" (S23, O). Interviewees in 2019 said spaces meant to be welcoming, including some LGBTQ+ and feminist spaces, had become hostile (S18, O). [VERIFY SOURCE for 2023-26 cases]

### 4.5 Leaving
- YouGov/Good Law Project (fieldwork 23 Jul to 11 Aug 2025, 457 trans and related adults, weighted): 26% likely to live abroad within 10 years; 38% of ages 18-24; 84% say Britain is unsafe for trans people (S22, O). Intentions, not departures.
- Reuters (3 Oct 2025): a trans man says he is constantly assessing where he is safe; people avoid hospitals, hide identities, plan to leave (S24, O). Paraphrase only.

## 5 Social contagion: claim and evidence

### 5.1 The claim and its origin
- 2018: Littman (PLOS ONE) described "rapid-onset gender dysphoria" from parent reports. Recruitment was posted on sites mostly sceptical of transition (S10, O).
  ⚑ 11 Nov (S357o, correction re-read): exact wording — parents "may have viewed the recruitment information from one of at least four sites with varied perspectives"; "three of the sites… expressed cautious or negative views" of the interventions, one "is perceived to be pro-gender-affirming"; where they first heard of it "were not collected".
- 19 March 2019: the correction states ROGD is not a formal mental health diagnosis, the study does not validate the phenomenon, and it collected no data from the young people or clinicians. It also says selection bias may push results either way (S10, O).
- Supporters say the results section was largely unchanged and the paper was kept (S10 context, L: WORLD, National Review, listing only). Critics say the design cannot support the claim (L: BuzzFeed). Report both.

### 5.2 Evidence against
- Turban et al. 2022, Pediatrics: using 16 states' Youth Risk Behavior Survey data (91,937 teens in 2017; 105,437 in 2019), the assigned-male to assigned-female ratio among trans teens was 1.5:1 in 2017 and 1.2:1 in 2019. The 2019 shift was driven by fewer assigned-male teens identifying, not more assigned-female teens. The authors say ROGD should not be used to restrict care (S3, L). A critic notes self-report limits in the CDC data (L). [VERIFY SOURCE]
- Social support helps: Olson 2016, 73 socially transitioned children aged 3-12 had depression at population norms and only slightly raised anxiety. Parent-report, convenience sample, correlational (S26, O).

### 5.3 What the review bodies say (documented, not settled)
- Cass Review (April 2024): from a secondary summary only, it did not choose sides on "social contagion" and linked the rise in referrals to broader mental-health problems, with social factors possible. [VERIFY SOURCE. I could not open the report.]
- HHS report (1 May 2025): says the evidence on interventions is low quality and favours psychotherapy; AAP opposes it (S16, O).
- So: the contagion claim is contested. The strongest documented point is that the origin study cannot show it, and sex-ratio data do not fit it. The strongest opposing point is that rising referrals and unclear causes are acknowledged by review bodies. Keep both visible.

### 5.4 How it is used as pressure
- It lets a relative treat the person's identity as an infection from the internet. In TransActual's survey the media link to family treatment is 85% (S11, O).
- It makes the feed itself suspect: "who got to you?" The teen in S17 describes watching "transition timelines" for hope while the father sent the opposite material (S17, O).
- Design note: the 2026 world can show the same app giving Maya's father a "concerned parent" feed and Maya a "community" feed. Sérgio's call.

## 6 Detransition: what the evidence says

- **Turban 2021 (LGBT Health), 2015 USTS, 27,715 adults.** Of 17,151 who had pursued gender affirmation, 2,242 (13.1%) reported a history of detransition. Of those, 82.5% cited at least one external factor; 15.9% cited an internal factor. Top external factors: parent pressure 35.6%, stigma 32.5%, job trouble 26.9%, other family 25.9%, partner 20.2%. Limits: non-probability sample, only currently trans-identified people were surveyed, cross-sectional (S1, O). The authors note internal doubts can themselves result from external pressure.
- **Irwig 2022 (JCEM commentary)** summarises the field: the 2015 USTS figure is 8% ever detransitioned, mostly temporarily; reasons parental pressure 36%, difficulty of transition 33%, harassment 31%, job trouble 29%. Littman (2021, 100 detransitioners): only 24% told their clinicians. Vandenbussche (2022, 237 detransitioners, selection bias): the most common reason was realising dysphoria came from other issues (70%). Roberts 2022 (US Military Health System): about 30% stopped hormones within four years, but reasons were not recorded. Regret after gonad removal in an Amsterdam cohort: 0.6% and 0.3%, with 36% lost to follow-up (S15, O).
- **Meta-analysis ~1% regret (Bustos 2021):** surgical regret; critics say follow-up was short (S30, L). [VERIFY SOURCE]
- **Honest summary:** two different groups exist. Some people stop because of pressure, cost or harassment and later return to living as trans. Some people stop because they come to feel the transition was not right for them, and they report poor aftercare. Both are documented. Rates are uncertain. Neither 1% nor 30% is supported as "the" rate (S30, L, review summary). The piece must hold both people.
- **Detransition as lure online:** I did not find a rigorous study showing detransition influencers recruit trans youth. I found a TikTok study of detransition narratives that argues they reduce detransition to regret only (S45, L, Oakley, Journal of Multimodal Rhetorics) and journalistic mentions of large view counts. [VERIFY SOURCE] Frame the "lure" in the piece as design from the programme's side, not as a documented fact about real individuals.
- **AI / chatbot services:** none documented for trans youth in my search. General reporting: FTC inquiry into companion chatbots (Sept 2025); a Character.AI safety report; an Oct 2025 report that Grok called youth gender care "child abuse" (S38, L). [VERIFY SOURCE]

## 7 Joy and resistance

- **Support is protective (documented):** In a Trans Lifeline survey of 4,147 trans and gender-diverse adults, family plus chosen-family support and offline friend support went with lower past-year suicidal ideation (S36, L). A school that was gender-affirming went with lower attempt rates in Trevor 2024 (S4, O). 54% of trans and nonbinary youth called their school gender-affirming (S4, O, via search summary).
- **Rejection vs acceptance:** family rejection gap is in USTS (S8, O).
- **Ballroom and chosen kin (local reporting, 2025):** Oakland to All uses ballroom for mutual aid and a mental-health ball (S35 KQED, L). Black trans mutual aid fundraisers by party (S35 Emancipator, L). A Houston DJ party fund pays for hormone therapy for about 20 people (S35 Paper City, L). A Cincinnati pop-up clothing shop run as dignity-based mutual aid (S35 Soapbox, L). The Okra Project, Black-led trans mutual aid (S35, L).
- **Policy resistance:** courts and states push back: federal judges blocked parts of the funding threats; Colorado's supreme court ordered a hospital to resume care (S34, L); 24 states require insurance to cover care (S21, O).
- **Ethics note:** CLAUDE.md law says respite is real joy and never a trap. These sources support showing Maya's joy as functioning, ordinary and not performed for the audience.
