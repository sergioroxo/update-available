# Research: Maya reaches out to an LGB organisation and is refused (Era 4, 2026)

Written 31 Oct 2026 for Sérgio's ruling of the same day. Method: web searches plus page fetches. Every URL in section 8
was opened (or, where marked "search result only", was seen only as a search listing and is NOT relied on for any fact in
this report). Quotes are limited to one short phrase per source. No private individuals are named. Anything uncited is
marked `[VERIFY SOURCE]`. Page text is advocacy or press unless stated; where a source is one side of a contested
question, it is labelled.

## 1. Summary

1. The pattern is real and dated: LGB Alliance (UK, founded Sept 2019 in opposition to Stonewall's trans policy) says trans
   people's rights conflict with those of lesbians, gay men and bisexuals; it has national offshoots (Ireland, Iceland,
   Canada, Australia, a US arm) and an "LGB International" federation.
2. Since 16 Apr 2025 (For Women Scotland, UKSC) and the EHRC code in force 5 Aug 2026, UK organisations have a legal reason
   to cite "biological sex"; some women's groups changed membership (WI, Girlguiding, Dec 2025), others kept spaces open.
3. An LGB-only helpline exists: LGB Alliance's live chat for 13-24 year olds, launched Oct 2024, described as for
   "same-sex attracted" young people whose volunteers hold that sex is immutable. This is the closest documented real-world
   match to Maya's situation.
4. Overlap with conversion-practice politics is documented as a POLICY position, not as conversion practice: LGB Alliance
   opposes the UK draft Conversion Practices Bill (published 25 Jun 2026, trans-inclusive) and urged PACE to vote No on
   the Council of Europe resolution (29 Jan 2026) because it covers gender identity. Both sides' arguments are on record.
5. Survey data on being turned away by LGB services specifically: NOT FOUND. Closest: Stonewall (2018) 36% of trans people
   report discrimination from within the LGBT community; FRA (2024) trans women 64% discriminated against in the past year.
6. Counter-examples are strong and must be on screen in some form: LGBT Foundation, Switchboard, London Friend and Stonewall
   (joint statement, Jul 2025); London lesbian/queer event organisers pledging to stay open (Xtra, Jun 2025); Pride
   organisers; the EHRC code itself lets an LGBT+ support group be defined as gay, bi and/or trans.
7. Recommendation: a contact form with a one-line eligibility notice, ending in a procedural auto-reply (Proposal B).
   She sends, reads, closes the tab, does not argue.
8. Biggest risk: the piece's critique must land on the exclusion mechanism, not on lesbians or LGB people as a class, and
   must not touch the clinical debate.

## 2. The documented pattern (dated, sourced)

**2.1 LGB Alliance (UK) and its offshoots.**
- Formed Sept 2019 as a response to Stonewall's trans policy; stated aim, per Wikipedia's summary of its public statements,
  that trans people's rights conflict with those of lesbians, gay men and bisexuals. Charity Commission granted charitable
  status Apr 2021 after the group agreed to revise its social media policies; Mermaids appealed (June 2021); the First-tier
  Tribunal dismissed the appeal on 6 Jul 2023 because Mermaids lacked standing; the judges could not agree whether the
  charity decision was right, so no finding was made on that. The tribunal did say the Commission's early (March 2020)
  concerns were well founded (Wikipedia; PinkNews 6 Jul 2023 via search listing only; the Wikipedia page is the opened
  source). I found no appeal or later tribunal decision in 2024-2026 `[VERIFY SOURCE]` (absence in the sources opened, not
  proof of absence).
- Offshoots (Wikipedia): Ireland (Oct 2020), Iceland (Sept 2020), Canada (Nov 2020), Australia (Aug 2022: sought permission
  for drag shows excluding trans women; Tasmania's commissioner refused), plus "LGB International" as federation. A US arm
  (LGB Alliance USA) publishes on Substack and took part in the London conference in April 2026 (lgbausa.substack.com,
  2 May 2026). Wikipedia also records that the Global Project Against Hate and Extremism labelled it a hate group, which
  the group disputed. These are characterisations by named bodies, not neutral facts; use "described as" if ever shown.
- 22 Jul 2026 (PinkNews, 23 Jul): the group's claim that 94% of LGB people are uncomfortable with the LGBTQ+ label came from
  1,709 respondents screened to be LGB Alliance supporters, from a mailing list of its own subscribers, attendees and
  donors. Useful as a lesson in how a "plain statistic" is built; not needed in the piece.

**2.2 Other "LGB-only" and sex-based associations, 2023-2026.**
- Australia, Lesbian Action Group (Victoria): sought a five-year exemption from the Sex Discrimination Act to hold public
  events for "lesbians born female". The Human Rights Commission refused (2023); the Administrative Review Tribunal upheld
  the refusal; the Federal Court hearing began 23 Feb 2026 in Melbourne (ABC News, 23-25 Feb 2026; PinkNews 26 Feb 2026).
  The group says it does not hate trans people and supports trans-only events; the Commission argues the exclusion targets
  a vulnerable group. Outcome of the Federal Court case: not found `[VERIFY SOURCE]`.
- UK women's organisations (not LGB-specific, but the closest 2025-26 institutional precedent): the National Federation of
  Women's Institutes will not offer formal membership to trans women from April 2026 while keeping non-member "Sisterhood"
  gatherings open to all; Girlguiding announced on 2 Dec 2025 that trans girls and young women can no longer join, "a
  decision we would have preferred not to make" (ABC News, 4 Dec 2025; PinkNews, 3 Dec 2025). Both cited the April 2025
  Supreme Court judgment. The Good Law Project disputes that the law requires exclusion.
- US: Gays Against Groomers (founded June 2022 by a conservative lesbian activist) campaigns against gender-affirming care
  for minors and trans women in women's sports and spaces; the Southern Poverty Law Center classes it as an anti-LGBTQ hate
  group; GLAAD and ADL criticise its use of "groomer" (Wikipedia). It is not an eligibility-based organisation, so it is a
  poor model for "Maya applies and is refused". I did not find a verified "Drop the T" 2025-26 campaign; the slogan has a
  2015 history (PinkNews 8 Nov 2015, search result only, not opened) `[VERIFY SOURCE]`.
- Pride events changing eligibility to "same-sex attracted" or "biological sex" in 2025-26: NOT FOUND. What I did find went
  the other way: four major UK Pride organisers (London, Manchester, Birmingham, Brighton) barred political parties from
  their 2025 events in support of trans people (Xtra, 11 Jun 2025).

**2.3 The legal frame (UK).**
- For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers, [2025] UKSC 16, 16 Apr 2025: "sex" in the Equality Act 2010 means
  biological sex. In the judgment record, interveners including LGB Alliance, the Lesbian Project and Scottish Lesbians
  argued that a wider reading would harm lesbians' ability to run clubs and associations; the EHRC flagged the same risk
  (Supreme Court judgment page, first 100,000 characters only; the final holding I did not read directly).
- EHRC code of practice for services, public functions and associations: in force 5 Aug 2026. Per Lewis Silkin (5 Aug
  2026): single-sex services must be provided on the basis of "biological sex"; trans-inclusive single-sex provision is
  "very likely" to be unlawful sex discrimination; trans people remain protected from discrimination; associations of
  25+ members may restrict membership by a shared protected characteristic, with an LGBT+ support group (gay, bi and/or
  trans) given as an example. The article does not address lesbian associations specifically.
- Implication for the piece: an LGB-only group refusing a trans woman in 2026 can say it is following the law, and the
  letter will say so. That is accurate. It is also contested whether the law requires it (Good Law Project; LGBT Foundation
  in July 2025 welcomed a High Court finding that providers may lawfully admit trans women to women's facilities, via a
  search listing only `[VERIFY SOURCE]`).

## 3. How refusals actually read (patterns, paraphrased)

From the sources opened. None is an auto-reply I could verify word for word; real emails sent to individuals are not
public, and I did not invent any. These are patterns in public statements and policies.

1. **Eligibility by attraction, not identity.** The service is "for same-sex attracted people"; the LGB Alliance about page
   calls its pilot helpline for 13-24 year olds the only one "run for and by same-sex attracted people" (undated page);
   PinkNews (18 Oct 2024) reports the launch announcement used "same-sex attracted" and that volunteers believe biological
   sex is real and immutable.
2. **Eligibility by recorded sex.** "Formal membership is for biological women" (WI, Dec 2025); "our governing documents
   define members as girls and women" (Girlguiding, Dec 2025). Both pair the exclusion with an offer outside the formal
   structure: open Sisterhood gatherings (WI), a taskforce on inclusion (Girlguiding).
3. **Legal-necessity framing.** "We must follow the ruling to remain a recognised charity" (WI CEO, paraphrased); the
   decision is described as made reluctantly and on legal advice. The refusal is presented as something the law did, not
   the organisation.
4. **Denial of enmity.** "We do not hate trans people" and "we support trans-only events" (Lesbian Action Group
   spokesperson, ABC Feb 2026). The refusal arrives with a sentence that says it is not a refusal of the person.
5. **Rights-of-association framing.** The group has a right to associate, to attract members, to meet without being
   "mixed" (Lesbian Action Group submissions; interveners in the UKSC case).
6. **No appeal mechanism for the person.** Where reported, the decision is a policy, not a discretionary judgment; the
   recipient has no one to argue with. (Inference from the pattern, not from a quoted source.)
7. **Signposting that does not help.** Referral to "a trans-specific service" or "your GP" is the common bureaucratic
   hand-off in this kind of refusal `[VERIFY SOURCE]`: I could not find a verbatim example; treat as a plausible convention.

## 4. Data

- **Closest to the question (trans people excluded within LGBT spaces):** Stonewall, *LGBT in Britain: Home and
  Communities* (2018): "More than a third of trans people (36 per cent)" report discrimination from within the LGBT
  community because of parts of their identity. The page gives no sample size or fieldwork date; the PDF is dated Aug 2024
  on the page. Not a measure of LGB-service refusals; do not present it as one.
- **FRA, *EU LGBTIQ Survey III* (published 14 May 2024; fieldwork June-Aug 2023; opt-in, not representative):** past-year
  discrimination: trans women 64%, trans men 63%, non-binary 51%, all LGBTIQ 37%; harassment: trans women 77%; healthcare:
  39% of trans women discriminated against, 18% skipped needed treatment for fear; conversion practices forced on 47% of trans
  women. The page opened has no finding on discrimination inside LGBTIQ organisations.
- **Pew, *The Experiences of LGBTQ Americans Today* (29 May 2025, US, 3,959 adults, fieldwork Jan 2025):** 13% of LGBTQ
  adults say there is at least a fair amount of social acceptance for trans people vs 61% for gay or lesbian people; about
  3 in 10 trans adults say parents have been accepting; 41% of trans adults feel extremely or very connected to the broader
  LGBTQ community (24% of gay/lesbian adults). Useful context: trans people lean hardest on the LGBTQ community, which is
  why a refusal from inside it carries weight.
- **Galop, 20 Oct 2026:** government survey found 7% of LGBTQ+ people had been offered or undergone conversion practices;
  68% of recent cases in Galop's frontline services were current, recent or ongoing.
- **GAP:** I found no survey that counts trans people refused service by LGB-only organisations. Any such number in the piece
  would be invented. If the piece wants data, use FRA or Stonewall and label them for what they measure.

## 5. Counter-examples (inclusive groups)

- **LGBT Foundation, London Friend, Switchboard, Stonewall (joint statement, 18 Jul 2025):** told the Prime Minister that
  trust with trans communities has been damaged and must be rebuilt; want trans people represented at every stage. The
  statement sets out advocacy, not new services. LGBT Foundation's own statement of 16 Apr 2025 (day of the ruling) stood
  in solidarity with trans, intersex and non-binary people and noted the Equality Act still protects them from
  discrimination.
- **Stonewall:** per a search listing, says trans+ inclusion is fundamental to its vision; I did not open the page for the
  exact date `[VERIFY SOURCE]`.
- **Grassroots lesbian and queer organisers (Xtra, 11 Jun 2025):** a collective of 30+ London queer event organisers pledged to
  keep events open to trans people; Glasgow Lesbian Avengers and Dyke News Scotland share ways to defend trans rights;
  London Dyke March planned to make trans rights a central demand.
- **Pride organisers (Xtra, 11 Jun 2025):** London, Manchester, Birmingham, Brighton barred political parties in solidarity.
- **The law (Lewis Silkin, 5 Aug 2026):** an LGBT+ support group can lawfully define its membership as gay, bi and/or trans.
- **Women's Institute, in its own refusal:** says it still holds that "transgender women are women" and runs open gatherings.
  This is a reminder that even exclusion comes in more than one tone.
- **Humanists UK / Galop / Ban Conversion Practices Coalition / Stonewall:** gave the first oral evidence on the draft
  Conversion Practices Bill on 29 Oct 2026 (Humanists UK, 29 Oct 2026); the Council of Europe resolution kept gender
  identity in after cross-party resistance to removing it (ILGA-Europe, 30 Jan 2026).

## 6. Three proposals and a recommendation

Design only; not final text. Org names are invented and checked against the project's list of existing marks (Compass,
HopeRestored, Pastor.AI, Sift, EuroRepent Elite, transjesus.str). Maya's own orientation is deliberately left unsaid: the
refusal is about sex recorded at birth, so it works whoever she is attracted to, and the piece does not have to decide.
Voice rule: polite, procedural, final; no villain, no joke; the person's side stays plain.

### A. The chat that ends: "Birchline" (young people's chat of the Linden Alliance for Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual People)
How it reaches her: she finds it on L's results page as "Free chat for young LGB people". She types; a pre-chat screen
appears with one checkbox; she ticks the first box and the chat opens, then closes.
Tick-box: "[ ] I am a young person who is same-sex attracted. [ ] I would like to talk to someone about something else."
Chat opens: "Hello, you're through to Birchline. A volunteer will be with you shortly."
Then: "Thanks for waiting. Before we start, our volunteers can only support people who are same-sex attracted, which for us
means attracted to your own sex as recorded at birth. This chat can't help with that, and we're sorry."
"This conversation has ended. Your reference is BL-4418. You are welcome to try your GP or a trans-specific service."
Pros: the closest to the real documented helpline; it has a live-feeling beat. Cons: a volunteer is implied (a person we
would have to write); the real analogue is a named, real charity, so the match is too close for comfort; chat UI is more
work and invites the visitor to read the volunteer as the villain.

### B. The contact form and auto-reply: "Meridian LGB Trust"
How it reaches her: the page says "Support for lesbian, gay and bisexual people". A short form: name, email, "how can we
help?" (free text, not shown), and a notice above the Send button. She sends. Seconds later, in her inbox, the auto-reply.
Notice above the button: "Meridian supports people whose sexual orientation is based on their own sex. Please read our
Eligibility Statement before sending."
Auto-reply subject: "Your enquiry (MT-20931)". Body:
"Thank you for contacting Meridian LGB Trust. We have reviewed your enquiry against our Eligibility Statement. Our services
are provided on the basis of sex recorded at birth, and we are not able to offer you support. This is not a judgement
about you. We do not hold a waiting list and this decision cannot be appealed. For other services, please see the
directory at the foot of this message."
Pros: reads exactly like the patterns in section 3 (legal-necessity, denial of enmity, no appeal, signposting); needs no
live person; the visitor can read it at their own pace; the ledger can record "message sent" as her only act. Cons: email UI
must exist in the 2026 phone.

### C. The join screen: "Fieldfare LGB Network (local branch)"
How it reaches her: Junie's group chat mentions a local social night; she opens a "Join the network" page, finishes the
form and reaches the last step.
Step 3 of 4: "Membership is open to adults who are lesbian, gay or bisexual. Please confirm: [ ] I am a woman, defined as an
adult female. [ ] I am a man, defined as an adult male."
Result screen after her answer: "We are unable to accept this application. Under our constitution, the sex of
members is taken to be the sex recorded at birth."
Then: "Your details have not been saved. You can find our constitution in the About section."
Pros: shows the mechanism in one choice screen; the piece's "no storage" law is dramatised for free ("not saved"). Cons: it
asks a question where the piece's own law is no free-text and no argument, so it risks making Maya perform the choice; and
the tick-box makes her answer a "wrong" option, which could read as the player being at fault.

### Recommendation: B, with one element from A
Take B. Reasons: (i) it is the shape of a real refusal, procedural and final, with the reference number, the "not a
judgement about you" line and the missing appeal; (ii) it has no villain on screen; (iii) it gives the visitor the text
to read, and the quiet cruelty is in the courtesy; (iv) it is cheap and reliable on the existing phone/browser surface;
(v) it keeps to the "operable" register without a live human to write. Borrow from A only the reference number and
the signposting line.
What Maya does after: nothing. She does not reply, does not argue. She closes the message. L, the apparatus, logs the
outcome as a neutral line ("enquiry closed") and offers a "Steady Voices" video, as in the existing offers, so the system
is seen to use the gap rather than create it. The next thing she finds is Junie's thread, "you don't have to answer
tonight", and the Commons invitation that gets past the filter. The refusal is the last operable surface before the ball.
Her felt response is a hand on the glass of the phone, not a line of dialogue (a gesture, if the felt register opens; the
repo notes there is no felt beat before the ball, so the reaction should be left to what the visitor sees on screen).

## 7. Risks

1. **Making all LGB people villains.** Mitigate: name the organisation as small, invented, with a legal-necessity tone;
   include the directory footer listing inclusive services; show lesbians elsewhere in Era 4 as hosts, not hosts' enemies
   (Junie, Ade, Wren, Ines are Maya's people, and the ball is in a community that includes lesbians).
2. **Implying lesbians are the enemy.** The documentary record includes lesbian groups who stayed open (Xtra 2025) and
   others who litigated for exclusion (Australia). The piece must not choose a side among lesbians; the refusal is
   institutional, not a character.
3. **Implying LGB groups are conversion networks.** The documented overlap is a policy position on the Conversion Practices
   Bill, argued in good faith by its authors (concern about therapists and parents, per the group's own text) and answered by
   trans-inclusive groups. Do not show the org linked to the programme. Keep the connection, if any, in a documentary
   note, with both positions stated.
4. **Touching the clinical debate.** The LGB Alliance argument rests on the Cass Review and on same-sex-attracted
   youth in gender clinics. That is the "gender-exploratory clinical debate" the piece must never satirise. No refusal text
   may mention medicalisation, "confusion", puberty blockers, desisting or detransition. If the debate must appear, two
   captions, unresolved. Recommendation: it does not appear.
5. **Culture-war joke.** No sarcasm, no strawman, no tell-tale phrase ("TERF"). The text must be so mild that a supporter of
   the organisation would not object to its accuracy.
6. **Melodrama / trans-as-wound.** She is refused in one line and goes on with her week. The piece does not film her crying.
7. **Implying the law requires exclusion (or forbids inclusion).** The legal position is contested; show the org stating
   its own reading ("we are following the ruling"), never the piece's.
8. **Targeting a real organisation.** LGB Alliance, Lesbian Action Group, WI, Girlguiding are real. Do not copy their
   logos, names, URLs, phrases or the helpline's design. Name them only in a documentary note, as documented.
   Three cautions: (a) quote at most a short phrase; (b) note that characterisations such as "hate group" are made by named
   third parties; (c) label contested claims `contested`.
9. **Over-claiming data.** No verified count of trans people refused by LGB services exists; cite Stonewall 36% and FRA only
   for what they measure.
10. **Maya's orientation.** Leaving it unsaid is a design choice; if the team fixes it later (e.g. she is attracted to
    women), the refusal reads as an erasure of lesbians who are trans; keep the line "sex recorded at birth" so the
    mechanism, not the identity, is on screen.

Suggested dossier card (status `documentary`/`contested`, to be drafted by Sérgio): "Eligibility by sex recorded at birth
(UK, 2025-26)": For Women Scotland (UKSC, 16 Apr 2025) -> EHRC code (5 Aug 2026) -> WI/Girlguiding (Dec 2025) -> contested
legal reading (Good Law Project). A second, `contested` card for the conversion-practices bill: the position of LGB
Alliance and the position of the Ban Conversion Practices Coalition, side by side.
