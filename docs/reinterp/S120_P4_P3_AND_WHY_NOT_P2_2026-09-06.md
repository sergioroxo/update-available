# 1 · P4 — THE FRICTION BEAT NOW OPENS THE BALL

**What it was.** `u5_friction`, the fifth of L's ten units, sitting between the two name beats. It
said *"Before I open the stream — one short reflection first?"* and then, on either chip,
**"Opening it now."** ⚑ **And no stream ever opened.** The next unit was a different subject and
the ball was eight minutes and a whole offers sequence away.

**What the canon says the stream is.** `REINTERP_E4_DEEP_PASS_2026-08-05.md` §1.4: *"the ball is the
room; the stream is how the apparatus receives it. Maya is watching a broadcast of a ball, and L is
trying to process the feed."*

**What it is now.** The beat moved to the offers, between the careful pause and the break. The order
is `curation → pause → friction → the ball`. So the reflection is **the last thing the apparatus
asks of her**, the press that answers it is what opens the ball, and *"Opening it now"* is the ball
arriving.

⚑ **It is a re-order, not a rewrite** — the text, the chips, the audio names and the witness lines
are byte-identical to what they were as u5. Nothing was reworded to make the move work.

⚑ **And it is not the pause twice.** The careful pause is about her LIFE — hold the care pathway,
and it files `care pathway: interrupted — flagged for review`. The friction beat is about the one
thing she wants in the next minute. She accepts the big suspension; the machine still delays the
small thing; and then it opens it, into the one room in thirty years where it has no power.

L now has **nine** units, not ten. The debug route `e4Friction` points at the offers.

# 2 · P3 — THE OFFER CARDS OPEN, AND OPENING FILES

**The payload was unreadable.** Four cards went up on a 1.1 s timer and the whole wall was replaced
ten seconds later. `fine` — *Available in your region.* / **On by default.** / *Sponsored.* /
*No appointment.* — is 8 px type and is where the export thesis lives. **Nobody had ever read it.**

Press a card and it opens at 360 px with the fine print at a readable size, and ⚑ **the wall's clock
stops while it is open** — nothing in the era can take a card away while she is reading it. Press
again to close. Any order, as long as she likes.

⚑ **This partly supersedes a standing note** and the supersession is recorded in the data
(`s4_offers.json`'s `wall._beat`). That note read *"Nothing is pressable — this era's presses are
spent elsewhere and a store you can buy from would be a fifth beat nobody asked for. The reading is
the beat."* **The reading is the beat, and the reading was not possible** — that half had to change.
**The half that survives untouched is the design position:** there is still no purchase, no
acceptance, no consent and no branch. Opening a card does exactly one thing besides showing it.

⚑ **It files.** `interest: recorded — feed filtering`, and three like it. **Witness symmetry running
toward the SYSTEM**, which Ethics #10 asks for and which this beat never did: the wall filed one
line about itself and *her attention was the one thing in the era nobody recorded.* With S119's
strip on screen, the line appears under the card she just opened, in the same second, and nothing
says a word about it. Once per card — reading something five times is not five interests.

# 3 · ⚑ P2 IS STOPPED, AND BOTH REASONS ARE MINE TO OWN

> P2, as I briefed it: *"L captions whatever you look at. The instrument follows your attention…
> it converts the 167 waiting events into the exact activity the era is arguing about."*

## 3.1 · It would break the era's strongest built idea
While the visor is worn the player is **not looking at Maya's room.** They are looking at a picture
of the Sunroom, and `space.ts setLook()` is explicit about what turning does:

> *"The picture leans, in steps, and stops. **It never reveals anything that was not already on it:
> turn all the way round and it is the same window.**"*

And the design doc it implements, `REINTERP_E4_THE_SPACE_2026-08-06.md` §3:

> **"The apparatus can only ever show you a picture of a room. The ball is a room."**
> *"The commercial space has the flatness of a screen no matter how immersive it claims to be —
> because it IS a screen, and the piece is not hiding that… That turns the project's hardest
> technical constraint into its argument."*

⚑ **Making the worn view reward looking around would give the apparatus responsive depth — the one
property this era exists to deny it**, and the one that belongs to the ball alone. It is not a
tuning question. It would hand the machine the thing the whole era is built to withhold.

## 3.2 · And the number I justified it with was wrong
I told him Era 4 has **167 waiting events against ten in the rest of the piece**, and used it as the
evidence that his "tiresome" note was about idle hands. ⚑ **I never checked WHERE in the era they
fall.** Attributed to the press each one follows:

| after | waits | what that is |
|---|---|---|
| `close-restart` | **79** | ⚑ **the Close.** After Era 4 has ended — the constellation, deliberately still |
| `pause_yes` | **42** | ⚑ **the ball.** 3½ minutes with no controls, ON PURPOSE |
| `undo1` | 11 | the tail of the second memory |
| everything else | ~33 | scattered, single digits |

**121 of the 165 are the two beats in this piece that are supposed to be still** — one of them
protected by a documented law this project already learned the hard way (`00_WHERE_THINGS_STAND`
trap 0b, *"Stillness is not a dead end… this work's slowest beat is its most important one"*).

⚑ **So Era 4 does not have the idle-hands problem I diagnosed.** The waiting inside L's conversation
is about twenty events. The honest version of Sérgio's note is not *"the player has nothing to do"*
— it is **"nine non-branching chip picks in a row feel the same as each other,"** which is a
legibility problem, and P1/P3 are the right shape of answer to it. **P2 was aimed at a target that
is not there.**
