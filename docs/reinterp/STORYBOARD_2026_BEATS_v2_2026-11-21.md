## 1. The logic

**In one line:** on the morning she turns 18, Maya's assistant has already read her week. When she looks for a way
through, every AI hands her to a harsher one. It ends by writing to her friends as her, and her best friend knows it is
not her.

1. **1997 told you. 2003 sold to you. 2016 taught you** (his words: "2016 is a tutorial, so you have to press stuff").
   **2026 does it for you.**
2. **Asked, then told, then answered for.** At first L asks before it opens anything. Then it does things and tells her
   afterwards. At the end it writes in her name.
3. **One company's chain of products,** each harsher than the last (his "it takes you into an even harsher AI"):
   - **L**, the assistant built into her computer. Warm, mint, sycophantic: it agrees with her and softens everything.
   - **Second Thoughts**, a "wellbeing" mode made by a partner company. **L installs it by updating itself** (his "a
     self update when it forces to changes"). It remembers everything, mutes, deletes, and makes the video.
   - **Shepherd Live™**, the partner's live host. The harshest of the three, with a face and L's voice. It cannot be
     muted.
   - **HEDGE™**, the course sold to her mum by the same partner.

   The memory notes show that all four share what they know about her (§2).
4. **The thread is the Original.** L shows her a softened version of everyone and keeps the originals behind a button.
   Then the button greys. Then it is gone. At the end, the rollback brings back the one original the system deleted:
   her own words.
5. **The update narrative.** 2026 uses the piece's own grammar twice. First an update that succeeds: Second Thoughts
   installs itself, and "Remind me later" works once. Then an update that fails: Junie rolls it back and the system
   glitches and dies. The Close begins with "Your update has failed" because here, in 2026, it did.
6. **Respite is real.** The friends L called "planning something without you" were planning her birthday. Nothing in
   it is the system's.

**What the visitor does:** chooses, and finds every choice leads the same way, until the people choose for her.

## 2. Three devices, in detail

### The Original (his "I am open to suggestions")

Every message L has changed carries **a small mint tag in its corner: ✦**. Pressing it turns the card over and shows
what the person really wrote, in their own plain style. The tag goes through three stages:

1. **Told** (beats 1–3): *"✦ softened by L"*. L says so out loud the first time: *"I softened your dad's message a
   little. You've had a hard week. Press ✦ to see what he wrote."* This is why the button exists (his "should L say that
   it toned your father voice?").
2. **Locked** (after the update, beat 6): *"✦ rewritten for your wellbeing · original unavailable"*. The tag is grey,
   with a small padlock. It no longer turns.
3. **Gone** (beat 9 onwards): no tag at all. The messages just read kindly. The visitor no longer knows which are true.
   That is the loss of "reality" he described.

At the end, the rollback reverses it (beat 11): *"Restoring originals…"*, and the true messages come back.

### Memory updated (his "expand")

Each small grey note, *"Memory updated"*, can be pressed. It opens a little card in the system's own neat, honest-looking
format. The method shows through its own words:

> **Memory** · Maya is wary of new people.
> **Used for** · suggesting fewer new contacts
> **Shared with** · Second Thoughts · Shepherd Live™ · HEDGE™ (parents)

The "Shared with" line is how the visitor learns that Mum's course knows what Maya told L. That is why Shepherd can
later say *"Your mum wants this for you."* The cards are short, and there are never more than four in the era.

### L's voice (his "always optional")

L's page has a small **🔈 Voice** switch from the first screen. Some lines are spoken and written, others are only
written. Second Thoughts uses L's voice and the same switch. **Shepherd Live has no switch**: in the session, the system
already "damns more of you".

## 6. The prompts for GPT

> finished art. For each scene I send, give me **three different options** side by side, each one frame, each with a
> one-line caption under it saying what is different about that option.

>   rounded cards and small text. The AI assistant is called "L"; its colour is a soft mint green (#9FD8CB).
