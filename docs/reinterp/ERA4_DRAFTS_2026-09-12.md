## 3 · The two room recordings — a music prompt for his generator
⚑ Constraints, from the data file's own laws: **no voice, no lyrics, no words** (nothing here may be
synthesized as a person, and TransJesus is silent); **no borrowed vernacular** — the sound of a ball
as a ROOM, not a sample of anyone's ball. Two files, same recording, one heard through a wall.

**`ball_room_landing.mp3` — the room, 2:40, loopable**
> Instrumental. A community hall at night, mid-event, recorded from inside the room. A steady
> four-on-the-floor house beat around 122 BPM, warm and slightly lo-fi, sub-bass soft; over it a
> single repeating synth chord stab and a hi-hat pattern, nothing melodic enough to be a song.
> Layered in, at a natural distance: a crowd of forty people — attentive, not chatting — with
> bursts of applause and cheering on a beat every 20–30 seconds, one or two whistles, hands
> clapping in time, a floor that creaks. No MC, no speech, no singing, no lyrics. Room reverb of a
> medium hall with a low ceiling. Loop-safe: no intro, no outro, no fade.

**`ball_room_bed.mp3` — the same room through a wall, 2:40, loopable**
> The same recording as heard from the next room through a closed door and a wall: low-passed
> hard (everything above ~1.5 kHz gone), the kick and bass carrying, the applause a dull swell,
> the crowd a murmur. Muffled, structured, unmistakably a room full of people you are not in.
> Loop-safe, same tempo, same length, so the two can be crossfaded when the wall comes down.

Reference points to describe to the generator (not to copy): the *room tone* of a live ballroom
category recording — the beat, the calls of the crowd on a landing — with the MC and the music's
melody removed. Good examples to listen to for the crowd/landing dynamic: any live category footage
where the audience reacts on the beat; what to avoid: laughter, chatter, generic "party ambience".
