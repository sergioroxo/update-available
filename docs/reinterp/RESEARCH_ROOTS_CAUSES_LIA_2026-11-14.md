# Roots and causes: what the programmes taught, read from the Love in Action Collection (14 Nov 2026)

*Research only. It is not a script. Nothing in `src/`, `data/` or `public/` changed. Asked for by Sérgio after he watched the
1997 orientation tape draft (`ORIENTATION_TAPE_2026-11-13.md`). His brief: a teaching tape, in more depth, on WHY people are
gay or lesbian, as the programmes taught it, broken up by commercials. Widened the same day: three tapes, one per room.
1997 = gay (Daniel). 2016 = lesbian (Vera). 2026 = trans (Maya), in the programmes' own words ("transsexuality",
"transvestism", "gender confusion", "gender identity").*

## 0. The ten things that matter most

1. **The male "Roots and Causes" booklet is staged as a play.** "The Setting", then Act I to Act V, birth to college. It has
   a triangle diagram (mother, father, son) and a "Words to Hear / Words to Fear" panel. This is ready-made tape grammar.
2. **The local OCR of that booklet was almost empty** (1,847 words for 24 pages). I re-read it with macOS Vision OCR
   (about 9,000 words). Its content had never been read before.
3. **Love in Action's director taught that being gay "develops out of a series of decisions".** The same booklet says a
   lesbian woman has no "file" for femininity, like a floppy disc with no folder. A computer metaphor from 1993.
4. **The Steps Out class manual names three root causes as class titles: Fear, Envy, Isolation.** Envy is taught as an
   equation: comparison, then admiration, then envy, then desire. It comes with worksheets.
5. **The lesbian booklet's "Big Three"** (relationship issues, an identity struggle, a spiritual fallacy) is word for word
   the Lexicon's 2016 arc. The Lexicon had it only from a later reprint. The original 2001 booklet is now read and matches.
6. **The archive fills four Lexicon gaps.** Media and schools as a cause for men; pornography as a cause; the "attachment"
   formula; school and university curricula as a cause for women (§3D).
7. **The genogram symbol chart puts "H Homosexuality" beside Drugs, Gambling, Alcoholism, Abortion and Criminal/Jail.** It is
   page 64 of the Radical Living manual (now confirmed in the binder itself, §8). It corroborates Conley's "H".
8. **The trans teaching in the archive is narrow and old.** The 1988 telephone manual's two tabs, a 1994 class manual, a 2001
   group manual and a 2004 workbook. Nothing after 2013. The archive cannot support a 2026 tape on its own (§2C, §6C).
9. **The marketplace is rich and priced.** Booklets at $3, videos at $20, a $40 clinical book "WARNING: graphic content", a
   35-cent leaflet for parents of a "pre-homosexual" child, a $29 CD-ROM, a 20-week group at $200 to $300 (§4).
10. **The collection holds the real thing the piece imitates.** Love in Action teaching cassettes ("Child Development #2")
    and VHS tapes. They are not digitized (§7).

**Added 14 Nov, from seven more folders (§8).**
1. **The LIA founder's handbook** (*Steps Out of Homosexuality*, 1984; revised 1995) is the source text of the triangle and
   of Fear, Isolation, Envy. A 1997 tape can now cite it instead of the 2001 booklets.
2. **A typed lecture outline from January 1989** is the best date fit for 1997. Its first point admits science does not
   agree.
3. **Rentzel's *Emotional Dependency*** calls dependency idolatry: sin, even without sex. Its 1990 edition says the same
   gestures are fine in marriage. That sentence is the 2016 tape's collapse (§8C).
4. **The programme's own pictures:** a 1998 family-roles chart, feeling faces, blue-framed overhead cartoons. Most cartoons
   belong to others: describe, never copy (§8F).

## 2. The causal model as taught

*Each step: the claim (plain paraphrase) · where · one short quote only if the words matter · how the documents tell it.*

### 2A. Gay men (for 1997, Daniel's room)

**A1. "Not born this way". And even if born, not normal.**
1. *Claim.* No study proves a genetic cause. The famous studies are flawed. Gay leaders push "born gay" because it wins
   sympathy. Even if it were inborn, that would not make it right: alcoholism may be inborn too.
2. *Where.* FOF male, PDF pp. 5–7 (printed 4–9). Exodus PR-01, PDF pp. 26–35. Smid PR-02, PDF pp. 2–3. Steps Out manual
   p. 270: people are born "neutral" in sexual direction (it cites Masters and Johnson).
3. *How it is told.* "Flawed Study #1, #2, #3" boxes (brain, twins, X chromosome). A banner: CLAIMS OF GENETIC CAUSATION
   HAVE BEEN DISPELLED. A newspaper-headline collage. A "Kinsey asked how they got that way" statistic. The closing page of
   both FOF booklets carries a psychologist's line that everyone is really heterosexual. The PR-01 booklet admits, near its
   end, that most gay people did not choose their orientation.

**A2. The Setting: the marriage before the child.**
1. *Claim.* The child's sexuality starts in his parents' marriage. If the husband is not emotionally there, the wife turns
   to her son for comfort. That burden confuses the boy.
2. *Where.* FOF male, PDF pp. 8–10 (printed 11–14).
3. *How it is told.* A section titled "THE SETTING: Before the Main Character Sets Foot on Stage". Bullet lists of what God
   "appoints" husbands to be (nurturing, the leader in the home) and wives to be (affirming of her husband's masculinity).
   **The triangle diagram**: MOTHER and FATHER at the top corners, SON at the bottom, arrows between. A DEFINITION box with
   question-mark icons ("Gender identity").

**A3. Act I, birth to 18 months: the mother and "basic trust".**
1. *Claim.* The baby needs a secure bond with the mother. Without it he is "vulnerable" later. The booklet adds that this is
   not the cause by itself.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF p. 10 (printed 15). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 3–5. Life Development Process (LDP) PDF pp. 4–5.
3. *How it is told.* The baby's imagined questions: "How long will she let me cry?", "Am I a person? Do I exist?". The
   **"performance scale"**: am I loved when I am bad? The LDP outline begins with conception: the father's sperm decides XX
   or XY, so "the father calls the child into existence". One version adds that, as the father pulls away after conception,
   he pulls away after birth too (LDP PDF p. 15).

**A4. Act II, 18 months to 5 years: the father.**
1. *Claim.* The boy must turn from his mother to his father. A warm father lets him. A cold, distant or critical father
   leaves a "hurt" that later becomes desire. The booklet says the cause is not an absent father but the boy's hurtful
   experience of him.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF pp. 11–12 (printed 16–19). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 5–7. LDP "Three to five years old: Gender formation".
3. *Quote.* The lack may exist only in the child's mind: "perception holds a faulty grasp on reality" (FOF male, printed 18).
4. *How it is told.* **"WORDS TO HEAR / WORDS TO FEAR"**, two columns beside a close-up of a mouth (FOF male, printed 19).
   Words to hear: a mother says the boy is just like his daddy; a father says "Let's go for a walk together". Words to fear:
   a father's insult, a mother calling the boy indoors to bake, a father who says nothing. Each line has an italic gloss of
   what it "says" to the boy. Smid adds father-son touch: wrestling, sitting on Dad's lap, swimming together. His example of
   a "perceived" lack: a father flies away for a family emergency and the boy reads it as rejection.

**A5. The mother: too close.**
1. *Claim.* A mother who leans on her son, or keeps him from his father, holds him back from manhood. A single mother is
   most at risk of doing this.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF pp. 9–10, 12. Steps Out manual ch. 34 "Mothers and Sons", pp. 219–220. Steps Out "Fear" p. 279
   (fear of injury: "the overprotective mother influence"). Smid PR-02 PDF p. 8 (the boy "stays in the kitchen with mom").
3. *How it is told.* A fairy tale: the key to the caged wild man lies under the mother's pillow, and the son must ask her
   for it. An anecdote: Mother's Day cards sell faster than Father's Day cards. The soul as an "emotional history chart"
   that records everything from the womb on.

**A6. Act III, 5 to 12 years: the peers, the "pre-homosexual" boy.**
1. *Claim.* A boy cut off from his father feels cut off from other boys. He avoids sport, is teased, retreats. He is not gay
   yet but "pre-homosexual". He is a polite, neat, "good little boy" with a "false self". Ages 6 to 10 are God's "safe
   years" to bond with his own sex. The names he is called become who he thinks he is.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF p. 13 (printed 20–21). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 7–9. LDP "Six to ten years old: Same sex development".
   Steps Out "Isolation" p. 295.
3. *How it is told.* Faded photo of two boys behind the text. "Remember the cootie stage?" Smid's labels passage: if they
   call me that name all the time, that must be who I am. His own memory of a family gathering, staying with the aunts.
   The LDP timeline puts "The safe years" and "The safe haven" (home) as bullet points.

**A7. Abuse.**
1. *Claim.* Early sexual abuse opens the "sexual being" too soon. A boy starved of his father's touch is easy prey; the
   abuse "seems to fill" that need. Healing needs comfort and belief from the adults.
2. *Where.* FOF male factor list, PDF p. 8 (printed 10). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 4, 7, 9. LDP PDF p. 15.
3. *How it is told.* A checklist with empty tick boxes: sexual violation, incest or molestation, pornography, negative
   spiritual influences, media, temperament, body image, peer labelling, fear of the opposite sex. **Documented harm: see §5.**

**A8. Act IV, puberty: desire for what you lack.**
1. *Claim.* At puberty the "pre-homosexual" boy finds other boys a mystery, as straight boys find girls one. Desire is "a
   symbolic attempt" to know his own body through other male bodies. What we lack in ourselves we are drawn to in others.
   Schools and the media push teenagers to take a gay label too soon.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF pp. 14–15 (printed 22–24). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 10–11 ("the object of our curiosity becomes the
   object of our sexual desires"). Exodus PR-06 PDF p. 43. Steps Out "Psychic Response" pp. 269–272.
3. *How it is told.* Smid's two boys: one who dates a girl from maths class, one with a "same-sex deficit" who stays small
   and undeveloped. Exodus PR-06 decodes body parts: arms mean strength and protection, chest means a father figure,
   genitals mean manhood. Steps Out names the **"identity transfer"** a father must give his son, and lists the
   "ingredients" of same-sex desire: an unfulfilled need, no unconditional love, no identity transfer, self-pity,
   rebellion, admiration, envy, isolation.

**A9. Fear, Envy, Isolation: the three root causes as class titles.**
1. *Claim.* Fear (of abandonment, failure, injury, women), isolation, and envy feed each other. Envy starts as comparison,
   becomes admiration, then possessiveness, then is "eroticized".
2. *Where.* Steps Out section 8, "ROOT CAUSES OF HOMOSEXUALITY: FEAR" (p. 279), "...: ENVY" (pp. 284–286), "...: ISOLATION"
   (p. 295). Exodus PR-06 PDF p. 43 ("Much same-sex lust is rooted in envy").
3. *How it is told.* An equation in the manual: Comparison = Admiration = Envy = Inappropriate Response. A worksheet beside a
   "PERSONAL NOTES" margin: describe a recurring fantasy; trace it back; find the envy in it. A "Scripture Sheet on Envy".

**A10. Act V, college and beyond: the decision.**
1. *Claim.* Away from home and church, the young man is offered bars, gyms, beaches "even the Internet" (FOF male, printed
   25). He decides to "come out" or "act out". The whole thing is a chain of choices, so new choices can undo it.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF p. 15. Smid PR-02 PDF p. 3. Steps Out p. 272 and p. 286 ("learned behavior").
3. *Quote.* "homosexuality develops out of a series of decisions" (Smid, PR-02, printed p. 2 = PDF p. 3; corrected S387 from the page image, was "printed p. 3").
4. *How it is told.* Steps Out tells the class to own its desire in capitals: "WE ASKED FOR IT, WE GOT IT!" (p. 272). And a
   slogan: "WHAT WE FEED WILL GROW; WHAT WE STARVE WILL DIE." The PR-06 booklet: the mind as a walled city with its wall
   knocked down; rebuild it one brick at a time.

**A11. The family on paper: genogram, lifeline, the chain of needs.**
1. *Claim.* The cause can be found by mapping the family and the life stage by stage.
2. *Where.* B1 F5 PDF p. 7 (Genogram Symbol Chart). LDP PDF p. 8 (timeline). Steps Out ch. 55 "Filling the Needs", p. 357.
3. *How it is told.* **The symbol chart**: squares for males, circles for females, a cross through a circle for "Death -
   suicide", triple lines for "Enmeshed Relationship", a zigzag for "Difficult or Hostile". Then big letters: **H**
   Homosexuality, **D** Drugs, **$** Gambling, **P** Promiscuous, **A** Alcoholism, **M** Mental Illness, **Ab** Abortion,
   **G** Gang involvement, **Po** Pornography, **C** Criminal/Jail. **The lifeline**: a horizontal ruler with ticks at 0-1,
   1-2, 3-5, 6-10, Puberty, Early, Middle and Late Adulthood, "Sunlight Years". **The chain** in Steps Out: early hurt,
   alienation from others, "defensive detachment", alienation from self, then narcissism and a "second self", each tied to
   one of Erikson's stages. The textbook page on Erikson sits in the LDP folder.

**A12. The cure follows from the cause.**
1. *Claim.* Same-sex desire is a "reparative drive": the man is trying to get the masculinity his father never gave.
2. *Where.* FOF male PDF p. 16 (printed 26). Smid PR-02 PDF p. 12: homosexuality is "one symptom" of a block in growth.
3. *Quote.* "meeting legitimate God-given needs in illegitimate ways" (FOF male, printed 26).
4. *How it is told.* Every booklet ends the same way: a "true story" of change, a referral page, an order page.

**A13. For parents: "Is this my fault?"**
1. *Claim.* Three possible answers. God made him so: false. He chose it: false. Many factors, "and I may have contributed":
   true. So the parent confesses to the child and asks forgiveness. Prevention is possible.
2. *Where.* FOF parents' booklet (B8 F5) PDF pp. 4–8 and 13.
3. *How it is told.* Contents: "The Bombshell Hits", "Haunting Grief", "The 'If Only' Trap". A model confession, word for
   word, for a mother to say to her son. A **prevention checklist**: affirm your child's gender; fathers, hug your sons; no
   opposite-sex nicknames; find same-sex role models (scout leaders, coaches). A model answer for a small child who asks why
   two women hold hands. A prayer list with a large faded word PRAY: one line asks God that the child will "experience the
   consequences of sin" (PDF p. 15). The booklet compares learning of a gay child to a family killed in a car crash.

### 2B. Lesbian women (for 2016, Vera's room)

*The archive's lesbian teaching runs 1987–2004. The richest single source is the 2001 FOF booklet (B6 F4).*

**B1. Not born this way.**
1. *Claim.* As for men. Three "flawed studies": twins, sisters, the inner ear. Then a psychiatrist's 2001 study is offered as
   proof that change happens.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF pp. 5–7 (printed 4–9).
3. *How it is told.* The same "Flawed Study" boxes. A sidebar about dolphins. A collaged newspaper headline about a gay gene.
   A quote from a feminist author that "biological" stories never appealed much to women.

**B2. "The Big Three".**
1. *Claim.* Lesbianism comes from three conditions: relationship issues, an identity struggle, a spiritual fallacy (a cold,
   distant picture of God learned at home or church).
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF p. 8 (printed 10); explained PDF p. 10 (printed 14–15).
3. *Quote.* "not a sexual orientation problem; it is an identity crisis" (printed 15).
4. *How it is told.* "THE BIG THREE: The Core of Lesbianism" in a purple banner, the words "the big three" in script down the
   margin, the three conditions stepped like stairs.

**B3. "Preconditioning factors".**
1. *Claim.* Before any same-sex thought, a girl has been "preconditioned", mostly by trauma. Physical: sexual abuse, incest,
   rape, neglect. Emotional: abandonment, rejection, unmet needs, no nurturing, no protection, verbal abuse. Also: a mother
   who fails to nurture, a father who fails to protect, a temperament (creative, bright, intuitive, self-protective),
   media, school and university curricula, peer labelling, fear of men.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF pp. 8–9 (printed 10–13).
3. *How it is told.* A DEFINITION box: "Preconditioning Factor". A two-column list. Then a full purple page over a woman's
   face, with huge numerals: a 2001 survey of 265 "women overcoming homosexuality" by an Exodus board member, giving the
   share who reported emotional abuse, sexual abuse, verbal abuse, abandonment, physical abuse, neglect. **Harm figures:
   see §5.**

**B4. Infancy and toddlerhood: the mother, "defensive detachment".**
1. *Claim.* The bond with the mother is "the foundation of her complete feminine identity". Even a hospital stay can break
   it. A girl whose mother seems distant, bitter, critical or overbearing pulls away to protect herself: "defensive
   detachment". She then distrusts her own sex.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF pp. 11–12 (printed 16–18). The term is credited to a Love in Action training series (1989).
3. *How it is told.* A DEFINITION box for "Defensive Detachment", with a second, plainer meaning: withdrawing trust to
   protect ourselves.

**B5. The father: the score card.**
1. *Claim.* At 18 months the father must "come alongside" and affirm his daughter. A girl looks to her mother for the model
   and to her father for the grade. Mockery, or a brother praised more, makes her copy boys. Abuse by a man teaches her that
   women are victims, so she refuses to be one.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF p. 12 (printed 18–19). Smid PR-02 PDF pp. 6, 9 (a father's reply is "the score card of passing
   or failing as a little girl"). Exodus phone manual, Lesbianism tab, p. 17.
3. *How it is told.* A survey sidebar: just over half the women had been "tomboys" (it adds that most tomboys do not become
   lesbians). Smid's healthy example: mother and daughter at the mirror putting on lipstick; a father praising a church
   dress.

**B6. Childhood: the friends, the words.**
1. *Claim.* Girls need girl friends who affirm femininity. Moving house, no neighbours, abuse, or a broken bond with the
   mother can stop that. Friends who point out "masculine" traits can scar her.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF p. 13 (printed 20–21).
3. *How it is told.* **WORDS TO FEAR / WORDS TO HEAR for girls.** To fear: being called a tomboy; told she should have been a
   boy; compared to a "girly" neighbour; other adults calling her "son" or "young man"; the church saying a woman is worth
   less. To hear: a father calling her his precious little girl; the church saying she is of equal worth.

**B7. Puberty and adulthood: emotional dependency.**
1. *Claim.* By puberty the "emotional dependency" is "planted". In adulthood the woman places her whole self in another
   woman. If that woman leaves, she collapses, like a baby separated from its mother.
2. *Where.* FOF female PDF pp. 14–15 (printed 22–24). Exodus phone manual, Lesbianism tab, p. 17.
3. *Quotes.* Dependency is when a woman "deposits her identity and well-being in another woman" (FOF female, printed 23).
   The 1988 phone manual: "emotional dependency being the foundation of most lesbian relationships".
4. *How it is told.* An analogy to an infant: wanting to nestle in another woman's arms began in infancy. **The booklet links
   a break-up to suicide and to infant death: see §5.**

**B8. Dependency as a checklist.**
1. *Claim.* Two failure modes: walls (defensive detachment) or clinging (dependency). Both have signs you can tick.
2. *Where.* Exodus PR-09 *Forming Healthy Friendships*, PDF pp. 16–20.
3. *How it is told.* Lists. Signs of detachment: anger, sarcasm, isolation, refusing help, needing control, "dressing down",
   investing in pets instead of people. Signs of dependency: jealousy, wanting time alone together, plans that always
   include her. "Manipulations": wearing each other's clothes, poetry and music, special nicknames, moving in together.
   Metaphors: a **lifeguard's "death grip"**; a **Chinese finger trap**. Short case vignettes (§5: testimony).

**B9. The floppy disc.**
1. *Claim.* A straight woman files each experience of femininity in her "woman" folder. A lesbian has no folder, so the
   affirmation is lost.
2. *Where.* Smid PR-02, PDF p. 11 (printed 19). © 1993.
3. *Quote.* "she has no file to put it in".
4. *How it is told.* A "computer analogy" in a 1993 booklet. This is the closest thing in the archive to the piece's own
   language.

**B10. Late discovery: the dissatisfied wife.**
1. *Claim.* Women who discover lesbian feelings in mid-life were dissatisfied with their husbands, or carry an unhealed
   childhood wound.
2. *Where.* Exodus PR-13 *Only In My Mind*, PDF pp. 54–55 (citing a 1998 newspaper story about a support group). FOF parents'
   booklet PDF pp. 11–12.
3. *How it is told.* As a news item, then as a warning to wives.

**B11. The cure: femininity, taught.**
1. *Claim.* Healing comes through healthy friendships with straight women and learning to be feminine.
2. *Where.* Exodus PR-09 PDF p. 22 (a woman learns to shop for clothes with a friend). FOF female PDF p. 17 (a "makeover"
   session at the annual Exodus conference). Women's programme, `ocr/F022.txt` ~l. 2876 (taken shopping for clothes and
   make-up). Dress codes and "False Images" (FI) in B1 F24 and F022: "mannish/boyish attire (on women)" is banned.
3. *How it is told.* Always inside a first-person story. The practice (a makeover session; clothes coaching; a dress code)
   is documented three times. **The stories are testimony: see §5.**

### 8B. What they add to the causal model (§2)

#### 8B1. Gay men (1997, Daniel)

**1. The earliest outline: a needs model (1989).**
1. *Claim.* Being gay comes from many responses to a lifelong search for love. A baby has needs. When they go unmet, he
   adapts: he squirms, then cries, then soon learns the ropes. Parents give or withhold: wanted or unwanted, loved or
   unloved, acceptance without performance. Then peers and teachers mirror his self-image back.
2. *The link, in three causes.* (a) loss of the same-sex parent's love; (b) an identity struggle with labels; (c) real or
   perceived rejection, for school, sport, body, masculinity or femininity.
3. *Where.* B3 F3, PDF pp. 2–3.
4. *Quote.* "This is an outside freedom, but an inside bondage." (p. 3, in bold)
5. *How it is told.* Numerals, one idea a line. A turn in capitals with two question marks: HOW DOES ALL THIS RELATE TO
   HOMOSEXUALITY??. Then a heading for a young listener: what if I think I might be gay? Then three questions: do you want
   a family; will society accept you; will it hurt your family? Then the claim that a celibate gay life will not satisfy.
   It ends on a house with no foundation: paint it, plant flowers, it still falls (p. 4).
6. *What it grants.* Its first point: there is a lack of agreement in science, in religion and among gay people. It also
   puts temperament in the womb.
7. *Also.* A mother who wants to do everything for the child; an abusive, oppressive or absent father; a gym teacher's
   mockery (the speaker's own asides).

**2. The broken triangle, by age three (1984; 1995).**
1. *Claim.* The deepest root is a break in family bonds. A child's security rests on three bonds: mother to child, father
   to child, father to mother. A real break and a perceived one have the same result. A child knows if he was wanted by six
   months. "Gender identity" forms between 18 and 36 months.
2. *Where.* B2 F13, PDF pp. 20–21 (printed 12–13).
3. *Quote.* A heading: "Homosexual by Three?" The answer given: no, but the roots may be in place.
4. *Note.* This is §2A's triangle (A2), seventeen years before the 2001 FOF booklet.

**3. The identity transfer.**
1. *Claim.* A boy needs three things from his father: STRENGTH, POWER and PROTECTION. Men INITIATE; women RESPOND. A boy must
   be ACTIVE, not PASSIVE, a RISK-TAKER. Much gay desire is a FATHER REPLACEMENT search. A man is ready to marry when the
   wish to be protected becomes a wish to protect. A present but indifferent father harms more than an absent one.
2. *Where.* B2 F13, PDF pp. 21–24.
3. *How it is told.* Key words in capitals inside the text. Beside each paragraph, a numbered question with space to write:
   "12. Were you a wanted child?" "15. … active or passive?"

1. *Claim.* A boy with an absent father withdraws. Three results. FEAR, of abandonment. ISOLATION: boys mock his

   ENVY and a wish to possess, usually one boy. At puberty the envy "becomes eroticized".
2. *Where.* B2 F13, PDF pp. 23–26.

### 8C. Rentzel's "dependency", exactly (B2 F9; B1 F4)

*Why it matters. The piece's platform files Vera's 2014 story as "emotional dependency" and publishes her as saying a
dependency "had taken the place of God" (`data/dialog/s3_vera.json`). This is the source's own frame.*

1. **Definition.** Dependency is when another person's ongoing presence and care is "believed necessary for personal
   security" (1987 PDF p. 4; 1990 p. 21). The care listed: attention, listening, admiration, counsel, affirmation, time.
2. **It is sin, with or without sex.** A friendship becomes sin when it becomes dependent, whether or not there is physical
   involvement (1987 p. 4; 1990 p. 22).
3. **The sin is idolatry.** A relationship is "made central to existence instead of God" (1987 p. 4). Vera's published
   line is a paraphrase of this sentence.
4. **The roots are spiritual first.** Four: covetousness, idolatry, rebellion, mistrust of God. Then hurts: low
   self-esteem, rejection, bitterness (1987 p. 6; 1990 p. 24). The 1990 edition adds families with alcoholism or abuse
   (p. 23). **There is no mother wound and no infancy story.** That frame (dependency "planted" by puberty, §2B B7) is the
   2001 FOF booklet's later layer.
5. **Officially not about lesbians.** It can happen in any friendship, in marriage, between parent and child (1987 p. 7).
   The 1990 edition opens with three cases: two men at a church camp, a man and a woman, two married women (pp. 19–21). The
   word "lesbian" never appears.
6. **But it is pictured as two women.** The 1987 cover is a line drawing of two women, one resting her head on the other,
   eyes closed. Subtitle: "A Threat to Close Friendships" (p. 1). The 1987 text's only case is two married women who become
   "emotionally and physically involved" (p. 3). The 1988 phone manual already called dependency the base of most lesbian
   relationships (§2B, B7).
7. **The signs: 12 (11 in 1990).** Jealousy and wanting her to yourself; wanting time alone together; anger or depression
   if she pulls back slightly; losing other friends; romantic or sexual fantasy; thinking about her all the time; no plans
   without her; not seeing her faults; being defensive about it (1987 only); too much physical affection; speaking for her;
   a closeness that makes others uneasy (1987 pp. 4–5; 1990 p. 22).
   **The manipulations: 16.** Sharing money and moving in together; gifts and cards; wearing each other's clothes; poetry
   and music; hugs, back rubs, tickling; looks; flattery; pet names and private jokes; hiding disagreement; needing help;
   guilt; threats of suicide or of leaving the faith; sulking; turning her from other friends; making her insecure; filling
   her time (1987 pp. 7–8; 1990 pp. 25–26).
8. **The marriage exception (1990 only).** After the list, romantic gestures and exclusivity are called "completely
   appropriate in marriage and engagement" (1990 PDF p. 26). Twice more, marriage is exempt from the cure: bonds must be
   broken, except in marriage (p. 28); separate, but not in marriage (p. 30).
   - So the same acts are love between husband and wife, and "manipulation" between two women.
   - The booklet has three boxes: healthy friendship, dependency, marriage. Two women who love each other fit only the
     middle one.
   - **This is the 2016 tape's collapse, in the source's own words.** Vera's sent line ("I don't have a word for the part
     that wasn't") names the missing box.
9. **Why it is hard to break.** It gives security, intimacy, self-worth, relief from boredom; its ups and downs can become
   addictive. The culture has turned "God is love" into "Love is God". The 1987 and 1990 texts call for spiritual warfare,
   "prayer, fasting and deliverance", and ask that ties be broken with every past partner (1987 pp. 9–10; 1990 pp. 27–28).
10. **The way out.** Confess to a third person, not to her. Separate slowly; meet only in groups. Throw out the albums or
    tapes with "our song" on them (1990 p. 30). Grieve; it can hurt like a divorce. Make new friends. Prepare for a long
    war (pp. 29–32).
11. **The promise.** It is "one of the most curable ailments known to man" (1987 p. 13; 1990 says "humanity").
12. **Editions.** First a series in LIA's newsletter, © 1984. Exodus booklet, 1987, priced $1.00 (back cover, p. 15).
    InterVarsity Press, 1990. Both 1990 copies have a print line ending "11 / 02": by the usual convention, the 11th printing,
    in 2002 [VERIFY the convention]. The 1990 text softens a little: "our dependency as sin" becomes "the sinful aspects"; a
    quoted phrase about "homosexual desirability" is dropped; a 1987 codependency self-help book is added to the sources.
13. **The note in the folder.** See §8A, handwriting item 2. Unattributed. Not proposed for the piece.

**In one line.** Rentzel frames dependency as idolatry: to need a person in place of God is sin, even without sex. Its signs
are the ordinary signs of love. The 1990 edition blesses the same signs inside a marriage.
