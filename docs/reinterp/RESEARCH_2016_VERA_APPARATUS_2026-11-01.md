## 3. The apparatus 2016 teaches

### 3a. The statement

> **The testimony pipeline.** A ministry platform turns a woman's private account into a managed asset. It takes the account through a *template* (the form decides what a story is), a *house style* (a kind person edits what the form allowed), *distribution* (four places at once, then cross-posts and screenshots) and *reuse* (a campaign about a law, months later) while the *raw version stays in an archive the woman cannot see*. The edited version is the one that travels. No step is cruel. Each is described as help.

**What the visitor should be able to say on leaving the era:** "What I send to an online ministry is kept, can be edited by someone who cares about me, and can be used for something I never agreed to. I cannot take it back." In digital-risk terms: **consent scope creep, persistence without recall, an editor with a care voice, and metrics that reward the tidy version.**

**In-fiction reading ("what is taught to the people").** The network also *teaches the women* the form: the template's prompts ("what changed", "who held you up", "what hope can you give") are how a woman learns to narrate herself before anyone edits a word. The testimony genre is documented (section 4, rows 10 and 11); the prompts here are reconstruction and labelled so.

### 3b. Argued against the other eras

| | The apparatus | Its verb on the person | What it does with her words | Who is the editor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1997** | The mentor channel and the intake form (Daniel) | **Finds and files** | Sorts them into a category; the first account is the form itself | A friendly stranger, one at a time |
| **2003** | Accountability software and testimony production (Daniel) | **Watches, and asks her to give her account** | Collects the account on camera with a release; reports go to a partner | The person herself (she is told what a good story contains) and her partner |
| **2016** | The testimony pipeline (Vera) | **Publishes without her** | Edits to house style, distributes, reuses as evidence, archives the raw | A colleague who is also a former subject (Vera) |
| **2026** | The detransition agent and automated interpretation (Maya) | **Infers, and does not need her to speak** | Reads her traces, files them, recommends the "pause", speaks as her default | No one visible |

Why this is the right one for 2016 and not another:

- **Not accountability reports.** They are 2003's (Covenant Eyes, phones from 2009 and 2012). In 2016 they are present but not new; the platform still carries a "Walk With" report (already built) as furniture.
- **Not search and ranking steering.** The recommendation practice (the existing card on GPAHE's audits) is 2026's centre.
- **Not social media lifestyle.** Lifestyle posts show the product, not the mechanism. They make a vivid scene but do not teach a risk the visitor can act on.
- **The pipeline is the one new thing 2016 adds** between a person who speaks (2003) and a system that reads (2026): the **human intermediary at scale**. It is also the only one where the player's hands are on the control, which suits a piece that makes the visitor perform the apparatus.

### 3c. Weaponised care as the cycle the pipeline runs

His article's cycle (injury judged, correction offered, testimony used as evidence, self-feeding) is *the pipeline's data flow*:

1. **Injury judged:** the template's tag ("emotional dependency", "relational brokenness") classifies a woman's love as the wound.
2. **Correction offered:** house style ("name the dependency, not the person"; "close on hope") and the "refresh" are offered as kindness: "gentler words reach further".
3. **Testimony used as evidence:** the edited story goes into a campaign kit for Malta.
4. **Self-feeding:** the published story ("seen by 1,211") recruits the next woman, whose intake is the next story ("New testimony received. Awaiting stewardship.").

Vera sits at step 2 and is on the receiving end of step 1 and 3 (her own 2014 story). That is why she is both perpetrator and victim, as the narrative's thesis says.

## 6. The testimony chain (article section)

**From confession to curation to inference: how testimony moves from the person to the machine.**

Across the piece's three digital eras, the same act repeats: a life is turned into proof that a person has changed. What changes is *who does the turning* and *how visible they are*. In 2003 Daniel is asked to **produce** his testimony: to speak, in front of a camera, to a script he was handed, and to sign a release. The editing hand is his own and his partner's, and he can refuse to speak. In 2016 Vera is the person **transforming** testimony into institutional evidence: she receives other women's accounts, applies a house style, distributes them, and sees them reused in a campaign about a law, while her own earlier account has been edited in the same way. The editing hand is a colleague's, a woman who loves the speaker and was edited herself. She can refuse to apply a correction, but the pipeline, the schedule and the archive continue without her. In 2026 Maya encounters a technology that performs some of that interpretive work **automatically**: it reads her traces (a search half-typed, a photograph, a coat), files them under the old record, and recommends a "pause". The editing hand is no one's. She is never asked to speak, so there is nothing to refuse.

We offer this as a research proposition about **dual-use narrative affordances**, not as a claim of unbroken technical lineage. Recording, templating, tagging, scheduling and recommending are ordinary tools that serve care and correction alike; each era's version reuses the same affordances with less friction and less visibility. Three things change along the chain. **Authorship** moves from the speaker, to an editor, to a classifier. **Visibility of the editing hand** shrinks from a person you can see, to a role you can name, to a default you cannot find. **Refusability** shrinks with it: Daniel can decline to speak, Vera can decline to apply a rule but not stop a queue, and Maya cannot decline what she never said.

The chain is **weaponised care** in the sense of the project lead's concept: care and love turned into an instrument of correction, "whatever its makers intend, even if through love". In his cycle (injury judged, correction offered, testimony used as evidence, and a loop that feeds itself), each era supplies a new medium for the same three moves. The literature gives us two anchors we do not overstate: Foucault's account of confession as a ritual of truth-telling inherited from Christian pastoral power, and ethnographic work on ex-gay programmes (Erzen 2006). We found no source that applies the first to ex-gay testimony, so the link is ours.

What is documented, and what is not, matters here. Documented: ex-gay testimony as media from the late 1990s; the movement's women's-strand vocabulary of "emotional dependency"; phone-based accountability reporting from 2009; application-only, moderated women's forums; and, after 2016, movements that collect testimonies in order to put them before lawmakers (2018). **Not found:** any source describing a ministry *editing* testimonies to a house style, any ministry analytics steering that editing, or a testimony campaign tied to Malta in 2016. The piece's Vera-as-editor is therefore *the fiction's extrapolation*, marked as such in its own sources.

## 8. Risks

1. **Erasing the lesbian a second time.** If she appears only as the thing cut, the piece repeats the edit. Guard: Nuala is a person (not a symbol); Dee is a person; Orla's laughter and Mam's Sundays (STORIES §3) stay available; the lesbian is on screen as a *voice* in "as sent".

3. **Weaponised-care framing blames the volunteers.** The group, Deirdre, Bea and the colleagues are never satirised. The *platform's voice* may charm; the charm is the propaganda and it collapses. Vera's care for Renata is sincere in the text.
4. **Overclaiming from the record.** The editing step, the analytics, the kit and the Malta campaign are not documented. The dossier cards must carry `speculative` for those and say what could not be sourced (existing cards 13, 14, 18, 20).
5. **Anachronism.** CHANGED is 2018; Covenant Eyes screenshots are 2019; Instagram Stories were 2 Aug 2016 and Highlights later. None is drawn in 2016 except as a descendant in the dossier.
6. **The Malta date.** Act LV was passed 5 Dec and published 9 Dec. The built lock screen reads 13 Dec and Bea's first message says the bill is "about to" be made an offence. That reads as before the vote. Either move the lock screen to a date before 5 Dec, or reword Bea's first message as "they voted". I read this from `s3_maiden.json`'s `_docMalta`, not the player build; check the screen.
7. **"Trans as trigger".** I interpreted his phrase as *a trans-related controversy used as an engagement trigger on social media*. If he meant something else (for example, trans content as a trigger for the characters), the beat changes. Guards: no satire of the clinical debate; if Noa's two corrections appear, both stay, unresolved; do not stage lesbians as opposed to trans women; do not name the retailer or the petition's sponsor; no real posts.
8. **Marriage pressure without a husband.** Dropping Thomas removes the notes' strongest pressure device. The loss is real. If the lead wants it back, put it on a friend (an anniversary post on the group feed) so it stays a pressure on Vera, not a plot about a man.
9. **The kit as a moral test.** If the Approve press reads as a choice scored by the piece, it breaks the frame law. It must converge both ways and be filed nowhere.
10. **Take-down as a heroic fix.** The counts and the "retained" line make it plain it is not. Do not animate a success.
11. **Clearance.** "Story Record", "Freedom to Choose", "Story Steward" and "SisterSignal" are untested invented marks. Run a search before use. I did not.
