## THE PROMPT

You are researching for a scholarly art project at the **University of Bergen, Center for Digital
Narrative** (`SurvivingSOGICE`). The work is a narrative game about how organised
**conversion-practice networks (SOGICE — sexual orientation and gender identity change efforts)**
recruit, retain and surveil queer people online across thirty years. It is critical of those networks
and is made with and for survivors.

I need two research pulls. **Both are about FORM, not about individual people.** I am writing fiction
and I need the fiction's shape to be true even though every name in it is invented.

### PULL 1 — Peer accountability groups in ex-gay / ex-lesbian networks

The scene I am writing: a small, private, phone-based group of women who describe themselves as
former lesbians, checking in with each other daily. It is the only place in the era where other
people appear as people. **It must never be satirised** — these are women trying to survive
something in each other's company, and the piece's position is that they are not stupid for being
there.

Tell me:

1. **The documented FORM of peer accountability groups** in ex-gay / ex-lesbian and "sexual purity"
   networks — group size, how membership is granted, whether there is a leader or facilitator,
   cadence of contact, whether check-ins are scheduled or continuous.
2. **The mechanics of accountability itself** — what members are expected to disclose, to whom, how
   often; whether disclosure is public to the group or private to a sponsor; what happens on a
   reported lapse; the vocabulary used for lapse, for progress, for a member who leaves.
3. ⚑ **REGISTER — how members actually address one another.** This is the part I most need and the
   part most sources skip. Terms of address, greetings, the standard shapes of encouragement, the
   standard shapes of correction. Whether scripture is quoted directly or referenced. How warmth and
   surveillance sit in the same sentence — because in these groups they usually do.
4. **The specifically LESBIAN / women's strand.** Where women's ex-gay ministry differs from men's:
   the different framing (emotional dependency, "relational brokenness", the wound-and-father /
   mother-wound theories), the different vocabulary, the different assumed cause. Name the strands
   and where they came from.
5. **How these groups moved onto phones and messaging apps** (roughly 2010–2018), and what that
   changed: persistence, screenshotting, who can read a backlog, whether leaving a group is visible.
6. ⚑ **What a CARICATURE of this would look like** — the clichés a writer reaches for that the record
   does not support. I want to know what to avoid as much as what to write.

### PULL 2 — Family-contact practice

The scene: a worker at a conversion-practice organisation has a queue of messages from **family
members** — mostly mothers — asking about someone who has stopped speaking to them, and her job is
to answer them.

Tell me:

1. **How these organisations solicit and handle family contact.** Who initiates, what the intake
   looks like, whether the family is a client, a referral source, or both.
2. **What families are told** about progress, about contact, about the person's own wishes; and what
   they are told to DO — the scripts and standard advice given to parents.
3. ⚑ **The documented instruction to restrict or condition contact** — where the record shows
   families being coached to withdraw, to set conditions on support, or to route communication
   through the organisation. Be precise about what is documented and what is alleged.
4. **The organisation's own register when it writes to a family** — is it pastoral, clinical,
   administrative? Sample the tone, do not invent it.
5. **Who does this work inside such an organisation**, and what it is called internally.

### RULES ON SOURCING — these are binding, and a pull that ignores them cannot be used

- ⚑ **Cite everything.** Survivor testimony collections, court records, investigative journalism,
  organisational documents, academic work on ex-gay ministry. Give me the source for each claim,
  with enough detail to find it.
- ⚑ **Anything you cannot source, mark `[VERIFY SOURCE]` and say so plainly.** An uncited claim that
  arrives dressed as a fact is worse than no claim: this project has a rule that uncited material
  carries that tag until the lead checks it, and I would rather have five sourced sentences than
  fifty unsourced ones.
- ⚑ **Distinguish DOCUMENTED from CONTESTED from ALLEGED**, explicitly, per claim. The project's
  dossier cards require exactly this three-way status and cannot accept material without it.
- **Do not invent organisations, programmes, or people.** If a real organisation is the source of a
  practice, name it and cite it — the piece may NAME organisations where the record documents them,
  but every mark used in the fiction is invented.
- **Do not reproduce survivor testimony verbatim.** Summarise the shape; never hand me sentences a
  real person said about their own life. I will write the fiction's own words.
- **Geographic and temporal scope:** the era is set around 2016, and Europe matters (Malta's Act LV
  of 2016 is in the piece). Anglophone material is fine and expected, but say where each practice is
  documented rather than implying a universal.
- **Where the record is thin, say so.** "There is little published on X" is a usable finding. A
  confident answer built on nothing is not.
