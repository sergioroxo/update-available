Prepared 2026-10-02 for Sérgio. Brief (his words, 2026-10-02): before the Close's panels, the piece is "missing the information about SOGICE and why this is important — the educational aspect that helps close the narrative". After the 2026 machine fails: "L will be waiting for the next update — coming soon", then a short run of plain text panels saying (a) violence against LGBTQIA+ people keeps rising, (b) SOGICE / conversion practices keep existing, rebranding and resurfacing internationally.

## 0. The most important finding: the two halves of the brief are not equally supported

- **(b) SOGICE persists, rebrands, resurfaces internationally: well supported** (UN Independent Expert 2020, GPAHE 2022/2024, ILGA World 2026, the EU Commission's own 2026 Communication, FRA 2024).
- **(a) "Violence keeps rising": supported only in part, and the data are EU/European.** The FRA survey shows harassment up sharply (37% to 55% in the past year, 2019 to 2023) and the five-year attack rate up slightly (11% to 14%), but the past-12-months attack rate was **unchanged at 5%**. The Trans Murder Monitoring 2025 figure **fell** (350 to 281, global), and TGEU itself says the fall likely reflects under-reporting. The Hate Review (Forbidden Colours, Sept 2026) is about the organised anti-gender *movement* and its legal reach; it contains **no violence statistics** (the continental analysis, pp. 1–29, never mentions violence against LGBTIQ+ people; the word occurs about 120 times in the file, all inside country fiches, which I did not tabulate). ILGA-Europe's Annual Review 2026 reports escalating state repression (Pride bans, "propaganda"/"extremism" laws) and country-level incident reports but, in the pages read, no single aggregate violence figure.
- So the honest wording for (a) is: **hostility, harassment and legal pressure are rising in Europe, and violence is higher than it was a few years earlier in the one EU-wide survey that measured it** — not "violence is surging everywhere". Both draft sequences are written to that standard.

## 1. Key findings from The Hate Review (Forbidden Colours, September 2026)

Source id **S1**: Reillon, Vincent. *The Hate Review: How the Anti-Gender Movement Spread Across Europe, 2000–2025.* Brussels: Forbidden Colours, September 2026. Local file read: `/Users/sergiogalvaoroxo/Documents/Shame Database/Relevant/Part 12/c/The_Hate_Review_-_Full_Report_-_Forbidden_Colours_-_September_2026.md` (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, per the report's colophon). Page numbers below are the report's own footer numbers ("The Hate Review 2026 N"), which the markdown preserves; the two-column PDF text is interleaved, so quote only after checking against the PDF.

**What it is.** Forbidden Colours is a Brussels civil-society organisation that monitors anti-rights actors (advocacy publisher; says so on p. 1 "About the publisher"). The report rests on "more than 3,200 elements" — individually sourced documented events, mid-1990s to May 2026, across 47 countries (46 Council of Europe member states plus Russia) (p. 4). It says its figures are "a floor, not a ceiling" (p. 5). Scores (0–4) for "contamination" (spread in society) and "impact" (reach into law) are *expert-judgement ratings* on seven dimensions, not mechanical counts (p. 5).

**Findings, in my own words:**
1. **No country is untouched.** By 2021–25 not one of the 47 countries is in the lowest band of organised anti-gender activity; Western and Northern Europe are "principal theatres", not spectators (p. 6; summary p. 1). Highest: Russia, Georgia, Hungary, Turkey, Azerbaijan; heavily affected also Italy, Spain, the UK (p. 6, p. 10 Table 2).
2. **It almost never recedes.** Contamination "behaves like a chronic infection that never clears"; no country's overall contamination fell on balance over 25 years; relief after a hostile government leaves is "shallow" and reversible (p. 8). Poland is the only recorded exit from state capture and its impact "has barely moved"; Hungary's April 2026 change of government is "the next test" (p. 11; p. 29).
3. **Laws are not protection.** Countries with the strongest legal records (it names Spain, Portugal, Germany, the Netherlands, Malta) can still carry high contamination; low contamination, not good law, is what safety consists of (p. 11–12).
4. **From street to state.** The movement's centre of gravity moved from protest to litigation, legislation and regulation (p. 13–14). Its success rate in contesting progressive measures rose from "under a third" in the early 2000s to "well over half" in 2021–25; "for the first time" the most frequent outcome is the progressive measure defeated (p. 14). It authored its own measures in **42 countries** in the latest window vs **13** in the early 2000s; roughly a third of anti-gender bills that reach a decision pass, but regulations and policies "outnumber adopted laws by nearly five to one" (p. 15–16).
5. **Targets moved to trans people.** "Legal gender recognition and healthcare" grew "exponentially" to become the third-largest topic overall (p. 21); "the frontier has moved from marriage towards the rights of trans people" (p. 22). (Handle with care; see §6.)
6. **Portable narratives.** Twelve narratives, three near-universal: protect the children (42 countries), the "natural family" (36), equality as foreign imposition (36) (p. 23, Table 3 p. 25). The narrative "homosexuality as illness and as curable / contagion / less than human" is documented in **21 countries**, "present throughout and stable; never displaced" (p. 24; Table 3 p. 25).
7. **Transnational layer.** Transnational actors documented in all countries but one; ADF International, CitizenGO and Ordo Iuris named as the largest; they supply campaign, legal strategy and meeting places, while domestic actors write the measures (p. 19).
8. **Civic space closes in proportion.** Correlation between a country's impact score and the space left for progressive actors: +0.80 (2021–25) (p. 26–27).
9. **Its own conclusion:** fighting each bill "is symptom management on a system built to reload"; the durable lever is the actors, mobilisation and narratives themselves (p. 12; conclusion p. 28–29).

**What it says about conversion practices specifically.** There is **no continental analysis** of conversion practices: the term occurs only inside country fiches (about 60 in-context mentions) and in one narrative line (finding 6). The fiches give a consistent, citable pattern:
- *Bans passed, then attacked:* Malta first EU state (2016 Act; p. 250); Cyprus May 2023 (p. 101; far-right party proposed amendments to dilute it, p. 103); Belgium July 2023 (p. 69); Iceland June 2023, 53–0 (p. 193); Norway Dec 2023, 85–15 (p. 294); Portugal law passed 21 Dec 2023, in force 1 Mar 2024 (p. 314), and a petition of ~17,000 signatures to repeal it (with the 2018 gender-recognition law) was submitted 20 March 2026 (p. 317); a litigation group affiliated with Alliance Defending Freedom petitioned the Ombudsman against it in 2025 (p. 318); Spain's national Trans Law banned conversion practices (p. 375).
- *Weak or partial:* Greece May 2022 partial ban (minors, paid practitioners only; critics said this left faith-based practitioners outside) (p. 175); Spain: the **only** sanction ever attempted (a fine of €20,001 on a coach offering to "cure" homosexuality) was crowdfunded by an advocacy group and then annulled on procedural grounds, so "no effective sanction … has ever been imposed" (p. 378); in May 2025 the PP–Vox majority in Valencia reintroduced "voluntary conversion practices framed as accompaniment" via the regional trans law (p. 376).
- *Not enacted:* Austria — "no conversion practices ban has been enacted despite repeated parliamentary resolutions" (p. 51, p. 54); UK — ban promised since 2018, no bill introduced; a pledge to legislate a trans-inclusive ban was repeated in the King's Speech of 13 May 2026 (p. 422); Finland — parliament approved a citizens' initiative in March 2025 but "the government refused implementation" (p. 130); Netherlands — opposition recorded on every motion since 2019 (p. 278); Switzerland — National Council backed a federal ban motion 143–37 in Dec 2022, but the Council of States rejected it and both chambers have since declined cantonal initiatives pending a report (p. 394); Denmark's centre-left government of June 2026 pledges a ban for minors (p. 114).
- *Medicalised/"curable" language in politics:* in Slovakia a Christian-Democrat founder said homosexuality was curable and a former health minister said the "curability" rate "could be as high as 50 percent"; a Slovak think-tank "promotes conversion practices" (p. 362).
- *International defence of a conversion-practices case:* in Malta, a prosecution under the 2016 Act for advertising conversion practices ended in acquittal on 4 March 2026; the defence was coordinated by a UK legal centre with support from two transnational conversion-advocacy networks, and the outcome was promoted internationally as "persecution" (p. 251–253; independently confirmed by MaltaToday, S20). **Contested; see §6 — I recommend not putting it on screen.**

Not in the Hate Review: any statistic on violence against LGBTQIA+ people, prevalence of conversion practices, or online conversion material. Do not cite it for those.

## 2. SOGICE persistence: rebranding, resurfacing, international coordination, legal picture (2026)

### 2.1 Rebranding and resurfacing (documented)
- **UN Independent Expert (A/HRC/44/53, 1 May 2020)**, ¶40: participants in an expert meeting said conversion practices became "a moving target … difficult to identify, monitor and punish"; "when facing restrictions or bans on practices specifically denominated 'conversion therapy', proponents have rebranded and reshaped their communications strategies." ¶86: the practices "provoke profound psychological and physical damage … of all ages, in all regions of the world." ¶83: they are "by their very nature degrading, inhuman and cruel and create a significant risk of torture." ¶87(a): recommends States **ban** them, including advertising, in all settings, public or private, with sanctions and no public funds. ¶75: at the time only a few States (the report names Ecuador and Malta) treat it as a criminal practice; bans existed at local level in Canada, Spain and the US.
- **GPAHE (Global Project Against Hate and Extremism)**, the sentence the project lead cites: "Conversion therapy never goes away, no matter the scandals, reports of its harms, official condemnations, or legislative bans. The movement, under new names and with new justifications and 'therapies,' continually rebrands and resurfaces to carry on its demonization of LGBTQ+ people in a coordinated and international way." Exact location: the **Introduction of "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players"** (S2); repeated verbatim in the "Related report" blurb on the companion report "Conversion Therapy Online: The Ecosystem" (S3). Dates: GPAHE's own text dates the research release to **January 2022** (S5: "In January 2022, the Global Project Against Hate and Extremism (GPAHE) published extensive research"); the joint press post is **12 January 2022** (S4); the web pages' own metadata read 21 Dec 2021 (Players) and 28 Dec 2021 (Ecosystem). Recommend citing it as **GPAHE, January 2022** and noting the pages went up in late December 2021. Authors are not named on the pages. It is an advocacy audit (search-result audits in six countries and four languages; platforms Google, Bing, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, some PayPal and Alexa).
- **What the audit found (2022):** "reintegrative therapy" and "unwanted same-sex attraction" returned mostly unauthoritative or harmful results; non-English results were worse; social-media bans were "largely ineffective" in 2022 (S3, S4). **2024 follow-up** ("The Ecosystem in 2023", published 23 Jan 2024; S5): improvement in the US and on TikTok/Bing after deplatforming, but outside the US Google and YouTube returned the least authoritative results in non-English languages; "same-sex attraction" and "unwanted same-sex attraction" still returned "dismal results for all platforms in all languages"; results disproportionately sent users to **American** content and organisations; and a Russian site appeared across languages.
- **The project's own research file** (S21, `sogice-social-media-layer.md`, prepared 1 Oct 2026) agrees and adds dated incidents: a removed provider back under a new handle by 2021; a renamed YouTube channel after removals; Meta's rules loosened in Jan 2025 (effect on conversion content not measured); GLAAD's May 2026 index credits only TikTok and Meta with an explicit ban on promotion. All labelled in that file; see its "Contested points".

### 2.2 International coordination (documented, with the usual limits)
- The Hate Review documents a transnational layer in 46 of 47 countries (p. 19), and the Malta case shows UK and international conversion-advocacy networks backing a local case (p. 251).
- GPAHE names the International Federation for Therapeutic and Counseling Choice (founded 2018 with a Norwegian provider's help, per GPAHE) as a transnational network; GPAHE 2024 documents Brazilian networks promoting one another and appearing together at events (S2, S5).
- The UN expert's own conclusion is that the practices harm people "in all regions of the world" (A/HRC/44/53 ¶86), and the report names practices in many countries on every continent; this is the best-sourced framing for "international".

### 2.3 The legal picture in 2026
- **Worldwide:** ILGA World's data update of 1 June 2026 (maps dated May 2026): **"17 UN member States have nationwide bans on 'conversion therapies'"** (S8). The 2020 baseline: ILGA World's *Curbing Deception* (Lucas Ramón Mendos, Feb 2020) is the first world survey of legal restrictions (S7); I did not extract a 2020 count from it and do not give one. (GPAHE's Jan 2022 page said "seven countries"; that is a different, older, non-like-for-like count; use ILGA's current number only.)
- **EU:** eight Member States ban: Belgium, Cyprus, France, Germany, Greece, Malta, Portugal, Spain (Commission Communication C(2026) 3333, §2.3; ILGA-Europe 13 May 2026) (S12, S9). Wider Europe: ILGA-Europe counted ten countries as of September 2024 — the eight plus **Iceland and Norway** (S10; dated). The Communication says sanctions include fines in all eight, imprisonment in most; advertising banned in all.
- **The European Citizens' Initiative "Ban on conversion practices in the European Union":** registered 24 Jan 2024; signatures collected 17 May 2024 – 17 May 2025; submitted 17 Nov 2025; **over 1.1 million valid signatures; thresholds reached in 11 Member States** (S11, S12). Parliament held a public hearing on 2 March 2026 and a plenary debate on 25 March 2026. **Commission reply, 13 May 2026:** it "intends to adopt a recommendation" in **2027** calling on Member States to ban conversion practices; it "considers that it would not be effective" to propose a legal act of the Union (reasons: euro-crimes needs a new unanimous Council decision; the 2008 Equal Treatment Directive has been blocked for almost 18 years; the Victims' Rights Directive is already revised) (S12 §3). It will hold a first LGBTIQ+ policy forum on conversion practices "before the end of 2026" and has launched a study. So: **no EU law; a non-binding recommendation promised for 2027.** The European Parliament (29 April 2026) had urged a proposal for a legal act; the European Economic and Social Committee (29 April 2026) supported the ECI; PACE Resolution 2534 (29 Jan 2026) calls on 46 states to ban, adopted 71–26 with 2 abstentions, after what ILGA-Europe describes as a campaign of tens of thousands of auto-generated anti-trans emails to members (S12, S13).
- **Gaps:** the ban is not the same as enforcement (Spain's only sanction annulled; Greece's partial scope; Malta excludes consenting adults, per A/HRC/44/53 ¶76; Finland passed but not implemented; UK, Austria, Netherlands, Switzerland none).

### 2.4 How common it is, in Europe (FRA, the strongest number)
FRA's EU LGBTIQ Survey III (fieldwork 2023, published 14 May 2024; over 100,000 respondents in 30 countries): **24% experienced some kind of "conversion practice"**: 11% interventions by family, 5% prayer/religious ritual/counselling, 3% physical violence, 1% sexual violence, 14% verbal abuse or humiliation (categories overlap); **47% of trans women and 48% of trans men**; 76% of those say they did not consent; similar across age groups (23% of those 55+, 27% of 15–17-year-olds); highest in Greece (38%), Cyprus, Czechia, Estonia, Slovakia (37%) (S15, key findings, and §3.2.5; the Commission's Communication repeats the 24%/47%/48% figures, S12 §2.1).

## 6. Cautions: contested, dated, regional

1. **Geography.** Almost all the "rising" data are **European** (FRA: EU-27 plus Albania, North Macedonia, Serbia; Hate Review: 47 countries; ILGA-Europe: 49 countries). The ILGA World count (17) and the UN report are global. The TMM figure is global and 68% Latin America. Panels must say "in Europe" / "in the EU survey" whenever the number is European. The piece is Bergen-made and its rooms are not geographically fixed, so a panel that says "in Europe" is both true and honest about limits.
2. **"Violence is rising" is the weakest claim in the brief.** See §0. The FRA past-12-month attack rate is flat at 5%; the 5-year rate went 11% to 14% ("small increase", FRA). Harassment is the real increase. Murders per TMM went **down** (and are under-counted). Never let a panel say "murders are rising" or "violence is soaring".
3. **FRA specifics.** Two bases exist: the like-for-like 2019/2023 sub-sample (LGBTI, 14% and 55%) and the full 2023 sample (all LGBTIQ, 13% and 54%). Don't mix them in one sentence. The 24% "conversion practices" figure is **not** all clinical "therapy": 14% is verbal abuse or humiliation, 11% family intervention; the reference period is not stated in the passages I read ("experienced"); the survey's sampling method is in the FRA technical report, which I did not read (linked from the FRA page). Self-reported.
4. **Do not add 17 and 8.** The ILGA World list of the 17 was not retrievable, so I cannot confirm how the EU's eight are counted inside it (some bans are partial or minors-only). Panels cite the 17 (worldwide) and the eight (EU) separately, never as parts of one sentence.
5. **Bans are not enforcement.** Spain's only sanction annulled; Greece's ban is partial; Malta's excludes consenting adults (A/HRC/44/53 ¶76; Hate Review p. 250–253); Finland passed but not implemented; the UK and Austria have none; Switzerland's federal motion failed. "17 countries ban it" ≠ "17 countries protect people".
6. **The ECI outcome is contested.** The Commission calls a 2027 recommendation a way to "meet the substantive objectives"; the European Parliament and the EESC (29 April 2026) asked for a legal act; commentary pieces in my search results disagreed about whether this was adequate. Say exactly what it did and when; do not characterise it as success or failure. A recommendation is non-binding.
7. **The clinical-debate boundary.** The most heated fight over *scope* is whether bans should cover gender identity and whether "exploratory therapy" or "pastoral care" falls under them (UK consultation responses, Hate Review p. 422; PACE debate, S13). That is adjacent to the gender-exploratory clinical debate the project never satirises and never resolves (ETHICS_CONSTRAINTS law 7). The drafts use the same neutral formula as the UN, the EU and PACE ("sexual orientation or gender identity") and take no side on what clinical care is. Do not borrow the Hate Review's or ILGA-Europe's labels for opposing actors ("gender critical", "anti-trans groups") on screen; these are advocacy characterisations and some named groups dispute them.
8. **Advocacy sources.** Forbidden Colours, ILGA-Europe, ILGA World, TGEU and GPAHE are advocacy organisations; FRA, the UN and the EU Commission are institutions. Where a panel rests only on advocacy sources, say who documented it ("a 2022 audit by GPAHE").
9. **Dates.** GPAHE audits are point-in-time (2021–2023). The 2022 ILGA-Europe "most violent year" is 2022 data. The "Ten countries" Europe list is September 2024. The Hate Review is a Sept 2026 report whose data end May 2026. Hungary's April 2026 government change is on record: don't write as if the trend runs in one direction (the Hate Review itself and the ILGA-Europe Rainbow Map 2026 record counter-examples: Poland's exit, Spain's rise to first place, courts holding the line, Poland repealing its last "LGBT-free zone" resolution in 2025, the EU Court of Justice's April 2026 ruling on Hungary's 2021 law).
10. **Malta's March 2026 acquittal (S20, Hate Review p. 251)** is a contested free-speech case about an interview, widely reported by conservative Christian outlets as "persecution" and by Maltese LGBTIQ advocates as a loophole; the court ruled on the 2022 date versus a 2023 amendment. It is **not** evidence that bans fail. Recommend leaving it off the panels.
11. **Named people and groups.** No private individuals are named in the drafts or the panels. Organisations named here are named only as sources, or in the Malta case, the Norwegian provider (documented by GPAHE) and the Spanish crowdfunding group, which I recommend **not** naming on screen: the lead should decide whether a Norwegian provider may be named in a piece made in Norway.
12. **The Hate Review's own limits.** It "reflects the situation from the point of view of anti-gender actors" (p. 4), records only what could be documented, and its scores are judgement. Its "gender critical" section and the "hydra"/"infection" metaphors are the authors' framing.
13. **Tone.** The 2026 register is "direct and educational", not "cosy persuasion" (project memory); the drafts therefore state facts and dates plainly. No panel is addressed to the victim; they speak about the record, not about the player.
14. **What I could not verify (listed so nobody assumes otherwise):** the official OHCHR page for A/HRC/44/53 (HTTP 403; the UN PDF itself was read); the ILGA World Database list of the 17 countries (JavaScript-only); a 2020 count from *Curbing Deception* (page read, PDF not); the FRA technical report's sampling description; any Norwegian or European helpline; a Hate Review PDF (only the markdown was read, so page numbers are footer-derived); the 2025 Rainbow Map; any 2025–26 aggregate violence figure from ILGA-Europe (none found in the pages read).
