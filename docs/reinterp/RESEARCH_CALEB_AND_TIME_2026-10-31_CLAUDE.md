### 2.2 Rules (documented patterns)

Contact between participants [S1]
- "On-level" clients may not speak to each other, except in a life-threatening emergency.
- They may not spend time alone together.
- They may not ride in the same car without permission.
- Dating and exclusive relationships of any kind are prohibited.
- Peer touch is limited to a brief handshake or a brief hand on the shoulder.
- No contact with people who left early, unless staff approve.

Speech and eye contact [S1][S2][S3]
- New clients start in "Safekeeping" for the first two to three days. No words, no gestures, no eye contact.

Music and media [S1]
- No music that is not explicitly Christian. This includes classical music.
- No TV, movies or secular media. One approved G or PG video a week.
- Reading only what staff approve.
- This matches the placement letter's "Leave behind: music, journals, anything from before."

Journals [S1][S2]
- No journaling or diary outside the required written "moral inventories" unless staff approve.
- That is the exact shape of your diary beat: the only writing allowed is writing the programme can read.

Searches and confiscation [S2]
- Clients are searched, body and belongings, before entering. Items staff think inappropriate are held. Photographs of clients may be taken.

Phones and internet [S1]
- No cell phones, beepers, computers, e-mail or internet without approval.

Doors and nights [S1]
- Bedroom doors stay open at all times. A closed door is allowed only for the toilet and one 15-minute wash.
- Silent time at 9 pm. Lights out at 10 pm.

Parents [S1]
- Marked "not to be given to client". Parents are told not to pass on who has called or asked about the client. They are told to keep the client from outside contacts.

Humour and style [S7]
- Ironic or sarcastic humour was discouraged in the adult programme. Staff called it "camping", because it linked residents to their past lives.

"False images" [VERIFY SOURCE]
- I did not find this phrase as a rule. The closest documented things are dress and grooming rules [S1] and the ban on "camping" [S7]. Please check where your phrase came from before it goes in text.

### 2.3 Room-sharing, friendship, attachment

- The theory was that non-sexual same-sex bonds repaired a "deficit". The programme in Erzen's study built belonging on regulated same-sex bonds. [S8][S9] (S8 is from a search summary; the page itself blocked me. [VERIFY SOURCE])
- So friendship was the treatment. A particular, exclusive bond was the danger. The rules above ban exactly that bond. [S1]
- Residents lived communally. They were pushed to share and confess. Secrecy was treated as damaging and could get someone expelled. [S7]
- Weekly small groups used long "accountability sheets" about thoughts and behaviour. Erzen calls this "public intimacy". [S7]
- Falling and being forgiven was a normal cycle. Confessing a lapse could mean forgiveness and staying. [S7][S8]
- One account says the adult residential rooms had no doors. I only saw this in a search summary. [VERIFY SOURCE]
- Love did form inside the apparatus. One co-founder of the largest network left in 1979 to be with another man from the same ministry. They held a ceremony in 1982. [S10][S11][S12] This is the documented pattern behind Caleb. See also LOVE_AS_LIBERATION_SOURCES.

### 2.4 Aftercare, follow-up, "accountability"

- Bussee, on the 1970s model: those who left disappeared. No follow-up existed. Leaders assumed that anyone who stopped coming had relapsed. [S6]
- He also says his whole social circle was inside the ministry. When he left, he had no network. [S6]
- Weekly groups opened with prayer. People confessed the week's lapses. The group prayed over those who had "fallen". [S6]
- Parents had their own meetings: a weekly "Friends and Family" group, monthly open meetings, and from 2007 a four-day "Family Freedom Intensive". [S1][S5]
- Public testimony worked as the programme's outward face. See SOURCES_TESTIMONY (Love Won Out from 1998, the Truth in Love campaign, Smithsonian holdings). [S16]
- Annual conferences brought alumni and staff together. A 1993 documentary visits one. [S13] This makes a chance reunion at an event plausible.
- "Accountability partner" as a named formal role: not confirmed in anything I fetched. A WORLD article mentions a "church-based accountability system". [S14b] [VERIFY SOURCE]
- Harm and law: the UN Independent Expert (2020) found that conversion practices cause severe pain and suffering and can amount to torture or ill-treatment. [S14] Tennessee found two unlicensed facilities at Love in Action in 2005. [S4][S5]

## 5 Virtual pets and streaks

### 5.1 Virtual pets, 1995–2004

- Tamagotchi: sold in Japan in 1996. About 20 million units in Japan in the first year and about 20 million elsewhere. [S54]
- The care list: feeding, play, toilet, discipline ("time-outs"), attention, medicine, sleep. A beep called for attention. The lifecycle was about 12 days and could not be paused. [S54]
- Neglect made the pet sick, then dead. Some children held funerals. [S54]
- In 1997, schools in China, Hong Kong, Thailand and the US banned them. [S54]
- Death was shown differently by market: a ghoul beside a gravestone in Japan, a gentle alien angel in the first US and European models. [S53]
- Neglected pets also evolved into more demanding, "disobedient" forms. [S53]
- One child was inconsolable when his first one died. An adult writer felt "I'd done that" after forgetting hers for a day. [S53]
- Discipline button: pressed when the pet calls for attention although its needs are met. (From a modern re-release manual and fan guides. The original 1996 logic is not confirmed.) Wrong discipline can cost the pet a happy heart on some releases. [S58] [VERIFY SOURCE]
- Control was central. Players managed diet, cleanliness and behaviour. Some outcomes were random. [S55]
- Petz (Dogz 1995, Catz 1996) pets could not die. They showed need by behaviour: getting fat, howling for attention. [S57]
- Neopets (1999): pets do not die from neglect. They get hungry and unhappy. [S57 for the site; death claim from a search summary, [VERIFY SOURCE]]
- Digimon (1997) was Bandai's follow-up, marketed to boys because pets could connect and fight. [S53]
- Giga Pet and Nano Baby: I could not source their mechanics. [VERIFY SOURCE]

Why it worked (sources, not mine):
- Turkle: the Tamagotchi reversed the usual relation. It did not offer to care for you. It asked you to care for it. She calls nurturance a "killer app" and says we grow attached to what we tend. [S56]
- Guilt is part of the design. One review calls the guilt of causing a death one of the strangest qualities in toy history. [search summary, see S53 for the guilt example]

### 5.2 Streaks and habit design

- Duolingo says its streak uses loss aversion on purpose. [S59]
- It reports that learners at a 7-day streak are 2.4 times more likely to return the next day. [S59] (Company data, not independent.)
- It tested a lower bar: one lesson keeps the streak alive. That raised day-14 retention by 3.3%. It also added Streak Freeze and a Weekend Amulet, which made people more likely to come back. [S60]
- A designer analysis of 859 streak designs says streaks are about losing, and that urgent copy and timers turn "keep going" into "defend the record". [S62] (A design blog. Treat as opinion.)
- Peer-reviewed: Silverman and Barasch (2023) ran seven studies. Showing an intact streak raised later engagement. Showing a broken one lowered it. The effect is stronger when people blame themselves for the break. It is weaker when they can repair the streak. [S61]
- The "repair" finding matters for you: the Caleb script has Lamby say "we'll get the days back together." That is a repair offer. In real design, repair softens loss. In Restorify it is used to bind.
- Snapchat streaks: sources describe them as a duty. Most are parenting blogs. [S69] [VERIFY SOURCE]

### 5.3 Critiques of care as compliance

- Foucault's "pastoral power": a power that cares for and directs people so that they govern themselves. Confession and obedience are its tools. [S66] (A secondary article that applies the idea; I did not find a primary text.)
- Bogost calls gamification "exploitationware". It keeps tracking and loses what makes games good. [S63]
- Gamified systems can steer people toward what can be monetised, with little given back. [S63]
- A 2025 working paper on AI companions found that many apps use guilt or fear-of-missing-out in farewell messages (37 to 43% depending on the count). It found curiosity and anger drove people to stay, more than guilt. [S64][S65] (Do not overstate: guilt itself did not explain the extra use.)
- Sara Ahmed: happiness can be a promise tied to certain "happy objects" such as the family. People who do not feel it are cast as the problem. Queer people are written into that role. [S67]
- Puig de la Bellacasa: care can feel good or bad. It can do good or it can oppress. [S68] (From a blog quote of her book. [VERIFY SOURCE])
- In the programme world itself: Erzen's account of accountability sheets and public confession is a documented case of "care" that is also inspection. [S7]

### 5.4 What this gives the Lamby design

- A pet that asks to be cared for is a proven way to produce attachment and guilt. It worked on children. [S53][S56]
- A streak that can be "repaired" keeps people inside. A streak you blame yourself for breaking hurts more. [S61]
- The gap in the market, and the piece's argument: a pet needs you. A programme says its pet needs you. The needs are the programme's.

## 8 Proposal: the Lamby pet streak in 2003 (PROPOSAL)

### 8.1 The idea in a paragraph

Make the daily check-in a care object. The 2003 Restorify window gets a small Lamby on a little LCD-style screen, with a few buttons. You press them. It reacts. The streak sits beside it. Lamby is "doing well" when you do what the programme asks. The "Personalise your guide" grid disappears. In its place, Lamby grows into a form based on how you answered. The point: the pet's needs are the programme's duties. When the commit-press comes, the pet is the first thing the programme uses against you. Then the whole thing collapses into silence.

### 8.2 What you press

Two ways to show it. Your call which.
- A. On screen: a pixel LCD inside the Restorify window (the "Today" page). It fits the browser and the 2D desktop canvas.
- B. In the hand: a keychain "Restorify Companion" object, like the flip phone in 2003 (VR-02). In the browser it is a 2D widget. In VR you press it with a finger.

Buttons (modelled on the 1996 care list [S54][S53], renamed in the programme's vocabulary):
- WORD (feed): a line for the day appears on the LCD. Lamby "eats" it. Invented text only.
- FELLOWSHIP (play): Lamby does a small bounce. Operable may charm.
- CONFESS (clean): a short list of "flagged thoughts" is swept off. This is the programme's own word for it.
- CORRECT (discipline): see 8.5. Optional. Never required.
- CHECK-IN: the four existing chips (Steady, Struggling, Grateful, Tired) stay as they are. They are the main daily press.

No beeps. The Tamagotchi's beep demanded attention [S54]. Your rule is no sound. A small blinking dot in the tray does the beep's job.

### 8.3 What it shows

- A 32 by 16 style sprite, nearest-neighbour, 2003 palette from `src/desktop/theme/`. No new colours.
- Two meters. Two hearts each. Suggested names: "Walk" and "Trust". They are the programme's rating of Daniel, drawn as a pet's health.
- The streak: "Purity Streak 412 days" with a new second line, "11 resets". That explains why 412 days is not six years. Existing wording "includes supervised period" stays.
- A week strip of seven pips under the pet. The first pips have a different stamp. They were filled in by staff during the "supervised period". The visitor sees that part of the streak was never his. [See 8.6.]
- Lamby's forms (replacing the Personalise grid): Cheer, Clinical, Sterile. A fourth, Sad, appears only when the streak breaks. You never choose a form. Your check-ins decide it. Steady and Grateful grow Cheer. Struggling grows Clinical. Tired grows Sterile. The ledger shows the cause in a cold witness line.
- Why a form and not a setting: Tamagotchi evolved by care quality, with neglected ones turning demanding. [S53] This is your "personalisation" turned into raising something. And the programme can say, truthfully, that Lamby is like this because of you.

### 8.4 How a sitting becomes days

- Players will not wait a day. The loop compresses: one press of CHECK-IN is "today". The strip shows the last six days already filled.
- Each "Not now" on a Lamby offer costs the pet one visible notch of mood ("concerned"). Dismissal always works and is logged. It costs the player nothing real. No score is shown in the piece's voice. This follows the "frame never plays" law: all of it is Restorify's feature.
- The pet cannot die. Neglect shows a concerned face only. This avoids a fail state and avoids blame. (Self-blame makes breaks hurt more. [S61])

### 8.5 The guilt dial and the safeguards

Documented effect:
- A pet that asks to be cared for produces attachment and guilt, and children mourned them. [S53][S54][S56]
- The programme uses that on purpose in the Caleb script: "the shame is the mechanism". [E2 draft]

Safeguards:
- The pet never mocks Daniel. It looks hurt. It does not blame him by name.
- No pet death, no gravestone, no ghost. The streak dies, not Lamby.
- Not shown during `felt` or `respite` scenes. Lamby is gone from the Caleb chat.
- ≤5 unsolicited balloons per era. One voice at a time.
- The CORRECT button is the risky one. The Tamagotchi's discipline is for a pet that calls for attention when its needs are met. [S58] The satirical use: pressing it on Lamby teaches a grammar (punish the false call) that the system then applies to Daniel's wanting. If it feels too heavy, show the button greyed with a coach-only label, like the greyed "Decline" in s2_testimony.json.
- No timers. The pet reacts to presses, not to elapsed time.
- Memory only. No storage. No sound.

### 8.6 How it collapses

Satire lives only in the programme's self-presentation, and it must collapse. Three stages, matching the existing script.

Stage 1: the commit-press (S2R.3)
- The flag fires. "412 → 0" on screen. The week strip's pips stamp VOID.
- Lamby moves to the Sad form (the sad animation already planned) and holds.
- Then "I caught it in time." Then the repair offer: "we'll get the days back together." The strip refills with the staff stamp. The visitor sees the days are filled in by the programme. The 412 was partly its own paperwork all along.

Stage 2: the recovery content (S2R.4)
- Lamby offers "Hatch a new Companion": a blank egg wobbles on the LCD. This is the Tamagotchi restart after death. [VERIFY SOURCE for the original behaviour]
- The video breaks. The egg stops mid-wobble.

Stage 3: the collapse (S2R.5)
- The pet does not die. It goes still. Both meters stand full and unchanged, then read "—". The tray dot stops blinking. A line from the system: "Companion process: no needs recorded."
- The truth the visitor learns: Lamby never needed anything. The needs were Daniel's file. The only one who was ever starving was Daniel.
- Existing beat stays: the streak reads "412 days · for nothing". Lamby's window is empty-eyed.
- After this, Lamby is absent. The Caleb thread is felt.

Where the object goes after
- The Close's receipt already promises "a purity streak". The still, dark LCD can be the item left unclaimed there. [close_restart.json]

### 8.7 What it replaces and what it touches

- Replaces the "Make Lamby yours" grid. The RESTRUCTURE proposal already simplifies it to one question (P7-62b). This goes further: no question at all. Raising the pet is the personalisation.
- Touches s2_lamby.json (streakNote, forms), s2_caleb.json (alert, caughtLine, repair line), the ledger (lamby form, dismissals), the Lamby rig (needs the Sad mood).
- Does not touch the Caleb text you wrote. The ⟨S⟩ lines stay exactly as they are.

### 8.8 Decisions for you

1. On-screen LCD or a keychain in the hand (8.2)?
2. Keep CORRECT, show it greyed, or leave it out?
3. Two meters or none? (None is simpler. The week strip alone carries the guilt.)
4. Do you want "11 resets"? It makes the 412 honest, but adds a number.
5. Is a blank egg in Stage 2 too cute?
