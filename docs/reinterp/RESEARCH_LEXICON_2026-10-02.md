# Research for the Lexicon: the TERMS and the REASONS conversion-practice networks use (2026-10-02)

Prepared for Sérgio. Brief (his idea, 2026-10-02): the piece does not yet show the **words** SOGICE networks use, or the **reasons** they give for people being gay, lesbian, bisexual or trans. In play the player collects each word where the system uses it (each era's software can push a cheerful "Word of the Day"); at the Close a retro-encyclopedia icon opens a LEXICON that turns each word over: what it claims, who uses it, what the evidence says.

**How to read this file.**
- Source ids (S01…) are in the table in section 3, with the URL actually fetched, or the local file name. Local files from `/Users/sergiogalvaoroxo/Pc_Simulation/PDF/MD` are cited as `[L: filename]` with a chapter or section where I could give one. Project documents are `[P: …]`.
- Two evidence questions are kept apart on purpose: **USE** (is the word or claim documented as used by the networks?) and **CLAIM** (is what it says true?). Labels: **DOCUMENTED** (shown by a source I read), **CONTESTED** (credible parties disagree, or the evidence is mixed), **NOT SUPPORTED** (the evidence I read does not support the claim). A few entries are marked **NOT AN EMPIRICAL CLAIM** (a moral or political frame).
- Quotes: at most one per source, each under 15 words. No private individuals are named. Published authors are named with their publications; interviewees in the books are not named.
- "Word of the Day" lines are invented, in the seller's cheerful voice, and the satire is only of the seller. Lines for the 2026 clinical-debate terms (entries 19-23) carry a ⚑: ETHICS #7 says the clinical debate is never satirised, so those lines mock the programme's own sales pitch only, and Sérgio should decide whether they ship at all.
- "Close" lines are plain and educational, 30 words or fewer.
- Wayback captures are cited with the capture timestamp that the archive actually served. Where my request was for a nearby date, the served date is the one given.

**Headline findings (read these first).**
1. The "reasons" were a **stable core with a moving surface**. 1990s-2005 ministry pages are confident: unmet needs, a distant father, an enmeshed mother, peer rejection, abuse (S01, S03, S07, S15). By 2012 Evergreen's FAQ says no single factor is established and "biology, environment, and early development all play a role" (S02). The 2010s-2020s versions say "trauma", "attachment", "signal" (`[L: www-josephnicolosi-com-articles-understanding-ssa-as-a-signa.md]`; `[L: www-focusonthefamily-com-get-help-what-do-we-mean-when-we-ta.md]`). The same claim, with the certainty turned down.
2. The **vocabulary was chosen, and known to be chosen**. Evergreen's own style primer says it prefers "same-sex attraction" to avoid "clinical and psychiatric baggage" (S02); a Catholic ministry's FAQ explains in writing why it discourages "gay" and "lesbian" (S05). The audits (GPAHE 2022/2024) show the euphemisms are exactly the search terms that still surface providers (S11, S12).
3. **The 2026 trans-specific terms are mostly not in the 1997-2016 record at all.** "Gender ideology" (a frame the Hate Review treats as an empty signifier), "social contagion"/ROGD, "desistance" and "detransition" are all live disputes. For these the honest label is CONTESTED, and the Cass Review (S17) and the Hate Review (S21) are the sources I would put on screen, with both readings.
4. **Gender "confusion" and "affirmation" were already movement words in the 1990s-2012**, used about gay boys and women (Nicolosi's guide, People Can Change, Evergreen's "Gender-Affirmative Therapy" FAQ). The Lexicon can show that these words were borrowed *before* they were aimed at trans people.
5. **The strongest lesbian-specific primary source is in the project's own Smithsonian folder**: Focus on the Family's *The Roots and Causes of Female Homosexuality* (2001; NMAH.AC.1428 B006 F004), OCR'd in `Boy Erased/New/`. Read in full below.

## 1. THE TERMS

Entries are in this order: the SSA family (1-6), the "cause" words (7-11), the names for the practice (12-16), the practice words (17-18), the 2016-2026 frame words (19-23), the community's own word (24), and two supporting entries (25-26).

### 1. same-sex attraction (SSA) / same-gender attraction (SGA)
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** that being gay is a *feeling* that a person has, separable from who the person is; the person is "a man (or woman) with SSA", not gay or lesbian; the feeling can be reduced.
- **Source and users:** Courage's FAQ (Catholic; captured 2004) explains why it discourages "gay"/"lesbian" and prefers persons "with same-sex attractions" (S05). Evergreen International (LDS-oriented, independent of the Church; founded 1989, dissolved 2014; S27) uses SSA and SGA "synonymously" and its 2012 style primer says it prefers them to avoid "clinical and psychiatric baggage" (S02). People Can Change (2005) and the 1998 Exodus booklets use it (S07; [L: Boy Erased OCR, NMAH B002 F012]). Robinson and Spivey (2015) list "female same-sex attraction (SSA)" among the movement's euphemisms for lesbians (S14). GPAHE 2024: "same-sex attraction" returns "dismal" results on every platform and language, a term "co-opted" by conversion proponents (S12). Brothers Road (a 2026 blog post) uses "struggle with attraction to the same sex" (`[L: brothersroad-org-you-are-not-a-sin-same-e2-80-91sex-attracti.md]`).
- **Evidence:** USE: DOCUMENTED. CLAIM (attraction can be separated from identity and diminished): NOT SUPPORTED; the APA task force found orientation change "unlikely to be successful" with "some risk of harm" (S09). Caution: "same-sex attraction" is also used neutrally by researchers, including the APA report itself (S09).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: ATTRACTION. Just a feeling you *have*, like a cold. Not who you *are*. Feeling lighter already?"
- **Close:** A phrase ministries prefer to "gay" or "lesbian". It frames attraction as a feeling to be reduced, not part of who someone is. Mainstream health bodies reject that framing.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s2_forum.json` ("You are a person who struggles with same-sex attraction"); `data/dialog/s3_recommend.json` (house-style tags, the explicit term greyed).

### 2. unwanted same-sex attraction (USSA / UHA)
- **Era:** 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** that the problem is only the client's *own wish* to reduce attraction; the practice is "voluntary" and so not conversion.
- **Source and users:** the 2018 US trademark text for "Reparative Therapy" itself speaks of "unwanted same-sex attractions" (S10, footnote 11). NARTH's 2006 bulletin and the 2009 *Handbook of Therapy for Unwanted Homosexual Attractions* (Hamilton and Henry) use it (`[L: Handbook_of_therapy_for_unwanted_homosexual_attractions…md]`; S36 for the bulletin note). GPAHE 2022/2024: searches for "unwanted same-sex attraction" returned provider links, including Focus on the Family and conversion providers (S11, S12). A 2026 court filing quoted by LGBTQ Nation describes services for clients "seeking to reduce or eliminate unwanted sexual attractions" (`[L: www-lgbtqnation-com-2026-03-the-right-knows-conversion-thera.md]`; the outcome of that case is not in the file and I do not state it).
- **Evidence:** USE: DOCUMENTED. CLAIM ("voluntary"): CONTESTED at best. The APA task force found the people who seek change tend to hold strongly conservative religious views (S09); the UN Independent Expert documents family, church and community pressure (S08, paras 17-19, 30); a 2025 systematic review found 60% of 6,164 participants were under 18 at first exposure (`[L: Salway_et_al._-_2025_…md]`, abstract).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: UNWANTED. Nobody *made* you want this to stop. You just… noticed that everyone around you does."
- **Close:** "Unwanted" suggests a free choice. Research finds the wish to change usually comes from religious or family pressure, and many people exposed to change efforts were minors.
- **In the piece now:** `data/provotypes/e3_theday.json`, `data/provotypes/origin_intake_e1.json` (the "why you want to overcome" form).

### 3. struggling with / the struggle / "strugglers"
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** same-sex desire is a moral or spiritual battle, like addiction; the person is a "struggler" and the goal is to resist, not to live it.
- **Source and users:** Love in Action (2005 capture) lists "Sojourners", "a support group for sexual/relational strugglers" (S07 → S06 programs page); Evergreen's 2012 pages (S02); Courage ("Those who struggle with same-sex desires", S05); the 1988 Exodus telephone manual has a tab for "Wife of a Homosexual Struggler" (via `[P: SOURCES_LIA_COLLECTION_2026-09-28.md §5]`; not re-read by me). Scholarly accounts: Erzen (2006) writes that members call themselves "strugglers" (`[L: annas-arch-22ecf1e49a40_StraightToJesus.md]`); Wolkomir (2006) (`[L: _Be_not_deceived…md]`). Focus on the Family 2014 still says "homosexual 'strugglers'" (`[L: www-focusonthefamily-com-get-help-what-do-we-mean-when-we-ta.md]`).
- **Evidence:** NOT AN EMPIRICAL CLAIM; it is a moral frame. The APA task force records that some participants found support and "a place to discuss their conflicts" in such groups, and that others were harmed (S09, ch. 5). Both belong on screen.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: STRUGGLE. Struggling means you're *trying*. Good for you! (We'll tell you what you're trying to do.)"
- **Close:** A word that casts being gay as a battle to be fought. The people who use it describe their own desires as a temptation; major health bodies do not.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json` intro ("the ROOT CAUSE of the struggle").

### 4. the lifestyle / "the homosexual lifestyle"
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** being gay is a chosen pattern of behaviour, a way of life one can leave.
- **Source and users:** Exodus's 2003 FAQ (advising on whether befriending a gay person condones their "lifestyle", S03); Love in Action 2005 ("addictive and out of control lifestyles", S06); Evergreen 2012's women's page (S02); People Can Change 2005 contrasts a "celibate lifestyle" with a "promiscuous, self-indulgent" one (S07). The 1998 Exodus booklet by John Smid attributes "totally disgusting lifestyle" to some conservative Christians while arguing its own case (`[L: Boy Erased OCR, NMAH B002 F012]`).
- **Evidence:** USE: DOCUMENTED. CLAIM (orientation is a lifestyle choice): NOT SUPPORTED. The APA task force reports same-sex attractions as "normal and positive variations" of human sexuality (S09). Caution: "lifestyle" is also used by mainstream media; only the claim, not the word, is the network's.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: LIFESTYLE. Like a gym membership, but with a view on eternity. You can cancel any time!"
- **Close:** A word that treats being gay as a habit or a choice. Attraction is not chosen, and health bodies describe it as a normal variation of human sexuality.
- **In the piece now:** none found in `data/` outside dossier URLs.

### 5. ex-gay / former homosexual / "left homosexuality"
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** a person can stop being gay; testimony proves it.
- **Source and users:** Exodus's 2003 tagline, "Freedom from homosexuality through the power of Jesus Christ", and a testimonials page headed "People who have left Homosexuality" (S03, S04); People Can Change 2005 ("our former homosexual condition", S07). The 1990s-2000s ads and magazine covers are documented in `[P: sogice-social-media-layer.md]` (Toscano 2009). Exodus's president announced in 2012 that he no longer believed in the merits of reorientation therapy, and Exodus closed in 2013 (Waidzunas 2015, ch. 4 opening: `[L: The_Straight_Line…md]`). The author of the 2001 study that ex-gay groups cited apologised in May 2012 (same chapter). Survivors use "ex-ex-gay" (ILGA 2020 citing Flentje et al. 2013; S10).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED as orientation change (S09; UN Independent Expert 2020, para 20, S08). ILGA notes that "ex-gay" may mean only that a person stopped identifying as gay while attractions or behaviour continue (S10).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: FORMER. Everything you were before today is *former*. Isn't that nice? Say it again, softly."
- **Close:** "Ex-gay" claims that orientation can be left behind. Research finds change efforts rarely change attraction and often harm. Many people who tried now call themselves ex-ex-gay.

### 6. overcomer / overcoming
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** same-sex attraction is something to be *overcome*, and those who do are "overcomers".
- **Source and users:** Exodus testimonials (2003) sign off "Victorious Overcomer" (S04); Evergreen's 2012 home page lists "People can overcome homosexual behavior and diminish same-sex attractions" among its five truths (S02); Love in Action's application asks for "reasons why you want to overcome" (`[P: SOURCES_LIA_COLLECTION §1]`); Erzen quotes a ministry author on "male overcomers" (`[L: …StraightToJesus.md]`). GPAHE 2024: "overcoming same-sex attraction" returns Courage and Mormon Church pages (S12). CNN (Oct 2025) says providers stress attractions can be "overcome" rather than eliminated (`[L: edition-cnn-com-2025-08-06-us-gay-conversion-therapy-lgbtq-r.md]`).
- **Evidence:** NOT SUPPORTED (S09; S08). ILGA: "ex-gay" language "validates the idea that sexual orientation is actually modifiable" (S10).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: OVERCOMER. Today you overcame a thought. Tomorrow: another thought! Overcoming never stops. Neither do we."
- **Close:** A word that treats attraction as an enemy. The "overcome, don't eliminate" version is a retreat from earlier promises of change, not a finding that it works.

### 7. brokenness / sexual brokenness / relational brokenness
- **Era:** 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** being gay (or bi) is a sign that a person is damaged and needs repair.
- **Source and users:** Desert Stream Ministries (home of Living Waters), 2005 capture: a book for those who want to "understand sexual brokenness" (S06b). LeMaster (2010) quotes Living Waters as for "sexual and relational brokenness" (`[L: Queering_conversion_An_ethnog.md]`, ch. 1). Love in Action 2005: "the relationally broken person" (S06). Erzen (2006): members call themselves "strugglers" dealing with "sexual brokenness" (`[L: …StraightToJesus.md]`). Nicolosi's guide (2017 edition) has a woman "living out her brokenness" (`[L: A_Parent’s_Guide…Nicolosi…md]`, ch. 7). ILGA: "reparative" conveys there is something "broken" about a person (S10). Anderson, Despott and Jones (2023): healing "for their sexual brokenness" is a recurring demand (`[L: Anderson et al. - 2023…md]`, "Conversion Ideology").
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED. The WHO and APA classify no orientation as a disorder; the UN Independent Expert calls the premise "medically false pathologization" (S08, para 20).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: BROKENNESS. Good news! If you're broken, you're *fixable*. (Terms and conditions apply to the fixing.)"
- **Close:** A word that treats sexuality as damage. No orientation is a disorder; the damage described in research comes from stigma and from change efforts themselves.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s3_recommend.json` ("sexual brokenness" among the house-style tags); `data/provotypes/e3_theday.json`.

### 8. root causes / "root issues"
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** attraction is only a *symptom*; underneath is a root problem that can be found and healed, and the attraction will then "resolve".
- **Source and users:** Evergreen 2005: homosexuality "is symptomatic of other problems" (S01; unchanged in 2012, S02). Exodus's 2003 FAQ calls family rifts and abuse "root issues" (S03). People Can Change 2005, "Root Problems": once the pain was healed, "the symptoms of homosexuality took care of themselves" (S07). Exodus's 1998 booklet is titled *Are People Born Gay? Understanding the root of homosexuality* and Focus on the Family's 2001 booklets are *The Roots and Causes of Male / Female Homosexuality* (`[L: Boy Erased OCR, NMAH B002 F012, B006 F004, B006 F005]`). Thorn (2015) tracks "root cause" talk across the movement (`[L: Thorn_Michael_E_2015_PhD_(2).md]`).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED. The APA task force: "no sufficiently scientifically sound evidence" for change, and family-relationship causal theories "have been discredited" (S09, ch. 2 and ch. 6). The piece's own ROOTCAUSE game already turns this into a satire that "collapses".
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: ROOT. Every flower has one. Let's dig yours up and look at it together. Don't worry; it'll hurt a little."
- **Close:** The idea that being gay is a symptom of a hidden hurt. Researchers reviewing the evidence found no such root; theories blaming family patterns have been discredited.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json`; `data/strings/slice.json` (`rootcause-opened`).

### 9. the wound / father wound / mother wound / father hunger
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** a distant, absent or rejecting father (or an overbearing mother) leaves a "wound" that is later sexualised; healing the wound is the cure.
- **Source and users:** "father hunger" is a psychoanalytic phrase Nicolosi cites to Herzog 1982 in *Reparative Therapy of Male Homosexuality* (1991, ch. 6; `[L: Reparative_Therapy_of_Male_Homosexuality_1…md]`). People Can Change 2005 lists "father hunger", "mother enmeshment" and "unhealed wounds" (S07). The "mother wound" is the lesbian version (section 2). Nicolosi's later site frames attraction as "a symbolic defense against the trauma of attachment loss" (`[L: www-josephnicolosi-com-articles-understanding-ssa-as-a-signa.md]`). Erzen (2006) records "father-love" as a recurrent ministry theme (`[L: …StraightToJesus.md]`). GPAHE 2022 quotes the Reparative Therapy site on childhood "trauma" (S11).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED. The APA 2009 history records these patterns (domineering mother, distant father) in the older psychoanalytic literature and concludes the theories "have been discredited" (S09, ch. 2 and ch. 6). Many people do have painful family histories; the evidence does not show that those histories produce orientation.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: WOUND. It's not your fault. It's your *father's*. (Or your mother's. We'll find out together.)"
- **Close:** The claim that a distant father or smothering mother makes a child gay. Studies do not support it; a painful childhood is real for some, but does not decide orientation.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json` ("Possible father-wound root"), `data/provotypes/pillow.json`, `data/dialog/s2_forum.json`.

### 10. emotional dependency (lesbian-specific)
- **Era:** 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** lesbian relationships are really an unhealthy need for nurture and identity ("enmeshment"), a symptom of a failed bond with the mother.
- **Source and users:** Rentzel's *Emotional Dependency* (InterVarsity, 1990) is the standard text (cited in S14); Focus on the Family's 2001 booklet: "emotional dependency is when a woman deposits her identity…" (`[L: Boy Erased OCR, NMAH B006 F004]`, near the end of "Relationship issues"); Evergreen's 2012 women's page describes such relationships as overly dependent and enmeshed (S02). Robinson and Spivey (2015) identify it as a characteristic euphemism for female homosexuality (S14).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED as a cause or a diagnosis of lesbian orientation (S09; S14). USE: DOCUMENTED.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: DEPENDENCY. Loving someone too much is a symptom. Don't worry, we have a worksheet for love."
- **Close:** A term for treating lesbian love as a disorder of "needing too much". Researchers who studied the movement describe it as a euphemism, not a clinical finding.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json` ("EMOTIONAL DEPENDENCY"); 2016 course and group copy per `data/provotypes/e3_theday.json`.

### 11. gender identity disorder → gender confusion; "gender esteem"; "gender deficit"
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** a boy or girl who does not fit gender norms has a gender-identity *deficit* or *confusion*, and this is the origin of later homosexuality (or of trans identity); strengthen "masculine/feminine affirmation" and it resolves.
- **Source and users:** Nicolosi 1991 has a chapter "Male Gender-deficit" and, in the 2017 edition of *A Parent's Guide to Preventing Homosexuality*, speaks of "gender-confused boys" and "masculine affirmation" (chapters 1-2; `[L: A_Parent’s_Guide…md]`). People Can Change 2005: "gender esteem" and a "Never-Never Land of gender confusion" (S07). NARTH's 2004 page "How Should Clinicians Deal With GID In Children?" reports a clinician's review (S10b). The Catholic Medical Association's *Homosexuality and Hope* links "gender identity disorder" and "chronic juvenile unmasculinity" to later attraction (`[L: Homosexuality-and-Hope.md]`, "At-risk, not predestined"). Ashley notes "gender confusion" is how conversion practitioners read trans youth (`[L: Banning Transgender Conversion Practices.md]`, ch. 2 definitions).
- **Evidence:** "Gender confusion" is not a clinical term. DSM-5 (2013) replaced "gender identity disorder" with "gender dysphoria", stating that gender nonconformity is not in itself a mental disorder (S19); ICD-11 moved gender incongruence out of the mental-disorders chapter (S18). APA 2009: gender nonconformity differs from GID, and there is "no research evidence" that childhood gender nonconformity and adult homosexuality are necessarily sequential (S09, ch. 6). **The clinical follow-up literature on gender-variant children is a live dispute, not a satire target: render both captions, unresolved (ETHICS #7).**
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: CONFUSION. Feeling confused? Wonderful. That means we can start by *clarifying* you."
- **Close:** "Gender confusion" is not a medical term. Current diagnostic manuals use "gender dysphoria" or "gender incongruence". The word has been used to read ordinary gender nonconformity as a problem.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json` ("GENDER CONFUSION"); `data/dialog/s3_queue.json` ("possible gender confusion — route to exploratory mentorship").

### 12. reparative therapy
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** same-sex attraction is a "reparative drive": an attempt to repair a lost bond with the father or same-sex peers; therapy can redirect it.
- **Source and users:** created in the early 1980s by Nicolosi (GPAHE, S11); the US trademark registration is recorded as 2018 (ILGA, S10, footnote 11); NARTH was founded 1992 (S06c). Waidzunas (2015) notes the name refers to healing a "reparative drive", not "repairing" gay people, though it is widely read that way (`[L: The_Straight_Line…md]`, chapter notes). ILGA: the term conveys that something is "broken", and should always be written in inverted commas (S10). Nicolosi's *Parent's Guide* is © 2017 (`[L: …Nicolosi…md]`).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED; APA 2009 found efforts "unlikely to be successful" with "some risk of harm" (S09). Several professional associations opposed it (GPAHE 2022 lists APA, NASW, AAP; S11). Proponents dispute the label "conversion therapy" (see entry 13).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: REPARATIVE. Tiny repairs, every week. Like a hotel, but for your insides."
- **Close:** A trademarked name for therapy aimed at reducing same-sex attraction. Mainstream associations oppose it; studies find it does not work and can harm.

### 13. reintegrative therapy
- **Era:** 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** a "trauma treatment" whose effect may be reduced same-sex attraction as a by-product; "not conversion therapy".
- **Source and users:** launched around 2017-2018 by a second-generation provider; GPAHE 2022 traces the changing mission statement across 2018 and 2021 and the provider's July 2021 lawsuit against two researchers who called it SOGIECE (S11). The 2020 IFEG statement (quoted in S11) names it among conversion terms. A practitioner's 2019 conference title called it "Re-integrative (i.e. Reparative) Therapy for men struggling with SSA" (S11). In GPAHE's audits searches for it return providers on Google, Bing and YouTube in several languages (S11, S12).
- **Evidence:** USE: DOCUMENTED. CLAIM ("not SOGICE"): CONTESTED by its proponents; GPAHE and the IFEG say it is a rebranding (S11).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: REINTEGRATIVE. Same cure, bigger word. Please don't call it a cure."
- **Close:** A newer name for therapy linked to reparative therapy. Its providers deny it aims to change orientation; researchers and forensic experts classify it with change efforts.

### 14. SOCE / SOGICE / SOGIECE (sexual orientation and gender identity (and expression) change efforts)
- **Era:** Close (survivor and research term, not a network word)
- **Claims:** an umbrella label for any effort to change or suppress orientation or gender identity.
- **Source and users:** the APA adopted "sexual orientation change efforts" (SOCE) in 2009 (S09; S10). ILGA recommends it as the technically precise term (S10). "SOGICE" is used by survivor networks in Australia ("SOGICE Survivor Statement"; S29) and by researchers (`[L: Anderson et al. - 2023…md]`); "SOGIECE" by ILGA, GPAHE and Salway et al. (2025). Salway et al. (2025): 41 studies in 14 countries; the most common modalities were gender-role enforcement (66%) and prayer (59%); 60% of participants were under 18 at first exposure.
- **Evidence:** DOCUMENTED (definitional).
- **Word of the Day:** none; the networks avoid this word. (⚑ If the Close wants one, "WORD OF THE DAY" belongs to the system, so leave it out.)
- **Close:** Short for efforts to change or suppress sexual orientation, gender identity or expression. Used by researchers and survivors; practitioners usually call it something gentler.

### 15. conversion practices / "conversion therapy" / "gay cure"
- **Era:** Close · 2026
- **Claims (the networks, about this label):** it is a "scare-term" for coercive practices that no longer exist; what they offer is "voluntary counselling".
- **Source and users:** the UN Independent Expert (2020) uses it "as an umbrella term" for efforts premised on the belief that orientation or gender identity "can and should be changed or suppressed" and notes proponents "rebranded and reshaped their communications" under bans (S08, paras 17, 40). ILGA: "conversion" and "therapy" are both misnomers and should appear in inverted commas (S10). GPAHE: rebranding is "calculated and strategic" (S11). Salway et al. 2025: practices "not immediately recognizable as such".
- **Evidence:** DOCUMENTED (definitions). The UN Independent Expert reports that most such practices are not "therapy" at all and that offering them is, in the words of the forensic experts he cites, "deception, false advertising and fraud" (S08, para 21).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: COUNSELLING. We don't do the *other* thing. We just talk. About changing."
- **Close:** An umbrella term for efforts to change or suppress who someone is. Major health bodies reject it. It is banned in a growing number of places and rebrands when banned.

### 16. accountability partner
- **Era:** 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** a trusted peer who checks in, hears confessions of "falling", and reports lapses is the key to staying on track.
- **Source and users:** Evergreen's 2012 "Map of the Way Out": "Hold each other accountable", in group and individual work (S02, map page); also "be accountable to" a woman friend in the women's page (S02). A 2010 study of ex-gay and ex-ex-gay boards found "find an accountability partner" among the most common advice to newcomers (Weiss et al.; S30; figures from `[P: sogice-social-media-layer.md]`, 2003 section; I read the paper's metadata only). Love in Action's workbook lists people who will "hold you accountable" (`[P: SOURCES_LIA_COLLECTION §1]`). Courage's fellowship goal: "no one will have to face the problems of homosexuality alone" (S05).
- **Evidence:** USE: DOCUMENTED. CLAIM (accountability prevents "relapse"): CONTESTED / mixed. The APA task force records that participants reported support and community as benefits, and others reported shame and distress from "relapse" framing (S09, ch. 5). The same structure is also used in non-SOGICE addiction recovery and church life; the network version carries a monitoring function.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: ACCOUNTABILITY. Someone who *loves* you will always want to know where you were."
- **Close:** A peer asked to monitor and report someone's behaviour. Support in itself is not harmful; here it was used to police a person's identity and treat lapses as failure.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s2_forum.json`, `s2_caleb.json`, `s3_maiden.json`, `s4_browser.json`, `data/provotypes/e3_theday.json`.

### 17. freedom / healing / restoration / wholeness
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** same-sex attraction is a captivity or injury; the network offers "freedom", "healing", "restoration", "sexual wholeness".
- **Source and users:** Exodus's tagline "Freedom from homosexuality through the power of Jesus Christ" (S03); Desert Stream's *Pursuing Sexual Wholeness: How Jesus Heals the Homosexual* (S06b); Love in Action's "Restoration Weekend" (S06); Evergreen: therapy alone "often lacks the power" for permanent healing (S02); People Can Change: "the path to healing" (S07); Erzen and Wolkomir on "healing" as the movement's master word (`[L: …StraightToJesus.md; _Be_not_deceived…md]`). 2025-2026: Brothers Road uses "Healing Work" as a site category (`[L: brothersroad…md]`).
- **Evidence:** CLAIM: NOT SUPPORTED. The UN Independent Expert points out that "therapy" means "healing" and that these practices are the contrary (S08, para 20). Anderson et al. (2023) describe the demand to "seek healing" as a core of conversion ideology.
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: FREEDOM. The door is open. (Please use the door we pointed at.)"
- **Close:** Words that present being gay as a captivity or injury. The research on these programmes finds the "freedom" was rarely delivered, and the harm that followed was real.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s2_testimony.json` ("Today he chooses freedom"); `data/dialog/s3_course.json` (a list that ends "walking, wholeness, freedom").

### 18. "affirming" / "affirmation" (borrowed by the other side)
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** the networks use "affirmation" to mean *affirming your masculinity/femininity*, not your identity; since the 2020s, "the affirmation model" names medicine's approach to trans youth, which opponents call hasty.
- **Source and users:** People Can Change 2005 says its members wanted to be affirmed as men, not as gay (S07). Nicolosi's guide: "masculine affirmation" (`[L: …Nicolosi…md]`, ch. 2). Evergreen's FAQ (2012) has an entry "What is Gender-Affirmative Therapy?", defined as helping individuals "fully develop their masculine or feminine gender identity" (S02, faq page). In the APA's own 2009 sense, "affirmative" therapy means acceptance and support without a specified identity outcome (S09, abstract). SPLC (2026, an advocacy organisation) reports that groups in a network of "gender-critical" clinicians call the affirming model "inadequate" and promote "exploratory" alternatives (`[L: www.splcenter.org_resources_reports_defining-pseudoscience-network_….md]`, "Research & Practice Groups").
- **Evidence:** DOCUMENTED (the word is borrowed). Whether medical gender-affirming care for minors is well supported is **CONTESTED**: the Cass Review (2024) rated the evidence for several medical interventions low-certainty while rejecting simple explanations from either side (S17, ch. 8 and ch. 15). The piece should render both readings (ETHICS #7).
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** "WORD OF THE DAY: AFFIRMATION. We affirm *everything* about you! (Except the part you came in with.)"
- **Close:** "Affirming" has two meanings. Therapists use it for acceptance without a set outcome. Change programmes used it for affirming "masculinity" or "femininity" as defined by them.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s4_offers.json`, `s3_queue.json`, `data/provotypes/e3_theday.json`, `e4_offers.json`.

### 19. gender ideology
- **Era:** 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** that "gender" teaching, trans rights and sex education form one coordinated ideology that denies male and female, harms children and is imposed from outside the nation.
- **Source and users:** the Catholic Congregation for Catholic Education's *Male and Female He Created Them* (2 Feb 2019) uses "the ideology of gender" for a set of positions it criticises while distinguishing "the whole field of research on gender" (S20). The Hate Review (Forbidden Colours, Sept 2026) describes scholarship that treats it "as an empty signifier" and describes it as a "foreign imposition" narrative documented in 36 countries (in Austria, for example, the frame emerges from around 2012) and also bound up with "protect the children" and "biological sex as the only reality" narratives (S21, Introduction; "Anti-gender Europe: a continent-wide evolution"). The RESIST Project (2024): its meaning "is adaptable, and it changes by context" (`[L: Anti-Gender Tactics in Europe. The RESIST Project Research Findings.md]`, section "What 'gender ideology' means").
- **Evidence:** NOT AN EMPIRICAL CLAIM. It is a mobilising frame; the sources I read (S21, RESIST) treat it as such. The Vatican document shows what proponents mean by it.
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** "WORD OF THE DAY: IDEOLOGY. Everyone else has one. We just have *the truth*."
- **Close:** A label used since the 2010s for gender studies, trans rights and sex education as one hidden agenda. Researchers describe it as a political frame, not a scientific claim.

### 20. social contagion / rapid-onset gender dysphoria (ROGD)
- **Era:** 2026 (precursor, "contagion and recruitment", present in earlier decades per S21)
- **Claims:** that some young people become trans because friends or the internet spread the idea.
- **Source and users:** the term ROGD comes from a 2018 survey study of parent reports (Littman, *PLOS ONE*), recruited from three websites; the 2019 correction states it is hypothesis-generating and "not a formal mental health diagnosis" (S15). The Cass Review (April 2024): peer and socio-cultural influence is "potentially the most contested explanation", the term "social contagion" causes "particular distress to some in the trans community", and "simplistic explanations of either kind" do not serve young people (S17, paras 8.24-8.25). The Hate Review lists "homosexuality as illness … contagion and recruitment" as a narrative "present throughout" the record, and documents "social contagion" framing in a 2024 French Senate report and a Belgian group (S21). SPLC (2026, advocacy) argues the framing is used to portray trans youth as "recruited" (`[L: www.splcenter.org_resources_reports_defining-pseudoscience-network_….md]`, "Narrative Manipulation Groups").
- **Evidence:** CONTESTED. ROGD is not a recognised diagnosis (S15). Professional bodies have issued statements against it: WPATH (2018), CAAPS (2021) and AusPATH (2019) are cited in Ashley (`[L: Banning Transgender Conversion Practices.md]`) and WPATH SOC-8 (2022) per a secondary summary (S25). Cass says social influence is plausible but contested and not simple (S17).
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** "WORD OF THE DAY: ENVIRONMENT. Protect yours! (We've already tidied it for you.)"
- **Close:** The claim that gender identity spreads among friends or online. It began as parents' reports; no validated clinical entity exists. Reviews call the topic contested and caution against simple explanations.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s4_ball.json` ("SOCIAL CONTAGION · HIGH · restoring your environment…").

### 21. desistance
- **Era:** 2026
- **Claims:** most gender-variant children "desist" and grow up cisgender, so early support is risky.
- **Source and users:** follow-up studies from a Toronto clinic and a Dutch clinic are the base; a 2018 critique by Temple Newhook and colleagues lists 12 methodological, theoretical, ethical and interpretive concerns (S16b); Zucker's 2018 reply defends the research and objects to the critique's framing (S16). The Cass Review, in its recommendations, states that children who present at a young age "are most likely to desist before puberty" (S17, rec. 144), and notes the Review uses "detransition" for people who medically transitioned and "desistance" for those who revert after a period of trans identification (para 15.44).
- **Evidence:** CONTESTED. Both the critique and the reply are published; so is the Review's use. SPLC calls it "the debunked desistance myth" (`[L: www.splcenter.org_resources_reports_defining-pseudoscience-network_….md]`, note 77); I report it as a dispute, not as settled in either direction.
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** "WORD OF THE DAY: PHASE. Children grow out of things. You did. We *saw* you grow."
- **Close:** "Desistance" means a child's gender-variant feelings fading. Studies from the 1980s onward are disputed over how they counted children and what the numbers mean for care today.

### 22. detransition (handle with care: both things are true)
- **Era:** 2026
- **Claims:** (a) *Detransition happens, for varied reasons, and the people who do it deserve care.* (b) *Networks collect detransition stories to argue against all trans care.*
- **Source and users:** a 2021 survey of 100 detransitioners (Littman, *Archives of Sexual Behavior*): reasons included being more comfortable as their birth sex (60%), medical concerns (49%), a new view that their dysphoria had another cause such as trauma (38%), discrimination (23%) and homophobia or difficulty accepting oneself as lesbian, gay or bisexual (23%); 55% felt they had not been adequately evaluated (S22). A 2021 secondary analysis of the US Transgender Survey (Turban et al., *LGBT Health*): of those who had pursued gender affirmation, 13.1% reported detransition, and 82.5% of those cited external pressure such as family or stigma (S22). The Cass Review includes a section on detransition and records young people saying it had not felt "like an option" to discuss (S17, ch. 15). SPLC (2026) alleges the network amplifies "one-sided" detransition stories and that research shows most detransition follows stigma (`[L: www.splcenter.org_resources_reports_defining-pseudoscience-network_….md]`, note 88, advocacy reading); the Hate Review notes a Portuguese group affiliated with Genspect works "as a resource for detransitioners and parents" (S21, Portugal).
- **Evidence:** DOCUMENTED that detransition exists and has several causes. The proportion, and how the networks use the stories, are CONTESTED.
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** none recommended. If the programme has a line it must be about *the programme*, never the people: "WORD OF THE DAY: WELCOME BACK. We kept your seat warm."
- **Close:** Some people stop or reverse a gender transition, for many reasons, including regret, stigma, family pressure and changed understanding. Both their care and the way campaigns use their stories matter.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s4_offers.json` (the "detransition rail"), `data/strings/close_network.json` ("detransition cohort study (Fenway)").

### 23. "just exploring" / exploratory therapy / SAFE-T
- **Era:** 2016 · 2026
- **Claims:** therapy should "explore" gender (or attraction) without commitment, and a pause is neutral.
- **Source and users:** "sexual attraction fluidity exploration in therapy (SAFE-T)" is listed by GPAHE among the rebrands (S11). The Gender Exploratory Therapy Association (formed 2021 by clinicians associated with the Society for Evidence-Based Gender Medicine and Genspect, later renamed Therapy First) advocates "gender exploratory therapy" (S24 secondary; S23). Its supporters deny it is conversion therapy; critics, including a legal director quoted by Slate in 2023 and ILGA (2020) on "gender critical therapy", say it is conversion therapy "by another name" (S23; S10). A 2018 Reintegrative Therapy page spoke of "the sexual-fluidity journey" (S11).
- **Evidence:** CONTESTED. The Slate piece is critical journalism and Wikipedia is secondary; neither is a trial of the therapy. I did not find a controlled study of either approach in what I read. The exact phrase "just exploring" is **not found** as a documented network term; it is the piece's phrase for the pitch.
- **Word of the Day (⚑):** "WORD OF THE DAY: EXPLORING. No pressure. No conclusions. Only a gentle, endless *not yet*."
- **Close:** "Exploratory" therapy says it only helps people think. Supporters deny it is conversion therapy; critics say it steers people away from transition. The question is disputed.
- **In the piece now:** `data/dialog/s3_queue.json` ("route to exploratory mentorship").

### 24. chosen family (the community's own word)
- **Era:** 2026 (Close contrast); also 2016
- **Claims:** family can be made by choice and mutual care, not only by blood; for people rejected by relatives it can be a source of safety.
- **Source and users:** the APA's *The Case Against Conversion "Therapy"* (Haldeman, ed., 2022) treats constructing a chosen family as "a healthy choice" and a part of therapy for clients whose family of origin does not accept them (`[L: The_Case_Against_Conversion_Therapy.md]`, guidelines section). Wikipedia summarises research that chosen family can promote resilience (S26). In the piece, ball-house kinship is described in `data/provotypes/e4_ball.json`. An origin date for the phrase: **not found** in what I fetched.
- **Contrast:** the networks also speak of "family": Love in Action's "Family & Friends Support Group" and "family companion" programmes (S06; `[P: data/strings/close_network.json]`), where family is a monitor.
- **Evidence:** DOCUMENTED as a clinical and community practice (APA 2022); the resilience claim rests on a secondary source here.
- **Word of the Day:** none; this is the community's word, not the seller's. Do not render a cheerful version of it.
- **Close:** Family made by choice and care, not blood. For people rejected by their families, it can be a source of safety and support.

### 25. born that way (as the networks use it, to rebut)
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003 · 2016
- **Claims:** "science has not shown anyone is born gay"; the "gay gene" is a myth; therefore change is possible.
- **Source and users:** Evergreen 2003: "Is homosexuality genetic?" with a list of study limits (S03); Evergreen 2012 "Myths: People are born gay. It's all genetic." and, on its women's page, calls the idea "Satan's propaganda" (S02). NARTH's page headed "'Born that way' theory" (S06c). The movement's later sites soften to "a complex interaction of personality, biological inheritance, and developmental experiences" (`[L: Same-sex-Attractions.md]`, "What Causes Same-sex Attraction?").
- **Evidence:** MIXED. The first half is partly right: the APA website, as quoted on `[L: Same-sex-Attractions.md]`, says no findings permit scientists to conclude that any particular factor determines orientation; the second half is a non-sequitur. Not knowing the cause does not show that change efforts work, and the APA 2009 task force found they do not (S09).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: GENE. Nobody's found one! (That settles everything. Don't read the rest.)"
- **Close:** Scientists do not know exactly why orientation varies, and no single cause has been found. That does not show it can be changed; research finds attempts do not work.

### 26. "homoemotional needs" / "legitimate needs" (the unmet-needs theory)
- **Era:** 1997 · 2003
- **Claims:** same-sex attraction is a *non-erotic* need for male (or female) love that has been "sexualised"; meeting the need "legitimately" removes the attraction.
- **Source and users:** Evergreen 2005 (the needs are called "homoemotional", S01); samesexattraction.org ("attempts to fill unmet emotional and social needs", `[L: Same-sex-Attractions.md]`); Moberly (1983) is the source cited by Evergreen's site and by Nicolosi (S09 cites Moberly 1983 and Nicolosi 1991 as theorists of "altering gender-role behaviors").
- **Evidence:** NOT SUPPORTED (S09; see entry 8).
- **Word of the Day:** "WORD OF THE DAY: NEEDS. You have needs! Wonderful. We'll tell you which ones are *legitimate*."
- **Close:** The idea that same-sex attraction is really an unmet need for love in disguise. It is a theory from the 1980s; researchers have not found it to be true.
- **In the piece now:** `data/strings/rootcause.json` ("legitimate needs", "unmet needs" among the therapy words).

## 4. PCMag: "7 Retro Religious Video Games Worth Exploring"

**What it is.** A short slideshow article by Benj Edwards, dated 19 October 2017, fetched via a Wayback capture of 21 August 2025 (S31). The author says he is not religious, sees a gap in games culture, and treats the seven games as a cultural survey rather than a religious analysis. All seven are **sincere, believer-targeted Christian games**. None is a game about conversion practices, and the article says nothing about them; do not cite it as evidence about SOGICE games. It is only a source for *how the genre looked and played*.

**The seven, in my words.**
1. A 1994 DOS point-and-click educational adventure with a Christian superhero who fights a cybernetic enemy with a "sword of the Spirit" and "shield of faith"; he grows stronger by finding Bible tracts lost around the city. Lush VGA graphics.
2. A 1992 NES overhead action-adventure in the style of a Zelda game, turning the "Armor of God" into literal weapons against "the army of Satan", including throwable "Vials of the Wrath of God".
3. A 1983 Atari 2600 cartridge about the crossing of the Red Sea: extremely rare (under 100 made), unknown until it appeared online in 2007, later sold at auction for over ten thousand dollars.
4. A 1991 NES (also Genesis and DOS) set of three platform games rooted in Old Testament stories; the Noah's Ark game has the player picking up animals and tossing them into the ark, in the manner of Super Mario Bros. 2.
5. A 1992 DOS adventure about David in chapters, with educational quizzes at the end of each.
6. A 1993 Genesis (also NES, Game Boy, DOS) puzzle game about Moses crossing a field of dirt and rocks, similar to Boulder Dash, with built-in educational quizzes.
7. A 1994 SNES game (the only unlicensed SNES release in North America per the article), built on the Wolfenstein 3D engine: a "biblical first-person shooter" where Noah puts animals to sleep by shooting food from a slingshot.

**Patterns the article shows.**
- **Genres:** all borrowed from mainstream hits (Zelda, Mario 2, Boulder Dash, Wolfenstein 3D); *reskins of proven mechanics*, not new designs.
- **Mechanics:** scripture turned **literal** (armour, swords, vials); **collectible tracts** as power-ups; **quizzes at chapter ends** as the "educational" layer; **gentle non-lethal "combat"** (feeding animals to sleep).
- **Tone:** earnest, educational, no irony; the games are sold as a safe, wholesome alternative.
- **Hardware and look:** the 8-bit NES and Atari 2600 for the early ones; VGA DOS for the early-90s PC titles; first-person 3D on a borrowed engine for the SNES; a **mix of unlicensed cartridges** and PC shareware-style releases. Context: the article notes Nintendo of America had a policy against religious iconography in NES games in the 1980s, which is why the publisher took the unlicensed route.

**What the piece's games could borrow (no names or logos copied; our law is that the programme's game cannot be won and the queer side always wins).**
1. **Literalised metaphors.** The programme's games could make the programme's own word an object: ROOTCAUSE already does this (a root to be dug), and 2003's REACH, 2016's FloppySheep and 2026's TIDY could each be one sentence of the programme's vocabulary made physical (REACH: a hand; FloppySheep: a flock; TIDY: a room being put in order). The retro games' "armour of God" equivalent is a safe model: the wholesome literal metaphor, played straight.
2. **A collectible that "powers you up" and a quiz that cannot be passed.** The article's tracts and end-of-chapter quizzes suggest a loop: gather the programme's "testimony tokens" for strength, then answer a quiz at the end where only the programme's answer scores, and where the honest answer scores as a "relapse", so the game cannot be won. The queer side wins by the quiz breaking.
3. **Reskinned mainstream mechanics.** Give each game a recognisable parent: ROOTCAUSE is Dig Dug; a Boulder-Dash-style grid for a "clearing" game; a Mario-2-style pick-up-and-toss for something the programme calls "moving things along". The programme's games are visibly cheap cousins, which suits the satire of the seller.
4. **Hardware by era.** 1997 DOS/VGA, 2003 early-2000s Flash/Windows shareware (the article's era table stops at 1994, so this is an extrapolation), 2016 mobile, 2026 platform. For a Game Boy look (4 greens) the article does not help; the PCMag games are NES, SNES, Atari 2600, Genesis and DOS.
5. **Rarity and ephemera.** The 1983 cartridge's story (under 100 made, unknown until 2007) is a model for how a ministry's own game would survive: as a rare item found on a disk, which the piece already stages with ROOTCAUSE on the companion disk.
6. **Tone:** play it as earnest and educational, with the quiz as the programme's sincere heart. The comedy comes from the player's honest answers, never from the people the programme addresses.

**Cautions for this section.** The games in the article were made by believers for believers and are not satirical; do not borrow their *content*, only their *form*. Nothing here is evidence about what real conversion programmes published as games.

## 5. CAUTIONS

1. **Contested items (render both readings, per ETHICS #7).** Entries 18-23 (affirmation, ROGD/social contagion, desistance, detransition, exploratory therapy) are live scientific and clinical disputes. The Cass Review says simple explanations "of either kind" fail; the Hate Review treats "gender ideology" as a frame; WPATH, CAAPS and AusPATH have statements against ROGD; SPLC (2026) is an advocacy organisation with a strong position. The Lexicon should show who says what, with dates, and **not** adjudicate. The Word-of-the-Day lines for these entries are flagged ⚑ for Sérgio's ethics call.
2. **Detransition (entry 22).** Real people detransition, for many reasons, and some deeply regret transition; networks also harvest their words to argue against all trans care. Both are documented. Never put detransitioners' own words on screen (ETHICS #2); never use the Word-of-the-Day to joke at them.
3. **Terms used by non-SOGICE actors too.** "Same-sex attraction" appears in the APA report itself; "lifestyle" is common in journalism; "healing" and "freedom" are ordinary religious words; "accountability partner" is standard in addiction recovery and in many churches; "exploratory" is an ordinary psychotherapy word. The claim, not the word, is the network's. The Close should say so, or the Lexicon will read as guilt by vocabulary.
4. **Evidence is mixed in places.** The APA 2009 report records that some participants in change efforts reported benefits (a place to talk, community, ways to live with their faith) and that these were distinct from claims of orientation change; it also found harm. The APA also acknowledges that the population that seeks change tends to hold strongly conservative religious views. Do not flatten this into "everyone was harmed in the same way".
5. **The "born that way" half-truth (entry 25).** The networks are partly correct that no single cause of orientation has been established; the leap to "so change works" is what fails. A Close line that says "science has proven people are born gay" would be wrong and would hand them the rebuttal.
