## ⚑ CULTURAL INFLUENCE — credit owed that no licence covers (added S79, 2026-08-13)

*A CC-BY table cannot hold this and must not be stretched to. Era 4's closing
scene (TRANSCENDANCE) is an outsider's homage to a living culture that belongs
to specific people, and the provenance pass
(`docs/research/BALLROOM_PROVENANCE_2026-08-06.md` §5) records what its
practitioners and scholars ask of outside engagement: **name the originators and
the lineage, not "the culture" in the abstract** — crediting it generally is the
extraction pattern restated politely. The block below is generated into
`data/strings/attributions.json` as `influences` and rendered in the game menu's
Credits view, beneath the asset table. Its long form, with sources, is the
Dossier card at `data/provotypes/e4_ball.json`.*

> **INFLUENCE — ballroom / house culture.** The closing scene of this piece
> draws structural and visual influence from ballroom culture: the house-and-ball
> system built from the 1970s onward in New York, principally by Black and Latino
> LGBTQ+ people — centrally Black and Latina trans women and gender-nonconforming
> people — as a space of chosen family, competition and self-determined judgement,
> founded in its modern form by Crystal and Lottie LaBeija's House of LaBeija
> (1972), after Crystal LaBeija objected on camera to racist judging at the 1967
> Miss All-America Camp Beauty Pageant. Its categories, houses and vocabulary
> remain the living culture of a specific community and not a generic aesthetic.
> This project invents its houses, categories, names and announcements, depicts no
> real ball, house or person, and borrows no vernacular, in recognition that
> ballroom's language and forms have too often been taken into mainstream culture
> without credit or benefit to their originators. For further reading, from within
> the culture and about it: Ricky Tucker, *And the Category Is...* (2022); Marlon
> M. Bailey, *Butch Queens Up in Pumps* (2013); and the Peabody Ballroom
> Experience at Johns Hopkins, co-authored with Baltimore ballroom leaders.
