## A. Nothing in 2016 waits on a clock that can stop

1. **The removal and the forwards run in the platform's own time once they begin**: an open job, the phone game,
   a minimised window, a held set: none of them pauses them (drop `!jobOpen` / `!floppy.open` for the `takeover` and
   `after` stages, and anything else that can freeze them). The system does not wait for her.
2. **The piece takes her there** (his October rule): when the review begins, an open job page goes back to the board
   and the Handset Window shows the group. A finished tile pressed while it runs opens nothing that hides it.
3. **The clock she sees: the real time of day.** Our reading of "the clock could be the real time": the time on
   Vera's phone and taskbar is the visitor's own time of day (the date stays Tuesday 6 December 2016), and nothing in
   the story waits on that clock. Message times are the times they land. If a beat today waits for the shown clock to
   reach a minute, make it wait for her press or for the beat before it instead. *(His edit wins if he meant
   something else; the orchestrator is asking him.)*
4. **Act II → Act III on her presses, never by itself** (HEADSET_RUN answer f): the memory's Close → **a Notes
   notification she taps** → **the Notes as their own window** in the phone (not the column switching screens under
   her), the line types → Nuala's message on the lock screen → **Bea's group as a notification she opens**. Each step
   waits for a press or the step before; none fires on a timer while she is elsewhere.
5. **The audit.** List every clock left in 2016 that moves the story (CADENCE_STUDY §3a is the map; it is from
   October, check what moved since). For each: KEEP (the fiction's own time: a boot, a song, a typing line, the

   Put the table in the BUILD_LOG line's doc.
