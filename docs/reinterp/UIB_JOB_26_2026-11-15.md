## Part A (S391): the v4 tapes (Opus; video only)

### A1. Every tape has four movements

1. **The label** (the piece's voice; 6–10 s; silent).
   - It comes before the tape's first frame, in the record's style (the RECORD palette, `src/desktop/theme/witness.ts`;
     the Fixedsys Excelsior face).
   - It says three things:
     - what this is: made from real programmes' teaching, with the years;
     - that every reason in it was taught to real people;
     - that you can stop it at any time.
   - Facts only, checked. Drafts are in the plan §3; improve them.
2. **The tape, straight: the bridge.**
   - The programme in its own grammar: earnest where it teaches, over the top where it sells.
   - **No pause cards. No "THE SAME SOURCE SAYS". The 2009 APA report appears nowhere.**
   - The satire lives only inside the programme's self-presentation, and it collapses.
3. **The margin: extra information that never pauses the tape. Make it as data, not burned into the room's video.**
   - Job 25 B measured burned-in text from the seat: 0.4–0.8° in the headset and 3–6 px on a phone, so it cannot be
     read. Part B therefore draws the margin live, at a readable size.
   - Deliver each tape's margin as `margin.json` in its output folder. Each line has: start s, end s, `kind`
     (`TAUGHT_IN` or `WHAT_IS_KNOWN`), the text (at most 90 characters), and its source with the page or URL.
   - **TAUGHT IN** says where this exact teaching came from:
     - the kind of document, the year, and the organisation where the knowledge base documents it;
     - it is education, not a rebuttal;
     - example: "TAUGHT IN: a ministry's handbook, 1995 — 'Our mind works like a computer.'"
   - **WHAT IS KNOWN** is one uncontested, dated fact, for example a classification change, a professional body's
     public statement, or what became of the programme itself. Use the research doc (§4) and its sources.
     - Never a single disputed study, and never the 2009 APA report.
   - At most one line on screen at a time. Each holds at least 6 s and stays while its lesson plays.
4. **The break: the hardness.** After the collapse, the satire stops.
   - The tape's colour, music and grammar drain away, the way an era's update fails in the piece.
   - Then, in plain words, as silent large type with a few words per frame, say what this teaching did and asked of
     the people it was taught to. Ground every sentence in a documented source:
     - the classes, the journals, the self-surveillance;
     - the blame placed on them when it did not work;
     - the music, friends and places they were told to lose;
     - the families turned into a cause.
   - Then the sources roll.
   - **No testimony, no names, no death or suicide figures.** It is quiet, and it can be stopped. In-video text here is
     at least 1/12 of the picture height, so it reads in the headset (the picture is about 13° tall).
   - Where the programme's own words admit the hardness, use them. 1997 has the handbook's "The more we fight against
     homosexual thoughts, the more intense they become" and "the most difficult task of your entire life" (PDF p. 141).

**"End of Part 1"** (option A, for Part B):
- Each tape has a clear end of its first part, after the label, the title, lesson 1 and the first commercial, at about
