## 1. How a visitor meets things: the grammar the piece already has

There are four ways in, and every new element must use one of them. **No beat may depend on looking somewhere** (his
2026-10-10 law): if the story needs the visitor to see something, the piece takes them there.

1. **The screen (the one UI surface).** Windows, icons and the taskbar on Daniel's monitor. You press with a click
   (browser), a tap (phone), or a pinch, trigger or fingertip (headset). Everything the story *needs* happens here.
2. **A room object that opens a screen beat.** The racket opens Release Work. The disk opens Un-Walk (1997 only, since
   S271). The object is a second door to something that is also on the screen. It is never the only door.
3. **A handheld you pick up.** The Game Boy (1997), the flip phone (2003), Vera's phone (2016), the visor (2026).
   - Browser and phone: a press brings it up to the view, and a press puts it down.
   - Headset: you grab it and it is in your hand (VR-02, VR-03).
4. **Things that run by themselves.** The boot text, the update, a side-message. They need no press and no look.

Who points: Lamby (operable scenes only, ≤5 balloons an era), the Start map's next step, and the era's "Today". **Felt
things are never pointed at by Lamby.** They are found, or the story arrives at them.

## 2. The years between: a folder, and one document in his file (idea 1, reworked)

**The problem.** 1997 ends the night before New Morning. 2003 begins six years later. The visitor never sees the
fourteen days or the years after (the survey: "happened rather suddenly").

**The design: two things on the 2003 desktop, one Daniel's and one the system's.**

### 2a. Daniel's folder: "1997" (felt)

- **What it is.** A folder icon on the 2003 desktop named **"1997"**, handwritten-style.
- **What's inside.** About five files, opened in a plain 2003 picture viewer:
  1. **ROLL_01: a scanned disposable-camera roll** (Claude §6.6). Twenty-six frames are what a programme lets you
     photograph: a group on steps, a chapel, a dining hall, a lake. They show places and backs only, never a face.
     **Frame 27** is dark: a door ajar, corridor light across two beds, no people. It is the find.
  2. **The New Morning certificate**, scanned, front and back. The front has staff signatures. The back has
     **Caleb's own line, signed the way people signed yearbooks**: a short sentence, "Room 3, Bed B", and his name
     (GPT §6.7, his "awww", 31 Oct). It is the only personal handwriting on it, and it replaces the pencilled "C.".
  3. **A cassette J-card**: a mixtape's track list in someone else's handwriting (GPT: "one cassette across six
     years"). The songs are invented titles, and the last one is "last night". It plants the song Caleb sends in 2003.
  4. **An unsent postcard** to "C.", addressed and stamped, never posted (GPT). Short, plain, and bare; it never
     explains.
  5. **A school photo of the room in 1999**: the same desk, two years on, with the racket gone. Time passing in the
     house itself.
- **Register: felt.** No Lamby, no satire, nothing scored, no witness line when it is opened. The system does not
  watch this folder.
- **How you reach it.** The icon is there from the start of 2003. In the room, the **shoebox on the shelf** opens the
  same folder (door type 2). In the headset you can also lift the shoebox (type 3). Nothing points to it. It is a
  find, as he wants.
- **Laws.**
  - No people's faces, and no likeness of anyone.
  - No programme interior. Frame 27 is a door and a corridor, not a recreated room. It is drawn as the rule (doors
    open), not as the place.
  - Nothing sexual.

### 2b. The system's sheet: in "Your file (read only)" (operable)

"Your file" already exists in 2003, and Restorify holds it. A new page inside it: **"New Morning: conduct record, 14
days"** (Claude §6.2):

- one row a day, in the system's flat voice: safekeeping, searches, inventories filed, a call home;
- one row reads "Room 3: speech after lights-out. Noted.";
- the last row is **"Last night: no entry."**;
- then "Completed. Certificate issued."

It is the same fourteen days seen by the institution. The visitor who finds both reads the gap between them. The blank
night is the respite: never shown, never revealed as anything. The sheet's wording keeps it the staff's observation,
never Daniel informing.

### 2c. Is the card seen? (his question, 31 Oct)

As designed, the unsent postcard is a **find**: the visitor sees it only by opening the "1997" folder or the shoebox.
He likes it, so here are three ways to make sure more visitors meet it, without breaking the law that no beat may
depend on looking. Pick one:

- **(a) The folder opens itself once.** On the first quiet moment of 2003 (felt, no Lamby), the folder window
  opens on the postcard alone and can be closed. *Sure, but it takes the find away.*
- **(b) Caleb's thread leads to it.** When the song arrives, the folder's icon gains a small pip, as an unread item
  does, and the card is first in the folder. *Gentle; it uses the story's own moment.* **Recommended.**
- **(c) The card is on the desk too.** A small physical postcard leans against the monitor in 2003. In the browser a
  press brings it up, and in the headset you can pick it up. *The most visible, and it is in the room in every
  version.*

## 6. The pocket pet (idea 5: yes)

**The object.** A small egg-shaped keychain pet, an invented mark: **"Restorify Companion"**, with Lamby on its LCD. It
sits on the 2003 desk beside the flip phone. It is 2003's handheld, as the Game Boy is 1997's. That makes the line of
devices this:

> desk → pocket → hand → face

That line is the article's argument about interfaces moving onto the body, and the virtual pet of 1996–2004 is one of
its most recognisable steps.

**How you use it:**

- Browser and phone: press it to bring it up. Headset: pick it up.
- It has three buttons, as the originals did. In the programme's words: **WORD** (feed), **FELLOWSHIP** (play) and
  **CONFESS** (clean).
- **CORRECT** (discipline) is drawn greyed with "coach only", like the greyed Decline on the release.
- The daily check-in stays in Restorify. A check-in "feeds" the pet. Lamby's mood on the LCD is the streak's mood.
- "Personalise your guide" is **gone**. Lamby's form grows from your check-ins: Steady and Grateful grow Cheer,
  Struggling grows Clinical, Tired grows Sterile. You never choose it, and the programme can say it is "because of
  you". That is the Tamagotchi rule: care decides the form.
- GPT's turn: Lamby looks **sad even after a completed check-in**, if the answer was "Struggling". Care means
  compliance.
- The week strip under the pet shows the first pips stamped differently: staff filled them during the "supervised
  period". Part of the streak was never his.

**How it collapses:**

1. At Caleb's commit-press, the streak drops 412 → 0 and the pips stamp VOID.
2. Lamby offers to "get the days back together", and the staff stamp refills them. The visitor sees the streak is the
   programme's paperwork.
3. At the intervention's collapse, the pet does not die. It goes still. The tray dot stops, and one line reads:
   **"Companion process: no needs recorded."**
4. Lamby never needed anything. The needs were Daniel's file.
5. The still LCD is the unclaimed item on the Close's receipt.

**Safeguards (the laws):**

- It never mocks Daniel, never dies, has no timers and makes no sound (a blinking dot does the beep's job).
- It is never present in felt or respite scenes. It is absent from Caleb's thread.

**Grounding:** virtual-pet care lists, guilt and mourning (Claude §5.1, S53–S58); streak repair as binding (§5.2,
Silverman & Barasch 2023). In the research doc only, the Tamagotchi is named as the reference. The piece's object is
invented.
