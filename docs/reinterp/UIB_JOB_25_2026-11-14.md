### A1. The counter-voice: "the record answers"

His ask is a clear contradicting element in each tape, so a viewer understands the theories are not true. Today each
tape has one collapse at its end, and its smaller cracks are too quiet. Add one device, the same in all three tapes:

1. **At each lesson's central claim, the tape holds,** in its own player's way: the VHS pause bars and jitter in 1997,
   the platform's pause overlay in 2016, a hard freeze in 2026.
2. **One plain line appears in the piece's own record style.** This is not the tape's style. Use the same face, layout
   and placement in all three tapes, sharp and undecorated, so a viewer learns it once and knows it is the piece
   speaking, not the programme. It has one of two heads:
   - **THE SAME SOURCE SAYS:** the programme's own hedge, quoted short, with document and page. The cracks are already
     listed in §1.6: "open to speculation" (Steps Out pp. 284–285), "real or only perceived" (the handbook's triangle),
     the handbook's own unsourced claims. For 2026: "No one is certain what causes gender confusion" (Building Blocks,
     p. 1).
   - **WHAT WAS FOUND:** the evidence, quoted short, with source and page, taken from the CLAIM side of the matching
     Lexicon row (for example APA 2009 p. 73 on family-pattern theories, "discredited").

   Under the head goes **the Lexicon's status word** for that claim: NOT SUPPORTED, CONTESTED or NOT AN EMPIRICAL
   CLAIM. The tape and the Close then say the same thing, and the visitor meets the stamp first on the tape and again in
   the Lexicon.
3. **Silent.** No voice, no sting, no Lamby (Lamby has no voice, and Lamby is the programme's). Each card holds 3–4 s,
   then the tape resumes. At most one per lesson. Use whichever head is stronger for that lesson.
4. **The collapse stays last and strongest.** With the cards before it, the viewer can recognise it as the same pattern:
   the programme contradicting itself.
5. **Words:**
   - every card's line is Claude's draft for his eye, with its source in the script table;
   - never use the Lexicon's `close` lines (his wording) or the dossier's;
   - never change a Lexicon row's status to fit a card.
6. **2026 and the clinical debate.** Where a claim touches the clinical debate, the card shows CONTESTED and both
   readings in one line, with no verdict. Never "false", and never satirised. "Social contagion" is the Lexicon's
   CONTESTED row (parents' reports only; the paper's 2019 correction, "not a formal mental health diagnosis"). Fear of
   medical transition appears only as the anti-gender campaign's tactic (his 10 Nov framing), never as the tape's
   verdict or the piece's.
7. **Considered and not used** (list them for his eye):
   - a second narrator answering back: the frame never plays, and it would sound like the programme;
   - survivor voices: no testimony;
   - a switch to turn the notes off: the notes are the point, so they always show.

## Part B (S390): the TVs: what everyone sees, and what they can go on watching (Opus design; code; walked)

### B1. What everyone sees: the short tape, once per room

1. In each room, at one moment, **the piece takes the visitor to the TV and plays the short tape** (A7, at most 60 s:
   the title, one lesson and its card, one commercial, the collapse and its card).
   It is a conducted beat: a scripted send or a conducted turn. **It is never triggered by a look, and never waits for
   one** (CLAUDE.md R28 rule 1, revised 10 Oct).
2. When the short tape ends, **the tape pauses itself on its own chapter list** (for example "5 more parts · ▶ Play"),
   and the visitor is free.
3. **When:** between beats of that era's story. Never during a `felt` or `respite` scene, and never while another voice
   speaks: side-messages, conduction, toasts and Lamby wait for one another. The TV turns on in narrative order, never
   at the era's first second (sequence over presence). Ground each moment in the story:
   - **1997:** the fellowship's tape arrives with Daniel's programme;
   - **2016:** the course is assigned to Vera;
   - **2026:** her family sends Maya the parents' course. That fits his 7 Nov ruling (the pressure and the shame are her
     family's): her father's link, "watch this", on her room's monitor.

   Choose the exact beat from each era's ledger, and name it for his eye.
4. **Pacing:** measure how much time the short tapes add to each era and to the default cut, and report it.

### B2. What they can go on watching: the whole tape, on the same TV

1. **The TV's own controls are drawn on its screen,** like the room's other device screens. They are:
   - ▶/❚❚ play and pause;
   - ⏮ ⏭ the previous and next part;
   - ⏏ eject (stop);
   - in 2016, "Skip ad" on the mid-roll spots, which is period-true.

   A press is resolved on release (the S80 rule). In the headset, it is the pointer and the trigger; on a phone, a tap.
   Nothing counts down.
2. **It remembers where you stopped,** the part and the second, for the rest of the visit: in memory only, wiped on exit
   like everything else (no storage, ever). Leaving the TV and coming back resumes it.
3. **Nothing waits on it.** No beat depends on watching. When the story needs to speak or send the visitor elsewhere,
   the TV pauses itself and keeps its place. One voice at a time.
4. **At the era's update, the TV stops.** Its place is kept for the Close.
5. **Lamby** may offer, once and ignorably, to take a stalled visitor back to the TV. It counts toward the era's
   unsolicited balloons (at most 8). Lamby never voices or delivers the counter-cards: those are the piece's record.
