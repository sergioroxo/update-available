# SOURCES — the "Truth in Love" TV ads and the ex-gay web directory (2026-10-31)

*Read on 2026-10-31 from four web.archive.org captures he found, plus two more pages of the same campaign on the same site. HTML was read as text in the terminal only. No images, audio, video or RealMedia were downloaded. Nothing was saved to disk except this report. No full script is reproduced: scripts are described by structure. No phone numbers are written out. Private individuals who appear in the ads are not named. Anything uncertain carries [VERIFY SOURCE].*

## 1. Summary

1. CWA's own site (cwfa.org) hosted two TV spots from 1999, "Mom" and "Families", under the campaign title "Truth in Love". The index page offers each as Play, Download, and Read the script.
2. The "mom" script page is a two-column table, On Screen and Audio, with 9 rows and 7 small stills. It is the nearest thing to a storyboard we have.
3. The mom ad's shape: childhood photo, family room, "bad choices", a cost-of-not-changing beat, a mother speaking to other parents, one line from the son, a dissolve to the earlier print ads, a black end card with a referral number.
4. The ad shows no programme. "THE PROGRAM" is a single phrase naming a faith source. That absence is useful for the 2003 Story Editor.
5. The end cards point to two different ministries (one per ad), and the directory in source 3 lists the same ministry with the same number. The funnel is documentary: ad, number, directory.
6. The GeoCities directory is "as of 4-23-2003" by its own header. That is a 2003 web artefact for the piece's 2003 era, captured in 2008.
7. The religioustolerance page is a critical secondary source. It gives the campaign's timeline, a count of the coalition, and the counter-ads. Some of its dates and counts conflict with CWA's own pages.
8. Four RealMedia files exist in the archive (2 streams, 2 downloads). None downloaded, pending his permission. Running time is stated nowhere.

## 5. What this means for the academic article

Argument: SOGICE as a visual and interface culture that redesigns coercion as care.

1. **The grammar of care.** The campaign turns an attack into an offer by moving the opposition to "hate" and the offer to "hope". The mother's address to other parents makes pressure on a third person look like love. The ad speaks first to the family, not to the queer person. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20011126031813/http://www.cwfa.org/library/family/truthinlove/mom.shtml
2. **The funnel is on the page.** The ad ends in a number. The index page repeats a referral number. The two ads end on two different ministries. The 2003 directory lists the same ministry. So media, number and directory form one chain. Sources: https://web.archive.org/web/20011204191826/http://www.cwfa.org/library/family/truthinlove/ ; https://web.archive.org/web/20011204191826/http://www.cwfa.org/library/family/truthinlove/families.shtml ; https://web.archive.org/web/20080516071642/http://geocities.com/exgaylinks/links.html
3. **Media quoting media.** The TV spot dissolves to the earlier newspaper ads as proof. The newspaper campaign had 19 named organisations; the TV list is 4. The coalition shrinks and the faces get closer. Sources: https://web.archive.org/web/20011204191826/http://www.cwfa.org/library/family/truthinlove/1998.shtml ; mom.shtml above.
4. **Distribution as a download offer.** The ads were offered as a RealPlayer G2 stream, a tiny downloadable file, and a text script, with bandwidth donated by a Christian radio network. A plug-in badge sits next to the call to action. Coercive messaging arrives as software and as an accessible transcript. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20011204191826/http://www.cwfa.org/library/family/truthinlove/
5. **The directory as interface of care.** The 2003 directory uses colour badges (new, updated, recommended), anchors, "back to top", mission statements and archived dead links. It looks like a library or a help desk. Its own voice uses the words hope and healing. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20080516071642/http://geocities.com/exgaylinks/links.html
   *Counter-record:* a contemporary critical essay documents professional-association rejection, counter-ads and a legal dispute. It is secondary and has some date conflicts. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20011127053432/http://www.religioustolerance.org/hom_ads.htm [VERIFY SOURCE]

- RealVideo 2 at **176 × 120 pixels, 15 frames a second**, with Sipro voice audio at 8 kHz. About **20 kb/s** in
  all.
- Each clip is **64 s** long (63.78 s).
- That is a dial-up stream: what a 56k visitor would have watched in a small RealPlayer window around 2000–2002.

**"mom", shot order** (one frame every 4 s; it matches the script page in §3):

1. Grainy black-and-white home footage of children.
2. A warm living room: curtains, a window and a plant behind. A mother and her adult son sit side by side.
3. A brief cut to bright holiday footage, then a night street scene.
4. Close shots of the mother speaking, then the son speaking.
5. The 1998 newspaper ads dissolve in, one after another, as "proof". The headlines are legible in the frames.
6. A black end card: a two-part slogan (it's not about one thing, it's about hope) over a referral phone number.

**"families", shot order:**

1. It opens on a 1998 print ad.
2. Couples embracing.
3. Couples, then couples with their small children, in front of a dark studio curtain, with name captions.
4. A row of couples standing.
5. A full group with children.
6. The same two-part slogan on the group shot, with a toll-free number.

**What it adds:**

- **The pipeline in one minute:** print ad → home video → the couple or the family → the earlier ads as evidence →
  a number to call. The 2003 Story Editor's BEFORE / AFTER / CALL NOW is that pipeline. Its missing beat (the
  earlier ads cut in as proof) could become a fifth chapter, "AS SEEN", built from the piece's own invented ads.
- **The look of the stream is period interface culture.** A 176 × 120, 15 fps, blocky RealVideo window is how most
  people met these ads online. The 2003 page's "WATCH: DIAL-UP (56k)" link already points at this. The editor's
  preview could render Daniel's cut at that size and frame rate.
- **The slogan template**, "it's not about [hate], it's about [hope]", is the care-not-coercion reversal in a single
  line. It belongs in the article (§3b of the companion) and, in invented words, could be the piece's own end card.
