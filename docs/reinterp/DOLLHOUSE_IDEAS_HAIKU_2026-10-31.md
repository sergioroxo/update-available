# The dollhouse: eight fresh ideas (31 Oct 2026)

*Written by Haiku 5.5, as a sub-agent. Ideas only. Nothing is built. Read first: DESIGN_MISSING_YEARS §0 and §5, and VR_AS_A_LAYER §5 (VR-06).*

## The brief, in short

- At a change of room, the room dissolves into a small model of the house. In VR it is held in front of the visitor for about 4 seconds. In the browser, the camera flies between rooms and has no model.
- The model shows the house only. No programme building, no camp, no clinic.
- The model must tell the missing years: 1999 to 2002, 2004 to 2015, and the years after 2016.
- No beat depends on the visitor looking somewhere. The piece takes them there. The changes run as a sequence the piece conducts.
- No clock and no timers. Each change is cued by the era change, never by time passing.
- No sound. No mascot. No mechanics. Nothing here appears in a felt scene.
- In VR the model comes to the visitor. Nothing in these ideas moves the visitor.

**A size note.** The brief says the model is held 45 cm out. VR_AS_A_LAYER says about 60 cm wide. These ideas use the brief's 45 cm. At that distance, any label on the model must be large. Keep labels to a word or two.

**Budget note.** Each idea below names its main cost. The budget is 75 draw calls on Quest 3, with at most 3 hero objects in a scene. Most ideas here use one hero object or none.

---

## 1. Flat-Pack

- **VR:** The model is built again at every era, from that era's own material. In 1997 it is a cardboard kit that folds itself up in front of you. In 2003 it is moulded plastic. In 2016 it is a grey printed shell, with its layers showing. In 2026 it is clean, with no seams. The piece does the building. The visitor only watches it come together.
- **Browser:** The flight shows the house folding into a kit and unfolding again in the new material. Then the camera settles.
- **Missing time:** Between the eras, someone rebuilt the house. It was renovated, refitted and re-clad. The model tells that history of making, not just the rooms.
- **Risk:** Each material is a new mesh and a new material. That spends draw calls. It can also turn into a joke about craft museums. Keep every material inside the era palettes.

## 2. Dust Sheets

- **VR:** At each change, the rooms nobody is living in are covered with white dust sheets. The room of the new era is uncovered. The sheet slides off the new room in one sweep, and the other two rooms go under cloth. Under the sheets, the furniture is still there.
- **Browser:** The same sweep happens during the flight. The sheets slide on and off in the same order.
- **Missing time:** The house is shown between occupants. From 2003 to 2016, and after, the house stands mostly covered. The visitor reads the gap as a shape, with no screen and no text.
- **Risk:** A covered room can read as a dead room, or a dead person. The piece must make the sheet mean "not in use", never death. Keep the sheets plain and white, and never show a shape under them that looks like a body.

## 3. The Fridge Door

- **VR:** The model's fridge is at the front, and its door fills with paper notes held by magnets. In 1997 one note reads: "Family Form, Fri." In 2003 there is a takeaway menu and a Restorify sticker. In 2016 there is a shared list. In 2026 there is a printed reminder: "Check-in tonight." Between eras, the old notes peel off and the new ones go up in one move.
- **Browser:** The notes slide onto the fridge in the flight, and the camera passes them.
- **Missing time:** The years from 1999 to 2002, and from 2004 to 2015, are a fridge with the old notes gone and the new ones not yet up. The gap is a bare door.
- **Risk:** Fridge notes can speak for the people who wrote them. That breaks the rule. Keep every note to logistics or the system's plain notices, and never let a note say what happened.

## 4. Lights Out

- **VR:** The model's windows are lit or dark. In 1997 every window goes out at once. In 2003 one window stays lit. In 2016 two stay lit. In 2026 every window is lit, and none goes out. The lights change on the era cue only.
- **Browser:** The same lights change in the flight. The camera does not follow any one window.
- **Missing time:** It tells how the house's nights changed between eras. Who was up late. Whose light was left on. The visitor never sees the nights in between, but the lit windows show that they happened.
- **Risk:** A lit window can read as surveillance, or as a trap in a respite scene. Lights out can also drift into horror, and the tone must stay soft lo-fi. Keep every lamp warm. Never tie a light to a clock.

## 5. The Loft Box

- **VR:** One cardboard box sits in the model's loft. Its label is blank, with no name written on it. In 1997 the box is in the bedroom, with its lid off. In the gap it moves up to the loft, and the lid is taped. At each change, the box drops a floor or climbs one, by a hand's width. The box is never opened in the model, and the visitor never touches it.
- **Browser:** The box slides between floors during the flight. The move is the same as in VR.
- **Missing time:** It tells where things went between the eras. What is packed away, and where it is kept. It links to the 1997 folder and the racket, with no face and no name.
- **Risk:** A box can look like the programme took his things. That is a felt beat on an operable surface, so the box must stay blank and neutral. Its label must never name a person or a place in the programme.

## 6. The Dome in the Ceiling

- **VR:** A small grey camera dome appears on the model's ceiling, in the year it is fitted. It is the sharpest object in a soft model. The witness side is the sharp side, so its edges stay crisp while the house stays soft. Each later era, the dome is smaller. By 2026 it has gone into the light fitting.
- **Browser:** The dome appears as the flight passes over the ceiling.
- **Missing time:** It shows when the house started to be watched. Nobody decided this on screen. The camera went up in the years between, with no scene for it.
- **Risk:** The dome can become a plain diagram of surveillance, which is too explanatory. It can also read as the programme's own eye. Keep it small, grey and unlabelled, and never let it turn toward the visitor.

## 7. Post Pile

- **VR:** The model's front door has a letterbox. In each gap, post collects in the hall inside the model. The envelopes are plain and unopened. Each one is stamped in the house's flat hand: "HELD FOR REVIEW." The hall fills up through the gap. At the next era, the pile is swept into the loft.
- **Browser:** The flight passes the front door, and the pile is there in the hall. It fills and clears in the same order.
- **Missing time:** It shows the post that was kept back, from 1999 to 2002, and from 2004 to 2015. The height of the pile shows how long the gap was. No one reads a letter in the model.
- **Risk:** A pile of stamped envelopes can turn into a counter, and that reads as a score. Keep the pile unnumbered and never let the height go up as a gauge. "Held for review" is a rule of the house's post, and it must stay in that register.

## 8. The Door Asks

- **VR:** The model's front door has a long strip of terms pinned to it. At each change, the strip has grown. The new terms come down from the top in one sweep. At the bottom, one line reads "I Agree." The piece reads the terms through the door. The visitor does not have to press it.
- **Browser:** The strip grows on the door during the flight. The camera passes it, and the strip is read as one line.
- **Missing time:** The house agreed to terms in years nobody sees. Updates ran in the gaps, and each one changed the terms. The strip shows that they happened, with no count.
- **Risk:** It repeats the in-era update beat, and that beat is already signature. It must stay short. The strip must not show a number of updates, or it becomes a score. Keep it unnumbered and on the door only.

---

## My two favourites

**1. Dust Sheets.** The gap shows itself as a shape, under cloth. The visitor reads the missing years with no screen and no words. The piece does all the moving, so no look is needed. Its cost is low, because the sheets are plain planes.

**2. The Loft Box.** One object runs through every gap. It links the 1997 folder and the racket without a face. Its label stays blank, so it keeps to the house and never names a person.
