## 6. JEV (P7-55: what it is, and how to reach it)

He sent two links:

- TypeSafe AI's announcement of **Jev**, a "System One model"
  (https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev).
- **OpenRouter's guide** (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/community/jev).

**How to reach it.** Through OpenRouter, with an ordinary OpenRouter API key:

- model `typesafe/jev-1.14` (or the alias `~typesafe/jev-latest`);
- the Decisions API at `POST https://openrouter.ai/api/alpha/decisions` (HTTP, or the TypeScript, Python or Go SDKs);
- you pay for input tokens only, and each answer reports its own cost.

*(I first wrote that it was waitlist-only and not on OpenRouter. That was wrong: TypeSafe's own page did not mention
OpenRouter, and he corrected it.)*

**What it does.** Typed decisions with probabilities:

- **Choice**: pick from a list.
- **Score**: rate something.
- **Box**: locate something in an image.
- **Noul**: the fourth type in OpenRouter's guide, not yet looked into.

It returns no prose, no explanations and no code. Its context is 262k tokens, but requests over about 32 KB of text
run in a slower, less accurate mode.

**What it could do for this project.** A fast, cheap router. Given his question ("where is the 2003 check-in?"), it
picks the right document or source file from a list we give it, or ranks which of `docs/reinterp/` is current. It
cannot explain what it finds. A small script in `tools/` could send the question with a short index of files and
open the top answers.

**The laws still hold.**

- It is a tool for working **on** the project, never part of the piece. The piece makes no network calls.
- His API key stays out of the repository.
- Nothing from the knowledge base or the survey goes out without his say.

**Status:** possible now. It needs his OpenRouter key on his machine, and his yes to try a small `tools/ask-jev.mjs`
router.
