## First, what `[VERIFY SOURCE]` is — in plain words
- **What it is.** A sticker we put on a sentence that cites something. It means *"Sérgio has not yet
  confirmed this citation himself."* CLAUDE.md's rule: anything cited carries it until you check it. It
  says nothing about whether the claim is true — only that the checking is not finished.
- **Where a visitor meets it today.** Not as the bracketed shorthand (S175 retired that for the public):
  - on the **Close's four panels** (the dossier pages), on the **menu's sources view**, and on the **public
    sources page**, the sticker reads **"(this source is still being verified)"** at the end of the sentence;
  - ⚑ **one place still shows the raw `[VERIFY SOURCE]`**: the 1997 debrief card (the pillow scene and the
    questionnaire, on Daniel's screen). I found it while writing this; Batch 1 makes it read like the others.
- **What clearing changes.** The words "(this source is still being verified)" disappear from that sentence,
  everywhere at once. Nothing else moves: the sentence, its label (documented / disputed / imagined) and its
  links stay as they are. A cleared sentence reads as settled — which is why only you can clear it.
- **What "the 24 July run" is.** Your ChatGPT Deep Research pass of 24 July: it checked each claim, said what
  was imprecise, and gave sources. `docs/SOURCE_VERIFICATION_RESULTS_2026-07-24.md` turned it into
  corrections (C1–C6); those corrections were applied to the text in S47. So for A and B, the checking **has
  been done and the text already says what the run said** — only the sticker is left.
