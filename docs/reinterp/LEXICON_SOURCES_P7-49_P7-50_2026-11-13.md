## 1. What each source is

### P7-49: the members' survey on causes (People Can Change, June 2004)

- **Title:** "Perceptions Among Men With Unwanted Same-Sex Attractions (SSA) of the Factors Contributing to the
  Development of Their Homosexual Feelings". "Survey by" People Can Change (an ex-gay peer organisation that ran online
  support groups and the "Journey Into Manhood" weekends), dated June 2004, 13 pages.
- **Read from:** his copy, `~/Pc_Simulation/PDF/MD/Surveyoncauses.md` (all 13 pages).
- **Checked against:** the organisation's own PDF as its site served it, Wayback capture of 10 Jun 2005,
  https://web.archive.org/web/20050610202501/http://peoplecanchange.com/Surveyoncauses.pdf (HTTP 200 on 13 Nov 2026;
  same title, date, sample and figures as the local copy). The same file was served again in 2007 and 2011 (identical
  Wayback digest).
- **How it reaches the Lexicon's Sources today:** through the IFTCC Archives record, which is NARTH's reprint of the
  survey (vol. 13 no. 3, December 2004, pp. 36–42). That record holds the title only, no text; this is why the S241 Deep
  Research could not reproduce any figure (LEXICON_SOURCES_VERIFIED_2026-10-11.md #41 and its "Primary publication
  access" note). **The new capture is the primary copy**; the JSON for it is in §6.
- **Method (p. 3):** sent on 16 May 2004 to the 16 online groups the organisation ran (about 600 men), closed 9 June;
  **205 replies, a 34% response**. "To be admitted to any of these groups in the first place, members must affirm to the
  moderator that they are seeking to diminish unwanted homosexual feelings" (or to support others who are). The
  questionnaire **offered a list of 25 factors**, "from 'biology' to 'personal choice'", introduced as factors that "some
  men have identified … as possible causes or sources" of their attractions, and asked how much each had contributed.
- **Its own caveats (p. 3, "Limitations and Caveats"):** it measures perceptions, "and not a measurement of actual
  causes"; the sample is men "dissatisfied with having same-sex attractions"; it "should not be considered
  representative of the gay community". The same paragraph then asks readers not to "dismiss the 'expertise'" of the
  members.
- **Terminology (p. 3):** "SSA" is preferred to "gay", which these men "strongly reject" as "a socio-political identity";
  used as an adjective "to avoid labeling one's self as 'being' SSA".

### P7-50: the LDS Social Services practitioner module (Wayback, 15 Feb 2005)

- **Title:** "Understanding Homosexual Orientation Problems" / "Changing Homosexual Orientation Problems", a training
  module for "the LDS Social Services practitioner". Men only ("it discusses only male homosexual orientation
  problems"). It requires the reader to know the Church's *Homosexuality* booklet (2nd ed., 1981).
- **Read from:** his copy, `~/Pc_Simulation/PDF/MD/web-archive-org-web-20050215014802-http-mentalhealthlibrary-.md`
  (all of it: Pre-Assessment, Sections 1–3, Charts 1–3, Appendices A–E).
- **Checked against:** the capture itself,
  https://web.archive.org/web/20050215014802/http://mentalhealthlibrary.info/library/same/samelds/sameldsfamily/understandingchanging/understandhomosexualx.htm
  (HTTP 200 on 13 Nov 2026; every passage quoted below is on it).
- **Its date:** the module is undated. It cites nothing later than 1980 (Masters and Johnson 1979; James and Jongeward
  1980) and depends on the 1981 booklet, so it was **written in the 1980s**; in 2005 it was still online, on a
  third-party "mental health library" site, not the Church's own. The Lexicon had dated its four-stage row
  "c. 1990s–2005"; that had no basis in the document.
- **Naming:** the document names itself (LDS Social Services, the Church). The Lexicon's glass names no organisation in
  the Reasons table; the Sources page carries the capture under that name, as before.

## 2. What they say that matters for the piece

### P7-49 (page references are the PDF's)

| What | Where | Figure |
|---|---|---|

| **Physical characteristics, body type** | pp. 11–12 | 69% |

| "a matter of **personal choice**" | p. 5 | 82% disagreed |

| **Change** (p. 13) | p. 13 | 84% reported "some decrease" in attraction to men; yet **57% still said their feelings were more homosexual than heterosexual**, and **1%** called themselves exclusively heterosexual |

**For the piece:** this is the programme's own list of causes coming back as its members' self-knowledge. The 25
"factors" were the survey's, the same roots the organisation's "Root Problems" page taught (S02: "father hunger, mother
enmeshment, peer rejection, poor gender esteem"). Men admitted to the groups only on affirming a wish to change rated
them, years later, from memory. It records what they had come to believe. It is not evidence that any of these causes
is real, and the survey says so itself. (APA 2009, p. 3: the studies of people in change efforts "have relied entirely
on convenience samples".)

### P7-50 (section references are the module's)

- **"A Gospel Position" (Section 1, Etiology):** moral free agency makes it "inconsistent to believe that a person's
  homosexual orientation is inborn"; it quotes church leaders calling "born 'that way'" "a base lie" and "false
  doctrine". Environment can limit choices, but "Negative influences … do not cause his homosexual orientation".
- **"Professional Theories" / "What Does the Research Show?":** biological findings "far from conclusive"; the module
  adopts the learning theory: "a homosexual orientation occurs through a process of learning". The man "knows he is a
  male but does not fully understand how a masculine male is supposed to think and act". Weak fathers (Bene 1965); the
  mother-child conflict of Bieber 1962 is noted, with the criticism of its method.
- **"A Suggested Etiological Framework":** four stages, *confusion* (a rejecting or uninvolved father; a mother who
  becomes "smothering" to fill his place; peers who reject the boy), *filling the void* (he discovers masturbation and
  "builds in his mind an association between warm acceptance, sexual performance, and homoerotic behavior"; his
  "protective methods", chosen because of "the deep hurt they feel from parents and peers", grow "self-serving" and
  "narcissistic"; "fantasy and fragmentation" are called "essentially universal for these individuals"), *sexual
  identity crisis*, and *resolution* (saying "I am a homosexual" is the danger: "it is so important not to incorrectly
  label").
- **Severity (Charts 1 and 2):** "mild, moderate, severe symptoms", graded by how often he has had sex with men and by
  masturbation, pornography, dating, church activity, his "self-focus" and whether he "feels he was born that way".
- **Implications for Change:** "Message of hope" (the counsellor "must believe, and he must come to believe, that
  change is possible"); "Chaste behavior" ("erase sinful homoerotic habits"); "Avoidance of labeling" ("Discourage the
  client from using that inaccurate label"). Change is compared to curing alcoholism, "subject to continual vigilance".
- **The treatments (Chart 3, Appendix A):** stop-thought (he yells "STOP" in his mind and counts how often), systematic
  desensitisation "to eliminate fears associated with heterosexual interaction", homoerotic fantasy control, "proper
  dress and grooming", "appropriate same-sex roles", "female interaction assignments", "Have him define the ideal
  female", visits to "model homes", "Forgive parents", "Write a special letter to parents", a "social-emotional
  genealogy", an autobiography "to see how his homosexual orientation was learned", guided imagery ("Imagine your father
  intervening for you against your mother's domination"), a repentance programme with "weekly meetings with priesthood leader",
  "Eliminate contact with homosexual associates", "Make financial contributions to the Church". Appendix D calls
  "masturbation therapy and some forms of aversion therapy" inappropriate for the agency; the APA lists systematic
  desensitisation among the behavioural change methods of the 1960s–70s (APA 2009, p. 22).
- **Appendix B:** three first-person "success stories" (one man's arrest, one man's church court, a wife's account), to
  be read to clients "in the agency office". **Not used** (§4).

**For the piece:** the cure is built to fit the cause. If the cause is a father who never taught the boy to be a man,
the cure is retraining him in "appropriate same-sex roles" and dates; if it is a mother, he imagines his father taking
his side against her; if it is "self-serving" coping, the cure is assigned "service" with its own log. The person is
assessed as the symptom, on a chart.

## 4. What I chose not to use, and why

- **The members' open answers** (p. 6: fathers described as "distant" 55%, "cold or mean" 31%; p. 7: "misfits, loners";
  p. 9: four quoted answers about pornography and boyhood sex play). These are the respondents' own words about their
  lives, close to testimony: ETHICS #2 (no verbatim survivor testimony), and the piece speaks about the system, not for
  the people in it. Only the survey's own labels are quoted.
- **Appendix B of P7-50** (three first-person accounts, an arrest, a church court, a wife's account). Testimony, and
  private lives. Never.
- **The hypothetical client "Ted"** (Section 2's case study): an invented person in someone else's document; nothing a
  row needs.
- **The change figures of P7-49** (p. 13: 57% still more homosexual than heterosexual; 1% exclusively heterosexual).
  Striking, and documented, but not a cause: they belong on a word's page ("ex-gay", "overcomer"), whose `close` lines
  are his. Offered in the eye list, not written.
- **The names of the church leaders quoted** in P7-50: real people; the Lexicon names no individual on its glass.
- **The full treatment list** (Chart 3, Appendix A): not the Reasons table's job; two of its items carry the learning
  row. It is a documented practice list if the receipt ("the receipt prints every practice", job 22 Part B) ever wants a
  primary source for the 1997-era practices.
- **"Ideal woman", "model homes", "proper dress and grooming"** as a joke: the material is absurd in places, but the
  Reasons table is the Lexicon's plain voice; satire lives only in the sellers' Words of the Day, and none was written.
- **Women's and trans rows:** P7-50 mentions a 1965 study of women in passing; neither source is about lesbians or trans
  people. No change there.
- **Rows already right:** father, mother, peers, abuse, "heterophobia" (P7-49's "fear, envy, resentment, anger toward
  other males" confirms it), media and "agency" (P7-50's free-agency doctrine confirms it): left as they were. The
  `born that way` and `symptoms` words already carry P7-50 correctly; their closes are his and were not touched.
