## Part A (S395): the tapes v6

### A1. The shape of a two-minute tape

1. **The label:** one frame, ≤ 4 s, silent, the piece's voice. "Made from real teaching materials, [years]. You can
   stop at any time." 1997's can add one line.
2. **Part 1, which everyone sees** (option A), about 50–60 s: the title, the first lesson, and the first advert.
   **End of Part 1** is a short card in the programme's grammar, then the tape stops there in the room (B).
3. **Part 2**, about 50 s: the second lesson or the strongest middle material, and an advert if it earns its place.
4. **The collapse, then the break:** one screen, the programme's own admission or line, with its [n] in the bar, about
   10 s.
5. **The end card,** ≤ 5 s.

**The bar,** below the picture inside the frame:
- the subtitles, large, one or two lines;
- a thin divider;
- the source as "[n] short title, year, p." in small type.

The long text, every full citation and the long break go to `references.json` and `break_long.json` for the Close (B2).

### A2. Look first, and WAIT for his yes

Before rendering any full tape:
1. **One style frame per tape**: the most typical frame, with the bar, at the set's shape.
2. **A 15 s motion test per tape,** with its sound, showing the identity in motion (how a lesson enters, how the bar
   behaves, how an advert cuts in).
3. **Name the identity in two lines each:**
   - 1997: the church-hall felt board on VHS, made clear and bright;
   - 2016: the DVD course as a designed infographic;
   - 2026: the investigative campaign film he loved.

   A better identity is allowed if you can show why.

Put the three frames and three tests on one page, "Tapes v6: the look", and tell the orchestrator. **Then wait for his
yes on the look** (the orchestrator relays it). Part B goes on meanwhile.
