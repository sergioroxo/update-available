# Lexicon sources: verified source map (11 October 2026)

This file keeps **USE** (evidence that an organisation used a term or presented a reason) separate from **CLAIM** (evidence about whether the reason is true). Each numbered entry points to source IDs; the source register gives the full title, publisher/author, date, exact URL opened or attempted, one short quotation, and what it establishes. The closing URL table records access and all entries using each URL.

**Verdicts** use only the requested labels. For terms that are values, metaphors, or political frames, the verdict describes their category, not whether a person may use them. “Not supported” means the cited evidence does not support the causal or change claim; it does not mean every individual’s experience is identical. **Contested** is retained for live disputes and does not settle them.

A quote appears once per source in the register (maximum 14 words). Where the source text could not be retrieved, that is stated in place of a quote. Wayback timestamps below are the exact capture timestamps served. Current pages without a publication date are marked “n.d.” Quotes from the Focus on the Family booklet are reproduced in an accessible ministry reprint, not checked against the original booklet.

## Words

### 1. same-sex attraction
**Verdict:** not supported (as a proposed condition to reduce).
**USE:** [S01], [S06b] — Evergreen uses SSA as a therapeutic target; [S03] — Exodus uses it in its change framework.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA review finds orientation-change theories and efforts unsupported and potentially harmful.

### 2. unwanted same-sex attraction
**Verdict:** not supported (as evidence that attraction is a disorder or readily changeable).
**USE:** [S10] — Reintegrative Therapy explicitly addresses unwanted attractions; [S15] — 2004 IFTCC record documents a member-perception survey with this phrase in its title; article text was not retrievable here.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S18] — APA and UN sources address change efforts and pathologization; “unwanted” describes the speaker’s evaluation, not a cause.

### 3. the struggle
**Verdict:** a moral frame, not a finding.
**USE:** [S05] — Love in Action describes strugglers; [S01] — Evergreen frames SSA as a struggle.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA records mixed participant experiences with change efforts; struggle is not an orientation diagnosis.

### 4. the lifestyle
**Verdict:** not supported (as a description of orientation as a chosen way of life).
**USE:** [S03], [S06b] — Exodus and Evergreen discuss a gay person’s lifestyle; [S05] — Love in Action uses lifestyle language.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S19] — APA and the Royal College of Psychiatrists do not support orientation as a chosen condition to change.

### 5. ex-gay
**Verdict:** not supported (as proof of orientation change).
**USE:** [S04] — Exodus published testimonials under a heading about leaving homosexuality.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S18] — APA review and UN report assess change efforts; testimony or identity change alone does not establish changed orientation.

### 6. overcomer
**Verdict:** a moral frame, not a finding.
**USE:** [S04] — Exodus testimonials use overcomer language; [S06b] — Evergreen says people can overcome behavior and diminish attractions.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S17] — APA review and resolution find no reliable efficacy basis and identify risk.

### 7. brokenness
**Verdict:** a moral frame, not a finding.
**USE:** [S05] — Love in Action describes relational brokenness; [S06] — Evergreen discusses women’s emotional/relational problems.
**CLAIM:** [S18], [S21] — UN calls pathologization medically false; WHO classifies gender incongruence outside mental disorders. Neither source says nobody can experience distress.

### 8. root causes
**Verdict:** not supported (as a hidden cause whose treatment changes orientation).
**USE:** [S02] — People Can Change lists root problems; [S03] — Exodus uses root-issues language; [S08] — FOF booklet titles explicitly claim roots and causes for lesbianism.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA says family-pattern theories of same-sex orientation have been discredited.

### 9. the wound
**Verdict:** not supported (as a cause of sexual orientation).
**USE:** [S02] — People Can Change lists unhealed wounds; [S08] — the FOF booklet presents a mother-wound account.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S19] — APA and RCPsych find no evidence that such family histories cause orientation.

### 10. emotional dependency
**Verdict:** not supported (as a cause of lesbian orientation).
**USE:** [S06], [S08] — Evergreen’s women’s page and the accessible FOF reprint use dependency/enmeshment explanations.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA finds family-pattern causal theories discredited; the evidence does not deny that unhealthy relationships can occur in any orientation.

### 11. gender confusion
**Verdict:** contested (the historical use is documented; it is not itself an explanation of trans identity).
**USE:** [S07] — LDS Social Services material uses confusion in a staged account of homosexual identity; [S14] — Transgender Trend uses confusion language about gender-questioning youth.
**CLAIM:** [S21], [S22] — WHO’s ICD-11 FAQ and APA DSM fact sheet distinguish gender incongruence/dysphoria from gender nonconformity as such.

### 12. reparative therapy
**Verdict:** not supported (as a proven orientation-change therapy).
**USE:** [S10] — Reintegrative Therapy Association defines its approach in relation to reparative therapy; [S20] — UK MoU lists reparative therapy among conversion-therapy labels.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S17], [S20] — APA, UN and UK signatories find no sound basis for orientation-change claims and note risks.

### 13. reintegrative therapy
**Verdict:** not supported (as a demonstrated means of changing orientation).
**USE:** [S10] — association describes trauma-focused treatment and reports some clients’ sexuality change as a byproduct.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S17] — APA review and resolution find efficacy evidence lacking; the association’s client reports do not establish causal efficacy.

### 14. SOGICE
**Verdict:** the community’s own word.
**USE:** [S11] — The Equality Project explains the SOGICE Statement and its umbrella term.
**CLAIM:** [S18], [S20] — UN and UK MoU describe the practices covered; SOGICE is a label, not a causal claim.

### 15. conversion practices
**Verdict:** documented.
**USE:** [S18], [S20] — UN Independent Expert and UK MoU use the umbrella terminology to describe practices.
**CLAIM:** [S17], [S18], [S20] — APA, UN and UK signatories report lack of efficacy evidence and risk of harm.

### 16. accountability partner
**Verdict:** documented.
**USE:** [S09] — Restored Ministries names accountability partners in its sexuality mentorship series.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA’s evidence review does not establish accountability arrangements as an effective orientation-change method.

### 17. freedom, healing, restoration
**Verdict:** a moral frame, not a finding.
**USE:** [S04] — Exodus promises freedom from homosexuality; [S09] — Restored Ministries uses restoration/healing language.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S18] — APA and UN sources do not validate these promotional terms as evidence of orientation change.

### 18. affirming
**Verdict:** contested (the word covers different practices and disputed policy positions).
**USE:** [S12], [S13], [S14] — Genspect and Transgender Trend criticize gender-affirming care; Jenkins describes exploratory therapy in relation to it.
**CLAIM:** [S31], [S32], [S33] — NHS/Cass response, Yale critique, and BMA evaluation statement document competing clinical and policy positions; the BMA page announces an evaluation, not its result.

### 19. gender ideology
**Verdict:** a political frame, not a finding.
**USE:** [S14] — Transgender Trend uses the phrase in its school-policy material; [S12] — Genspect uses political language about trans ideology in its position materials.
**CLAIM:** [S21], [S22] — WHO and APA sources classify clinical concepts; neither validates gender ideology as a medical diagnosis or causal mechanism.

### 20. social contagion
**Verdict:** contested.
**USE:** [S12] — Genspect FAQ advances social-influence concerns; [S23] — Littman’s parent-report study proposed ROGD as a hypothesis.
**CLAIM:** [S23], [S24], [S25] — Littman’s correction limits the study’s scope; Ashley critiques the ROGD construct and its evidence.

### 21. desistance
**Verdict:** contested.
**USE:** [S12] — Genspect discusses desistance in its account of gender-questioning youth.
**CLAIM:** [S26], [S27], [S28] — Steensma reports an older referred cohort; Newhook critiques persistence/desistance methods; Zucker argues against that critique. Do not generalize older cohorts to current services.

### 22. detransition
**Verdict:** contested.
**USE:** [S12] — Genspect publishes detransition material.
**CLAIM:** [S29], [S30] — Littman’s self-selected 100-person survey and Turban et al.’s large US survey measure different samples and questions; neither alone gives a population rate or a universal cause.

### 23. just exploring
**Verdict:** contested (the exact phrase was not verified as a network’s formal term).
**USE:** [S13], [S14], [S37] — Jenkins’ Genspect article and Transgender Trend advocate exploration; a peer-reviewed survey documents therapists affiliated with Therapy First who describe their approach as exploratory. The survey does not test efficacy.
**CLAIM:** [S20], [S22], [S34] — UK MoU protects help for uncertainty; APA and WPATH materials provide professional guidance. The dispute concerns what exploration means in practice, not whether young people may be uncertain.

### 24. chosen family
**Verdict:** the community’s own word.
**USE:** No good USE source located in conversion-practice organisations; it is a community term, not their causal vocabulary.
**CLAIM:** [S36] — a peer-reviewed study describes chosen family as kinship made by choice among queer and trans young adults. This supports the term’s community use, not a universal family structure.

### 25. born that way
**Verdict:** half right.
**USE:** [S06], [S06b], [S07] — Evergreen’s women’s page and FAQ address claims of innateness; LDS Social Services denies it in its staged theory.
**CLAIM:** [S19], [S16] — RCPsych describes multiple influences and no evidence of choice; APA does not support a single definitive cause. Multi-factorial uncertainty does not support change efforts.

### 26. legitimate needs
**Verdict:** not supported (as evidence that unmet needs cause orientation).
**USE:** [S01] — Evergreen frames same-sex attraction as an attempt to meet unmet emotional/social needs.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA finds family-pattern causal theories discredited; people’s needs are real, but the proposed causal link is unsupported.

### 27. symptoms (mild, moderate, severe)
**Verdict:** not supported (as a severity scale for sexual orientation).
**USE:** [S07] — LDS Social Services training material labels homosexuality-related problems mild, moderate, or severe.
**CLAIM:** [S16], [S19] — APA and RCPsych do not classify orientation itself as a disorder.

### 33. fear of heterosexual men (“heterophobia”)

### 34. a series of decisions / habit / sin
**Verdict:** a moral frame, not a finding.

### 35. pornography, masturbation, fantasy
**Verdict:** not supported as a cause of orientation.
**USE:** No good public primary source located in this pass that says these cause orientation. [S07] discusses sexual behaviours in a change programme, but it is not sufficient evidence for the stated causal claim.
**CLAIM:** [S16] — APA’s review provides no support for a behavioural-cause theory of orientation.

### 36. media, schools, “gay activists”, culture
**Verdict:** a political frame, not a finding.

**CLAIM:** [S16], [S18] — APA and UN sources do not treat exposure to LGBTQ people or ideas as a cause of orientation.

### 37. spiritual causes (demons, sin)

**CLAIM:** [S18] — UN documents faith-based practices as practices, not as evidence that spiritual causes produce orientation.

### 38. “biology, environment and early development all play a role” (softened version)
**Verdict:** half right.
**USE:** [S06b] — Evergreen’s 2012 FAQ says no particular factor is established, then gives the softened multi-factor formulation.

### 39. trauma and “attachment loss” in clinical dress
**Verdict:** not supported as a cause of sexual orientation.

**CLAIM:** [S16], [S19] — APA rejects family-cause theories; RCPsych does not support orientation as a choice or disorder.

### 40. four stages (confusion, filling the void, identity crisis, resolution), “born this way” denied

### 41. programme members’ own 2004 survey of causes

### Trans

### 52. gender confusion from family dynamics
**Verdict:** contested (historical causal narratives should not be applied as a settled account of trans youth).

### 56. “gender ideology” in schools, media, law

### 57. homophobia as a driver of trans identity
**Verdict:** contested.

**CLAIM:** [S29], [S30], [S32] — Littman’s self-selected detransitioner survey records participants’ own explanations; Turban et al. report external pressures in another sample; Yale critiques Cass. These sources do not establish homophobia as a general cause of trans identity.

### 58. availability of puberty blockers as an explanation for rising referrals
**Verdict:** contested.
**USE:** [S14] — Transgender Trend’s briefing discusses blockers and new referral cohorts; [S12] — Genspect’s position materials argue against medical pathways.
**CLAIM:** [S31], [S33], [S35] — NHS/Cass response, BMA evaluation announcement, and York systematic review address policy and treatment evidence. None establishes blocker availability as the cause of referral increases.
