## Wall text (≈330 words)

**YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED**
*PC Simulator — a browser and VR narrative, 45 minutes, seated*

You sit at one computer, in one room, for thirty years.

The room is a teenager's bedroom in 1997. On the desk is a beige PC that has been set up for you,
and the software on it is kind: it asks how you are, it finds you a place to talk to people like you,
it promises that things get easier. Over four eras — 1997, 2003, 2016, 2026 — the software updates
itself, the room ages around you, and the person at the desk changes: Daniel, then Daniel grown up,
then Vera, then Maya. What does not change is what the software is for.

*Your Update Has Failed* is about how organised conversion practices — the networks that try to
change people's sexual orientation or gender identity — moved online, and what that looked like from
the chair. It is told in the apparatus's own materials: a questionnaire, a message board, a wellness
programme, a moderation queue, a "support" assistant, a headset. Nothing here lectures. The systems
speak in their own voice, and the piece's argument is made by letting them run.

You never walk. You turn to look, you click what is in front of you, and you read. The one thing
the machine cannot classify — other people — is what finally breaks it.

The four rooms are invented. The organisations, products and names on the screens are invented
marks that stand for documented practices; the claims behind them rest on the project's research
and are set out, with their sources, in the piece's own dossier. No real person's name, likeness or
testimony appears. The piece keeps nothing you do: there is no account, no storage, no record —
which is the one promise none of the systems inside it could make.

Made by the SurvivingSOGICE project at the University of Bergen's Center for Digital Narrative, in
collaboration with an AI system (Claude, Anthropic): the code, the rooms, the sounds and most of the
writing are the model's, made under the project's direction, review and ethics rulings. That
collaboration is disclosed inside the work, at its end.

*Content note: depicts conversion practices, coercion by family and community, and a system that uses
a name a person does not use. Nothing graphic. You can leave at any time.*

## Programme blurb (≈70 words)

One computer, one room, thirty years. *Your Update Has Failed* is a seated browser-and-VR narrative
about how conversion practices moved online — told through the software itself, from a 1997 message
board to a 2026 "support" assistant, as it updates around four people who sit at the same desk. You
turn, you click, you read; the machine runs until the one thing it cannot classify breaks it. 45 min.
SurvivingSOGICE / UiB CDN, made with an AI collaborator.

## One line

A seated VR story about thirty years of conversion-practice software, told from the chair it was
aimed at.
