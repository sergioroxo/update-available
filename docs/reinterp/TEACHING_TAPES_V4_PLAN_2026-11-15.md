## 2. The rules for v4

1. **The TV is the system's screen.** It plays what was sold as reasons, and it never stops to correct itself. The
   pause cards ("the record answers") are gone.
2. **Extra information runs beside the tape and never pauses it** (§3, the margin).
3. **No contested report is used to claim something.** The 2009 APA report is out of the tapes.
4. **The programme is never used to answer the programme.** "THE SAME SOURCE SAYS" is gone.
5. **Honest, not sugarcoated.** Satire is the bridge: playful and over the top, inside the programme's own sales
   grammar, and it collapses. Then comes a space where the hardness is said plainly. People can always stop.
6. **A label before each tape**, in the piece's own voice: these are real teachings, shown as sources of education.
7. **What everyone is taken to must make sense as watching.** No montage that jumps to the end.
8. **Every tape richer and clearer:**
   - the medium is period-true (2016 is a DVD);
   - real styling;
   - no filler shot used over and over;
   - no on-screen text that the sound ignores, unless the silence is the point;
   - the sound louder and clear.
9. **ROGD is said plainly: it is not a diagnosis.**
10. **2026 teaches how their reasons about trans people changed, 1997 → 2026,** from the research.

Unchanged laws:
- the clinical debate is never satirised;
- fear of medical transition is shown as the anti-gender campaign's tactic, never voiced as a clinical position;
- no testimony, no real names, no deadnames;
- no death figures (his 10 Nov rule; see §6, question 4).

## 3. The shape of every tape: four movements

1. **The label** (the piece's voice, about 8 s, silent, in the record's style: near-black panel, blue-grey ink, the
   record's pixel face). It says what this is, where it comes from, and that you can stop. *Draft (1997), every fact
   to be checked:* "This tape is made from the teaching of real programmes, 1984–1997. Every reason in it was taught to
   real people as the reason they were gay. It is shown so you can see how it worked. You can stop it at any time."
2. **The tape, straight: the bridge.** The programme in its own grammar:
   - earnest where it teaches;
   - over the top where it sells (the commercials);
   - never pausing to argue.
3. **The margin**, extra information that runs beside the tape and never pauses it. At each lesson a one- or two-line
   strip in the record's style slides in at the bottom of the screen and stays while the lesson plays. It is clearly
   the piece's voice, never the tape's. It carries two kinds of line:
   - **TAUGHT IN:** where this exact teaching came from: the kind of document, the year, and the organisation where the
     knowledge base documents it. It tells the viewer this is real, and it is education, not a rebuttal.
   - **WHAT IS KNOWN:** one uncontested, dated fact. Examples: when a classification changed, what professional
     bodies have said, what became of the programme itself. Never a single disputed study; never the 2009 APA report.
4. **The break: the hardness.** After the collapse, the satire stops. The tape's colour, music and grammar drain away,
   the way an era's update fails in the piece. In plain words, from documented sources, the piece says what this
   teaching did to the people it was taught to:
   - the methods, the classes, the journals, the shame;
   - the years spent, the families turned into a cause.

   No testimony, no names. Then the sources roll. It is quiet, and it can be stopped.

## 5. Each tape

**1997, "Understanding Your Roots", made clearly, with the update at its centre.**

His note on the handbook's computer passages (15 Nov): "if this is about homosexuality like this is golden content for
the 1997 video … You read the documents, you know these elements you found, like use them."

The handbook's Chapter Thirteen, **"Displacement: Putting Off / Putting On"** (Love in Action, *Helping People Step Out
of Homosexuality*, rev. © 1995; LIA B2 F13, PDF pp. 136–141, printed 128–133), is the programme's own procedure for
overwriting a person, taught as a computer. All of it is about homosexuality.

What it teaches, in its words (short quotes; each page checked in the OCR, to be read again on the page image):
- **"Displacement is the idea that one thing pushes out or dislodges another."** "There is no neutral ground." "If
  there is a vacancy there, evil will fill it." (p. 128–129)
- **"Interference is the technical name for displacement":** new material "interferes with the old material that is
  there and pushes it out of your mind". (p. 129)
- **The forgetting curve as a blessing.** We forget "about 80 percent of everything we learned on day one". For a
  student that is failure; for someone leaving "the gay lifestyle, this principle can be a blessing". The "forbidden
  knowledge" "can be pushed out". (pp. 129–130)
- **Garbage in, garbage out.** Television is "a mind-boggling barrage of new morality propaganda", and "Gays on
  television are shown as superior people". "In the computer industry the saying is: garbage in/garbage out." (pp.
  130–131) *A tape on a TV, warning against TV.*
- **"What Do We Lose?"** The associations "triggered by music, clothing"; knowing your way around; "bars will close,
  the scene will change". (p. 132)
- **Memory tapes:** "mountains of material", "this bulk of the material that ejects the desires". (p. 132)
- **"We forget things that are contrary to our opinions."** If you are ambivalent, "the new information will find no
  permanent home in our minds". The failure is yours. (pp. 132–133)
- **"Our mind works like a computer; it is constantly placing information into categories."** (p. 135)
- **The admission (the hardness, in their own words):** "The more we fight against homosexual thoughts, the more
  intense they become." They "can't 'just stop'." "Leaving the gay lifestyle and the gay orientation may be the most
  difficult task of your entire life." (p. 133)
- **The workbook questions, printed in the margins:** "Have you ever been able to stop thinking about something? Did
  you succeed?" "What do you do when associations from the past overwhelm you?" (pp. 129, 132)
