## 2. AESTHETIC PIVOT — flat low-poly replaces the pixel-paint plan for 3D

Sérgio's call, prompted by the Poly Pizza CRT style: the 3D world is
**clean flat-shaded low-poly** (Crossy Road / Kenney / Poly-by-Google
language: chunky silhouettes, flat color faces, no textures or near-none),
NOT pixel-painted PS1 textures as ASSET_STRATEGY §3 proposed.

- **Pixel art remains the language of the screens** (desktop OS, witness
  panel) — unchanged, canon.
- The 3D pivot is also a **performance win for Quest 3**: untextured
  flat-shaded meshes are about the cheapest thing a mobile GPU can draw
  (few/no texture samples, small materials, low poly counts). Correct
  instinct.
- The room palette (moodboard) maps onto flat face colors directly.
- **Witness-side transition**: dusk-cream + blocky aesthetic gives the flip a
  geometric grammar — the same blocky language, drained of warmth and color,
  re-lit cold. "Blocky" carries across the flip; *palette and definition* are
  what change.
- ASSET_STRATEGY §3 (pixel-paint pass) is **demoted to optional accent**
  (e.g., a poster, a cassette label) — not the doctrine.

## 6. ⭐ THE STARTER KIT — fundamental redesign of the Era-1 entry (and of MentorRob)

Sérgio's question — "what is this mentor figure?" — exposed that MentorRob
as spontaneous DM was under-motivated. His proposal, adopted:

**Era 1 begins with physical media.** The person has received a document /
pamphlet with instructions (from family, parish, school counselor — off-screen,
composite). It comes with a **ministry CD-ROM or floppy** — the Starter Kit —
that the player inserts into the machine. The kit:

- autoruns a period-true shovelware interface (booklet pages on screen);
- plays a **MIDI hymn**, and/or an accompanying **cassette (K7) with a prayer**
  — heard *and* read (subtitled; also an accessibility win);
- walks the person through "first steps" — and its final step is **going
  online**: it points to the support channel and tells you who to trust
  (e.g. "in the chat, ask for Rob").

Consequences:

- **MentorRob is demoted from spontaneous warmth to pre-routed node.** He is
  still warm, still human-seeming, still never a cartoon villain — but the
  DM is no longer a coincidence: the network sent you to him before you ever
  typed a word. Witness side shows it: `referral source: starter kit v1.2` ·
  `trusted contact: assigned`. This answers "is he complicit?" — he is a node
  in a routing system; the kindness is real and the routing is the harm
  (thesis intact, now mechanically visible).
- **The disk is the era-1 proto-Assistant.** The Assistant-absent-in-Stage-0/1
  law stands: the disk is not a character, it does not talk back, it is
  *interface as instruction*. But it lets the Assistant lineage start one era
  earlier in *material* form: physical media → installed helper → cloud agent.
  Same eye motif may appear printed on the disk label (forerunner).
- **New prop needs**: floppy/CD-ROM + drive slot interaction (insert = the
  era's defining gesture), booklet, cassette player. The insert action is the
  player's first "consent" beat — you put the disk in yourself. ⚑ tone: the
  kit's content is perpetrator self-presentation → satire-permitted register
  (`operable`), but the hymn/prayer must read as period-real, not mockery of
  faith itself.
- mIRC flow updates: channel entry now motivated (the kit sent you);
  MentorRob's DM arrives *by name* ("you must be the one Pastor M. mentioned").

## 8. ⚑ ERA-1 ENDING — "sent to the camp" (ethics reconciliation required)

Sérgio proposes Era 1 end with the person **taken to a residential
center/camp** — "a bit shocking but culturally important" — as it was the
era's most common escalation.

**Conflict with canon:** ETHICS_AND_CARE hard line = *European grounding:
referral abroad, not a van* — the American kidnap-van trope is excluded.

**Proposed reconciliation (preserves the shock, respects the line):** the
violence is **administrative, not physical**. The machine itself stages it:
- the kit/channel escalates to a **referral packet**: enrollment form printed
  on-screen, parental consent line *already signed*, itinerary to a program
  abroad, suitcase checklist;
- mid-session the screen simply **powers down** — input dead, no melodrama;
- the Era 1→2 update installs "while you were away"; Era 2 boots with the
  machine noting the absence (`profile suspended — enrolled`, date gap);
- the camp itself is **never shown** — the horror is that a bureaucratic
  pipeline you watched assemble itself could remove a teenager from their
  room, and the computer treats it as routine.

The player was the *subject* of the era's targeting; the disappearance is
documented by the machine, not performed as spectacle. ⚑ **Sérgio decides**
whether this staging carries enough of the cultural weight he wants.
