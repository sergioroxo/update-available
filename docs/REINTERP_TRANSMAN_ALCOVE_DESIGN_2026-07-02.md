## 0. The alcove's argument (one paragraph)

He is not who any era's apparatus was built to find. In 1997 and 2003 the tools that exist — the mailed
kit, the accountability app — were built for someone else's shape, and they keep filing him under a label
that isn't his: a girls' circle, a father-wound worksheet that doesn't fit, a "concern note" about a
daughter. The alcove is not about a person being targeted; it's about a person being **misplaced**, over
and over, by systems too confident in their categories to notice the mismatch. Only from the mid-2010s does
a apparatus finally develop a vocabulary built *for* him — and being seen, at last, is worse: not
recruitment, not self-surveillance, but a calm, parent-facing machine that manufactures doubt about him
specifically. **The alcove begins as absence and ends as overinterpretation.**

## 1. The device, not a person (why there's no single aged composite)

The other three identities each anchor one lead era with one composite person. This alcove doesn't have
that luxury and shouldn't manufacture one — a single character aging naturally from a 1997 kid to a present-
day adult would force an implausible throughline onto a real history where the "youth panic" tactic is
specifically a 2010s-and-later phenomenon (per the research: no documented bespoke 1997/2003 device exists
to anchor him to). Instead, the alcove's throughline is an **object**, not a person: a case file / folder
that keeps reappearing, mislabeled, across the first three eras — closed by someone else's hand each time,
never resolved. Only in the present-day alcove does a specific (invented, composite, never named as a
playable character in the enforcement sense) teen's *file* — not the teen himself — become visible, because
that's what the mid-2010s apparatus actually produces: not a person the system talks to, but a profile
built about a child by adults and software.

This also solves the scope problem directly: a recurring **object** motif is cheap to build, reads
instantly in a *light* peripheral alcove (no dialogue tree required), and the "misfiled, again" beat repeats
with variation rather than needing fresh backstory each era.

## 4. What the alcove must support (build requirements, if this thread proceeds)

1. **Stays dim/peripheral by default** in E1–E3, per the spotlight-mechanic rule: idle-alive (a CRT-hum-
   style ambient detail — a slightly shifting folder-corner shadow, a soft idle glow on the stuck card, a
   comment-count ticking up by one occasionally) even when not the lit node, never fully dark.
2. **No reading wall anywhere in E1–E3** — each beat's full text is 1–2 lines, expandable once, then closes.
   E4 is the only alcove allowed a short multi-screen sequence, and even that stays shorter than Maya's.
3. **Never lets the player enforce anything here** — TM3 explicitly withholds moderation tools the player
   has in Vera's own room; the point is that this thread is happening *near* the player's usual verbs, not
   *to* them.
4. **E4's portal must visually rhyme with Settle** (calm-UX, pastel, genuinely well-designed) without being
   literally Maya's OS — a distinct but sibling brand, since the historical grounding is a *family-facing*
   portal, a different audience than Maya's own wellness app.
5. **No new named playable character** — the folder/card/thread/portal carry the whole alcove; the "teen"
   the E4 portal is about is never shown, spoken to, or given a face. This keeps the alcove buildable without
   opening a new backstory the way a fifth full composite would.

## 5. Ethics / gates — do not draft real copy past this sketch until cleared

Per the deep-research brief, this specific content sits inside **all three** of the named hard-gate zones:
butch/trans-masculine overlap (E1 folder), family distancing/rejection framed as "concern" (E3 thread, E4
portal), and detransition-adjacent content functioning as an apparatus tactic (E4 cautionary-clip queue).
**An ethics/trans-reader (ideally trans-masculine specifically) consult is required before any of this is
turned into real copy** — this sketch is geometry/beat-structure only, written to make that consult concrete
to react to, not to pre-empt it.

Two flags for whoever reviews it:
- TM2's dual-label mis-resolution risks reading as "the system tried both boxes and both are equally wrong"
  — reviewer should confirm this lands as *institutional confusion*, not as equivalence between the
  father-wound and emotional-dependency frames themselves (they're each already tagged `[VERIFY SOURCE]`
  elsewhere and shouldn't be re-litigated here).
- E4's cautionary-clip thumbnail must never autoplay real-sounding testimony — per the existing project rule,
  it should read clearly as the *portal's* selected content, not as a person's actual voice.
