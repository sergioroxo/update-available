## A. The ending — point cloud / falling stars / bridge to SurvivingSOGICE

**The note:** "The ending needs the educational panels, that will flow into the survivingsogice project and
point cloud start appearing as an introduction to the network part (figurative introduction to the other
projects). The night lights stars that fall down into the point cloud."

**What this is:** an expansion of the global Close (currently a shell — version history, "Your update has
failed," the Dossier flip, "Restart as you are" — see `pc-simulator-project.md` BUILD STATE). The new idea:
after the educational panels, the screen/room transitions into a **point cloud** — a figurative threshold
into "the network," i.e. a visual bridge from this one fictional piece into the real **SurvivingSOGICE**
research project and its other works. The connecting image: a night sky's stars **falling down and
becoming** the point cloud — individual warm lights condensing into a shared field.

**Cross-ref:** this directly extends an already-flagged pending item — the BUILD STATE "STILL OPEN" note
that the text-review pass should "add a connection to the SurvivingSOGICE project" to the resources
overlay. This note gives that connection a real visual device instead of just a link.

**One thing worth flagging before this goes further:** a "point cloud" of data-points is also, visually,
very close to the piece's own **surveillance/Watcher grammar** (the witness side is explicitly the
hi-def/data/cold register throughout). If the ending's point cloud is meant to read as *warm* — a
constellation of other survivors/other projects, solidarity rather than surveillance — that needs a
deliberate visual differentiation from the Watcher's data aesthetic (color temperature, motion quality,
whatever the visual grammar ends up being), or the final image risks accidentally closing the piece on the
system's register instead of the person's. Worth being explicit about which one it's meant to evoke before
this gets built — that's a Fable-5 dramaturgy question, not just a visual one.

## B. The AI assistant + the pillow → brochure device

**The notes:** "The ai voice Assistant, the pillow slapping." / "Maintain the same exploration we press the
pillow and a brochure appears to explain stuff of the historical elements (we don't need to do it, but it
teaches you about it) (maybe the system tells you to do it, to go grab the pillow)."

**What this is:** a physical comfort object (a pillow — something to hit/hold, ordinary bedroom-side
texture) doubles as an **optional entry point to historical context**: interacting with it surfaces a
brochure/pamphlet with real documentary information, same job as the Dossier, given a physical home in the
room instead of a menu. The twist worth keeping: **the assistant (Lamby/Lambient/Echo lineage) may be the
one who tells the player to go get it** — even the "learn more" gesture is something the apparatus notices
and can nudge you toward, which stays consistent with the "every act is filed" doctrine and is a small,
sharp irony (the system directing you toward information about itself).

**Cross-ref:** this is the same job the Dossier already does (`status: documentary | contested |
speculative`, "returns after the scene, never during it" per the deep-research-validated museum-panel
pairing in `REINTERPRETATION_FOUR_ROOMS_ANALYSIS_2026-07-01.md` §9) — reuse that discipline rather than
inventing new rules for brochure content.

## C. The identity-erasure "collect" mechanic — the one real escalation here

**The notes:** "Playful manipulation of you forcing to change the queer elements — you ended up cleaning
the space of queerness — and it subverts the narrative of what the realities of SOGICE [are]. For example,
see posters and 'collect' them by changing to straight/cisgender version! ... You're having to do identity
erasure exercises. ... Example: a trans flag that starts on the wall and in the process gets in the bin and
then destroyed. ... The system needs to actually demand you because it represents the violence of SOGICE."

**What this is:** the player is invited into what plays, at first touch, like an ordinary completionist
side-activity — find and "fix" the queer-coded objects in a room (posters, a flag) by swapping them to
straight/cisgender versions, one by one, "collecting" the swap. The subversion is the point: the player
discovers, mid-activity or after, that what felt like harmless collecting was **them personally performing
the apparatus's erasure** — the horror the whole piece critiques, now done by the player's own hand instead
of just witnessed.

**This is a genuine escalation from existing canon, worth naming plainly.** `ERA1_LOGIC_v1.md` §6 item 5
already designs a poster-conversion beat — queer posters swap to religious/straight-coded imagery mid-era —
but that design is explicit that **the swap is the system's act, done to the room while the player is
looking at the screen**, with the tone-check note "never queer art destroyed for spectacle." Sérgio's new
notes ask for the opposite: the **player's own click** does the swapping. That's a bigger ethical lift, not
a bigger production lift — it needs two things to land as critique instead of becoming the erasure it's
supposed to indict:

1. **The demand, not just an option** ("the system needs to actually demand you" — Sérgio's own note).
   Consistent with the piece's title thesis ("the demand never changes"), the system should apply real,
   escalating narrative pressure toward doing it — nagging, blocked progress, an assistant line repeated
   with mounting insistence — the same register as Era 1's no-refusal-UI packet beat or Era 4's soft-lock
   friction. It should **never** become an actual inescapable trap: a working Leave/pause stays reachable
   the whole time (Ethics Constraints #4 is non-negotiable) — the coercion is fictional-diegetic, the exit is
   real.
2. **A traceable consequence after**, not just the twist itself. Per the piece's own ledger law ("every
   meaning traceable, every interpretation wrong" — Era 3) and the anti-victim-blaming rule (Ethics
   Constraints #10 — the system pathologizes compliance **and** resistance symmetrically), whatever the
   player did here needs to be reflected back afterward — a Dossier-style beat that recontextualizes the
   tally, and just as importantly, **tracks refusal too** if the player doesn't play along (leaving posters
   queer shouldn't be invisible to the system either — that symmetry is what keeps this from reading as "you
   failed the minigame if you didn't erase enough").

**The trans-flag-in-the-bin beat specifically should sit in `felt` register, not `operable`** — per the
piece's register law, this is the one image in the set that shouldn't carry any of the system's charm; it
should land as loss, plainly, the way the piece's other `felt` beats do.

**Cross-ref:** this also directly extends the already-designed **Straight/Cis Restoration Filter**
(Era-2 funny seed → Era-4 harsh payoff, "the funny→sad arc = the era curve" per BUILD STATE) — this new
mechanic is a plausible way to make that filter's logic **interactive** rather than only something the
player watches happen to someone else's photo.

These don't need Fable 5's creative pass — they're prop-list additions for whichever room-build session
picks up the relevant era.
