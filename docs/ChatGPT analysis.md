## Core argument

The best-supported reading is **not** that lesbians were absent from conversion practices, but that the ex-gay movement made **gay male sexuality the public emblem of “homosexual sin/addiction”**, while women’s same-sex desire was often treated through **gender discipline**: failed femininity, “female masculinity,” emotional dependency, trauma, and restoration to heterosexual womanhood. That asymmetry is well evidenced in scholarship on Exodus/Love in Action and ex-gay discourse, but **actual participation numbers by gender are much less well documented**, especially outside the U.S.

## 1. Gendered visibility: why gay men became the most visible “problem”

### What is solid

The organised ex-gay movement emerged in the U.S. evangelical/therapeutic milieu of the 1970s, especially after the 1973 APA removal of homosexuality from the DSM created space for conservative Christian “counselling” alternatives. Chris Babits shows that early interventions were initially offered by conservative Christian men and that Love in Action testimonies helped construct the “masculine lesbian” as an object of religious/therapeutic intervention. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-intellectual-history/article/specter-of-female-masculinity-how-women-shaped-the-exgay-movement-in-the-1970s-and-1980s/CA2F3513F4D5BD945399D0D4E2310598 "The Specter of Female Masculinity: How Women Shaped the Ex-gay Movement in the 1970s and 1980s | Modern Intellectual History | Cambridge Core")) Robinson and Spivey similarly describe the ex-gay movement as U.S.-originating in the 1970s and later transnational, with religious and psychological narratives merged into reparative/conversion discourse. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0760/4/3/879 "Putting Lesbians in Their Place: Deconstructing Ex-Gay Discourses of Female Homosexuality in a Global Context"))

For gay men, the public movement centred on **masculinity repair**: homosexuality was framed as deficient masculinity, father-wound, failed male bonding, lust, pornography, masturbation, and “sexual addiction” or “struggle.” This is clearest in work on Exodus, New Hope, Love in Action, NARTH/Joseph Nicolosi, and later “same-sex attraction” ministries. Tanya Erzen’s _Straight to Jesus_ is important because it shows how residential ex-gay programmes combined evangelical conversion, psychosexual-development theory, confession, group accountability, and everyday discipline of desire. ([Google Books](https://books.google.de/books?hl=de&id=MfvTWhTg9boC&source=gbs_navlinks_s&utm_source=chatgpt.com "Sexual and Christian Conversions in the Ex-gay Movement")) GPAHE’s mapping of contemporary groups also notes that ex-gay/change efforts include support groups, talk therapy, and “versions of 12-step programs,” which is the later online continuation of this addiction/accountability idiom. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

For lesbians, the emphasis was different. Robinson and Spivey argue that ex-gay discourse constructed lesbianism as sinful and sick in order to control women’s sexuality and enforce rigid gender roles; their analytic frame explicitly draws on Adrienne Rich’s compulsory heterosexuality and Barbara Risman’s gender-structure theory. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0760/4/3/879 "Putting Lesbians in Their Place: Deconstructing Ex-Gay Discourses of Female Homosexuality in a Global Context")) Babits’s 2024 article sharpens this point: women were present as counsellors and clients, but lesbianism was repeatedly explained through **female masculinity**, with women instructed to stop dressing, working, or living in “masculine” ways if they hoped to stop same-sex desire. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-intellectual-history/article/specter-of-female-masculinity-how-women-shaped-the-exgay-movement-in-the-1970s-and-1980s/CA2F3513F4D5BD945399D0D4E2310598 "The Specter of Female Masculinity: How Women Shaped the Ex-gay Movement in the 1970s and 1980s | Modern Intellectual History | Cambridge Core"))

### Why lesbians were comparatively marginalised in visibility

The asymmetry seems to come from four overlapping mechanisms:

**First, patriarchal theology made male sexuality politically central.** Evangelical ex-gay discourse worried intensely about failed male headship, effeminacy, fatherlessness, and male bonding. Gay men could be displayed as proof that “biblical masculinity” could be restored.

**Second, women’s same-sex desire was often made less visible by being translated into gender nonconformity.** Instead of being treated primarily as erotic desire, lesbianism was often reframed as masculinisation, emotional dependency, mother-wound, feminist rebellion, or rejection of male authority. Robinson and Spivey note that ex-gay discourse demanded femininity, submission to male authority, and heterosexuality, while Moberly’s British reparative model described female homosexuality through failed maternal identification and “quasi-masculinity.” ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0760/4/3/879 "Putting Lesbians in Their Place: Deconstructing Ex-Gay Discourses of Female Homosexuality in a Global Context"))

**Third, ex-gay testimony formats privileged men as public proof.** The “I was gay, now I am restored” testimony mapped neatly onto evangelical male leadership, marital restoration, fatherhood, and public ministry. Women’s stories circulated too, but often as cautionary narratives about feminism, masculinity, abuse, or emotional need rather than as the movement’s central public image. Babits cautions that some early Love in Action testimonies should not be read as transparent autobiography, because interviews were guided and produced a therapeutic object — “the masculine female” — for conservative audiences. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-intellectual-history/article/specter-of-female-masculinity-how-women-shaped-the-exgay-movement-in-the-1970s-and-1980s/CA2F3513F4D5BD945399D0D4E2310598 "The Specter of Female Masculinity: How Women Shaped the Ex-gay Movement in the 1970s and 1980s | Modern Intellectual History | Cambridge Core"))

**Fourth, scholarship itself reproduced the imbalance.** Babits explicitly notes that women were prominent in the 1970s and 1980s but had not received extensive scholarly review; Robinson and Spivey also describe female homosexuality as a neglected topic within ex-gay studies. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-intellectual-history/article/specter-of-female-masculinity-how-women-shaped-the-exgay-movement-in-the-1970s-and-1980s/CA2F3513F4D5BD945399D0D4E2310598 "The Specter of Female Masculinity: How Women Shaped the Ex-gay Movement in the 1970s and 1980s | Modern Intellectual History | Cambridge Core"))

**Uncertain / contested:** it is harder to prove that ex-gay ministries always considered lesbians a “lesser” concern numerically. The stronger claim is about **public visibility, leadership, theology, and discursive priority**. Lesbians were not ignored; rather, they were often absorbed into a gender-order project: making women feminine, submissive, heterosexual, and legible within Christian family structures.

## 2. The 2010s pivot: from ministries to platform testimony

### What changed

By the late 2000s and early 2010s, the classic ex-gay brand became reputationally damaged. The APA’s 2009 review concluded that sexual-orientation change efforts were unlikely to work and involved risk of harm. ([APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2009/08/therapeutic?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Insufficient evidence that sexual orientation change efforts ...")) Exodus International shut down in 2013 after Alan Chambers apologised for harm, and several related groups closed in 2014, including Love in Action and Love Won Out. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

But the rhetoric did not disappear. It migrated into **platform-native forms**: YouTube testimonies, influencer confession videos, documentary-style “formerly gay” narratives, app-based ministry, paid social ads, and later “X-LGBT” / “once gay” / detransition testimony networks. GPAHE describes this as continual rebranding under new names and justifications. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

Examples:

- In 2018, reporting on Anchored North described ex-gay videos using queer-coded titles and aesthetics, promoted on Facebook and YouTube, with one Facebook video reaching nearly 6 million viewers. ([Them](https://www.them.us/story/conversion-therapy-social-media "Conversion Therapy Videos Target LGBTQ+ Youth on Facebook and YouTube | Them"))
    
- In 2019, Google removed the Living Hope Ministries app after pressure from LGBTQ groups; Axios reported that the app contained testimonials and sections for men, women, young people, and parents. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2019/03/29/google-pulls-lgbtq-conversion-therapy-app-pressure "Google pulls \"conversion therapy\" app amid pressure from LGBTQ groups"))
    
- In 2021, _Pray Away_ popularised a retrospective documentary form: former Exodus leaders and survivors narrating the rise, power, and harm of the movement. ([Multitude Films](https://www.multitudefilms.com/films/pray-away "Pray Away — Multitude Films"))
    
- In 2020, Teen Vogue covered YouTuber Lohanthony’s “Surviving Sexuality” video as an influencer-era example of same-sex attraction being framed through trauma, addiction, and Christian celibacy rather than a formal “therapy” claim. ([Teen Vogue](https://www.teenvogue.com/story/youtuber-lohanthony-anti-gay-video "YouTuber Lohanthony Seemingly Renounced Same-Sex Attraction in Anti-Gay Video | Teen Vogue"))
    

### How this differed for lesbian/bi women

For lesbian and bi women, the platform shift often intensified older themes: trauma, sexual assault, emotional dependency, rejection of feminism, “masculinity,” and the idea that lesbian identity is a detour from proper womanhood. The Anchored North example is especially relevant because it centred a woman’s “ex-lesbian” testimony and used queer-friendly visual language before pivoting to evangelical anti-gay theology. ([Them](https://www.them.us/story/conversion-therapy-social-media "Conversion Therapy Videos Target LGBTQ+ Youth on Facebook and YouTube | Them"))

In the 2020s, a related but distinct framing appeared around young same-sex-attracted girls: the claim that gender transition is “transing away the gay.” LGB Alliance’s own 2021 consultation response argued that including gender identity in a conversion-therapy ban could, in its view, promote gay conversion by placing young people — “most of whom would have gone on to become LGB” — on puberty blockers and medical pathways. ([LGB Alliance UK - LGB Alliance UK](https://lgballiance.org.uk/lgb-alliances-response-to-the-government-consultation-on-banning-conversion-therapy/ "LGB Alliance’s Response to the Government Consultation on Banning Conversion Therapy - LGB Alliance UK")) Its 2024 blog sharpened this into the phrase “transing the gay away,” claiming that gender-identity conversion-practice bans threaten lesbian, gay, and bisexual teenagers. ([LGB Alliance UK - LGB Alliance UK](https://lgballiance.org.uk/conversion-practices-bill-blog/ "Time to stop “transing the gay away” - LGB Alliance UK"))

**Solid:** this rhetoric exists and is politically influential in UK debate.  
**Less solid:** the empirical claim that “most” gender-questioning young people would otherwise become LGB is heavily contested, and should not be treated as established fact without careful clinical-demographic sourcing.

### Evidence on recommendation / visibility asymmetries

The strongest evidence is not that “the algorithm always radicalises people,” but that **search terms matter**. GPAHE found that searches for “conversion therapy” or “ex-gay therapy” often returned authoritative sources, while euphemistic terms such as “unwanted same-sex attraction” and “reintegrative therapy” more often surfaced providers, pro-conversion material, or weakly moderated content. It found this pattern in English-language searches, including Ireland/UK-linked provider leakage, and noted YouTube frequently returned pro-conversion material. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-ecosystem/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Ecosystem - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

GPAHE also found that once a user lands on a provider, platform recommendations and networked accounts can lead to related providers, hashtags, and off-platform material, although it cautioned that YouTube suggested videos did **not uniformly** create a conversion-therapy rabbit hole. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-ecosystem/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Ecosystem - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

So the careful claim is: **visibility asymmetry is well evidenced at the level of euphemistic search terms, provider networks, and inconsistent moderation; stronger algorithmic-radicalisation claims are more contested and platform-specific.**

## 3. The contemporary anti-trans turn — handled carefully

### What is well evidenced

There is documented overlap between older conversion-practice networks and contemporary anti-trans campaigning. Robinson and Spivey’s 2019 article analyses ex-gay movement discourse on “transgenderism” from the 1970s onward and argues that trans oppression is fed by religious, scientific, and political discourses of the transnational ex-gay movement. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0760/8/6/191 "Ungodly Genders: Deconstructing Ex-Gay Movement Discourses of “Transgenderism” in the US")) GPAHE identifies contemporary networks such as the Alliance for Therapeutic Choice and Scientific Integrity, Core Issues Trust, IFTCC, X-OUT-LOUD, and “Sex Change Regret,” showing how some actors moved from sexual-orientation change rhetoric into gender-identity and detransition campaigning. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

This is particularly relevant in Europe. GPAHE describes Core Issues Trust as a Northern Ireland-based organisation and a major European node; it also identifies the London-based IFTCC as a network opposing conversion-practice bans and providing counsellor networks. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism")) It also names Norway’s Til Helhet as a self-described Christian provider whose purpose was to help LGBTQ people with unwanted sexual orientation or “transsexuality,” linking it to Norwegian debates before the 2023–24 ban. ([GPAHE](https://globalextremism.org/reports/conversion-therapy-online-the-players/ "Conversion Therapy Online: The Players - Global Project Against Hate and Extremism"))

### LGB-identified anti-trans advocacy

Some LGB-identified organisations and figures have contributed to anti-trans advocacy by arguing that gender-affirming care or trans inclusion can itself operate as gay/lesbian conversion. LGB Alliance is the most prominent UK example. Its own consultation response recommended separating sexual-orientation and gender-identity conversion legislation, argued that puberty blockers for minors should be classified as conversion therapy in some circumstances, and framed detransitioners as the “primary victims” of gay conversion therapy in the UK. ([LGB Alliance UK - LGB Alliance UK](https://lgballiance.org.uk/lgb-alliances-response-to-the-government-consultation-on-banning-conversion-therapy/ "LGB Alliance’s Response to the Government Consultation on Banning Conversion Therapy - LGB Alliance UK"))

That does not prove LGB Alliance is a conversion-practice provider. It shows a **rhetorical and policy overlap**: conversion-practice language is redirected against trans healthcare and against trans-inclusive bans. The UK Charity Commission’s 2023 response to the Mermaids v LGB Alliance tribunal also cautioned that public debate includes deeply held views on all sides and that the tribunal did not decide the merits of the wider sex/gender dispute. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/charity-commission-responds-to-mermaidslgb-alliance-tribunal-verdict "Charity Commission responds to Mermaids vs LGB Alliance tribunal outcome - GOV.UK")) The Guardian’s explainer similarly notes the tribunal was decided on legal standing/technical grounds, not a broad ruling on trans rights or LGB rights. ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/law/2023/jul/06/mermaids-v-lgb-alliance-whos-involved-in-tribunal-ruling "Mermaids v LGB Alliance: who was involved in tribunal case? | Law | The Guardian"))

### “Gender-exploratory” / “watchful waiting”: where the line is

This is the most contested part and needs careful language.

A legitimate clinical debate exists about youth gender care: evidence quality, assessment, comorbid mental health needs, neurodevelopmental issues, informed consent, puberty blockers, and long-term outcomes. NHS England’s post-Cass implementation states that the Cass Review was commissioned in 2020, published in April 2024, and calls for safe, holistic care; it also says NHS England restricted puberty blockers for under-18s with gender incongruence/dysphoria because of limited evidence around safety, risks, benefits, and outcomes. ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/children-and-young-peoples-gender-services-implementing-the-cass-review-recommendations/ "NHS England » Children and young people’s gender services: implementing the Cass Review recommendations")) The Royal College of Psychiatrists welcomed a comprehensive, biopsychosocial, person-centred approach and stressed that clinicians should not let personal views shape care. ([www.rcpsych.ac.uk](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/news-and-features/latest-news/detail/2024/04/22/detailed-response-to-the-cass-review%27s-final-report "Detailed response to The Cass Review's Final Report"))

So: **exploration is not inherently conversion therapy.** The UK Memorandum of Understanding defines conversion therapy as practice that assumes some sexual orientations or gender identities are inferior and seeks to change or suppress them; it also says conversion therapy relating to sexual orientation and gender identity is unethical, potentially harmful, and unsupported by evidence. ([BACP](https://www.bacp.co.uk/about-us/advancing-the-profession/influencing-decision-makers/mou/ "Memorandum of understanding on conversion therapy in the UK"))

The critique is narrower: “gender-exploratory” or “watchful waiting” becomes conversion-like when it is **asymmetrical** — for example, when it treats trans identity as a pathology to be explained away, assumes cisgender outcome is preferable, blocks social recognition by default, frames gender diversity as trauma/autism/social contagion, or aims to reduce/suppress non-cis identity. Ashley’s “Interrogating Gender-Exploratory Therapy” is a key critical source; its abstract argues that gender-exploratory therapy discourages affirmation in favour of searching for pathological roots of youths’ trans identities. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018052/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Interrogating Gender-Exploratory Therapy - PMC - NIH")) A peer-reviewed BCMJ article also notes that if an approach cannot be clearly distinguished from conversion therapy as defined in law — especially where one identity is assumed preferable — its benefit and non-maleficence are doubtful. ([BC Medical Journal](https://bcmj.org/premise/gender-affirming-care-youth-separating-evidence-controversy "Gender-affirming care for youth—separating evidence from controversy | British Columbia Medical Journal"))

**Contested:** proponents of exploratory psychotherapy argue that psychotherapy is not conversion therapy and can support autonomy when medical evidence is uncertain. D’Angelo’s 2024/2025 _Journal of Medical Ethics_ article makes that argument explicitly. ([PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/DANSAI-4?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Supporting autonomy in young people with gender dysphoria")) The defensible distinction for your project is: **open-ended, patient-centred exploration is not conversion practice; exploration with a predetermined anti-trans or anti-gay endpoint is.**

## 4. European grounding

### Malta

Malta is the key European legal starting point. Its **Affirmation of Sexual Orientation, Gender Identity and Gender Expression Act** entered into force on 9 December 2016 and remains in force; the legislation is explicitly about sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, and affirmation. ([Legislation Malta](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/567/eng "LEĠIŻLAZZJONI MALTA")) The Council of Europe in 2026 described Malta as Europe’s pioneering example and quoted former equality commissioner Helena Dalli saying the Maltese law did not criminalise belief or legitimate therapeutic support, but set a boundary against denying another person’s identity. ([Portal](https://www.coe.int/en/web/portal/-/council-of-europe-body-calls-for-ban-on-conversion-practices "Council of Europe body calls for ban on conversion practices - Portal"))

### Norway

Norway is highly relevant because it demonstrates a European model centred on **systematic methods**, not a U.S.-style “camp” stereotype. In June 2023, the Norwegian government proposed a new criminal offence targeting systematic methods intended to influence another person’s sexual orientation or gender identity, with different thresholds depending on whether the person is under or over 18, plus a marketing offence. ([Regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-132-l-20222023/id2984891/ "Prop. 132 L (2022–2023) - regjeringen.no")) In December 2023, the Storting adopted the ban in substantially those terms. ([Stortinget](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Saker/Sak/Voteringsoversikt/?dnid=1&p=94762 "Voteringsoversikt - stortinget.no"))

### UK

The UK remains a central unresolved case. The government promised a draft conversion-practices bill in the 2024 King’s Speech and again in the 2026 King’s Speech. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/the-kings-speech-2024 "The King's Speech 2024 - GOV.UK")) In December 2025, the Minister for Equalities told Parliament the government would bring forward a comprehensive trans-inclusive ban “as soon as possible,” while also responding to concerns that clinical trials and the Cass Review should build evidence for youth gender care. ([Hansard](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2025-12-10/debates/99AFB445-09C7-4417-9179-39DAF1C5F40E/ConversionPracticesBan "Conversion Practices Ban - Hansard - UK Parliament")) As of the 13 May 2026 King’s Speech, this was still a **draft** bill commitment, not an enacted UK-wide ban. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/the-kings-speech-2026 "The King's Speech 2026 - GOV.UK"))

### EU / Council of Europe

At EU level, the strongest recent development is the successful European Citizens’ Initiative. The initiative was registered in January 2024 and called for a binding EU legal ban. ([European Citizens' Initiative](https://citizens-initiative.europa.eu/initiatives/details/2024/000001_en?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Ban on conversion practices in the European Union")) In May 2026, the European Commission replied that it would adopt a **Recommendation** in 2027 calling on Member States to ban conversion practices; this is significant but non-binding. ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1070?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Commission replies to the European Citizens Initiative to ban ...")) Separately, in January 2026 the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe voted 71–26 to call on European countries to adopt legislation prohibiting conversion practices, with criminal sanctions and clear definitions. ([Portal](https://www.coe.int/en/web/portal/-/council-of-europe-body-calls-for-ban-on-conversion-practices "Council of Europe body calls for ban on conversion practices - Portal"))
