# 1 · ⚑ THE FIND — ballroom is not a mood for this era. It is the era's argument, exactly inverted.

**The apparatus has done one thing for four eras: it assigns you a category and files it.**
E1 the profile and the intake form · E2 the check-in chips · E3 the correction list and its style
guide · E4 L's spoken caption on every object in Maya's room.

**A ball is also nothing but categories.** *The category is…* — announced out loud, before anyone
walks. It is the most category-saturated room imaginable.

**The difference is the whole piece:**

| | the apparatus | the ball |
|---|---|---|
| who names the category | it does | **it is announced, and you choose which to walk** |
| who judges | the record | **your own people, out loud, in front of you** |
| what the naming produces | a file | **a title, and a place to belong** |
| what happens to it after | it is retained | **it is yours** |

So when L attempts its caption and returns **`NO CATEGORY FOUND`**, the joke is not that there is no
category. **The room is made of nothing but categories.**

### ⚑ CORRECTED 2026-08-06 — and the correction makes the beat better
I originally wrote *"they are simply not the machine's to assign."* The provenance pass
(`docs/research/BALLROOM_PROVENANCE_2026-08-06.md`) says that reading is **a simplification, and it
flattens what scholars treat as the culture's central insight.**

**Community authorship and community judging are real and well supported** — houses and commentators
set the criteria, community panels score them, no outside institution issues the rubric. **But many
of the signature categories are not invented from nothing.** "Realness" categories are skilled
re-performances of categories the outside world *already imposes* on the same people daily — passing,
as a literal safety requirement. Bailey's scholarship and Muñoz's *disidentification* both describe
working **on and against** dominant categorisation from within it, not escaping categorisation.

> **So the accurate line is not "these categories are alien to the machine."**
> **It is: the machine's own vocabulary is being performed by the people it was imposed on — and the
> judging has been taken away from it.**

⚑ **That is a better beat than the one I wrote, and it changes what the categories should be.** My
instruction to invent categories in the piece's own register was half right: they should invent, but
they should **echo the apparatus's own words** — because that is what realness does. Which means the
piece already has them: **the profile, the check-in chips, the correction list, L's captions.** Four
eras of categories imposed on people, walked and judged by the people they were imposed on.

**And `NO CATEGORY FOUND` gets sharper.** The machine is not baffled by alien material. **It
recognises every word and cannot occupy the role.** It is looking at its own vocabulary with the
scoring taken out of its hands.

**Three traps the pass names, all avoidable:** claiming total independence from imposed norms (it
undersells the real point) · treating *Paris Is Burning* as the default reference without bell hooks's
1992 critique, which is the standard citation on exactly this failure mode · crediting "ballroom
culture" in the abstract instead of naming lineage and originators, **which repeats the extraction
pattern rather than correcting it.** And one more: **do not imply a documented link between ballroom
and SOGICE survivors specifically** — it is not in the sources. The supported claim is the broader
one: family and religious rejection → housing precarity → houses as one chosen-family response.

## ⚑ 1.1 · And the rhyme that was already in the repo, unplanned

E3's invented scripture — the book the correction list cites under every rule — is called
**`Household`**. Written two days ago because "household of faith" is period-true movement vocabulary
and carries the family as the unit that does the correcting.

**Ballroom's houses are chosen family that shelter the people their households threw out.**

**Same word. Opposite institution.** The apparatus quotes *Household* at strangers to justify
correcting them; the ball's **house** is where you go when a household is what happened to you.
Nothing needs to point at this. It is one word appearing twice, thirty years apart, meaning opposite
things — and it is already in the data.

## ⚑ 1.2 · Why "no laughter" is right, and what replaces it

The old design said *"many voices at once, laughter bleeding through"* against L's single voice. He
is right to cut it: **generic warmth is not ballroom, and "community = laughter" is the lazy image.**

The true contrast is sharper, and it is a *contrast of the same act*:

> **L narrates you privately. The MC announces you publicly.**

Both are one voice naming a person out loud and never stopping. **The politics are opposite.** L
speaks *about* you, *to you*, in a room with no one else in it, and the naming becomes a record. The
MC speaks *about* you *to the room*, in praise, on your behalf — and the naming becomes a welcome.

**So the era's wound and the era's answer are the same gesture.** L speaks a name that is not hers,
neutrally, as correct data. **The MC speaks the name she chose, loudly, as celebration.** Nothing has
to explain that. It is two scenes with the same verb.

**The texture, then:** the MC's chant and commentary · the beat · the calls · applause on the
landing. **Not laughter. Not chatter. Not ambience.** A ball is loud, structured and *attentive* —
which is exactly the register the piece needs, because the apparatus's version of attention is
surveillance.

## ⚑ 1.3 · What the player DOES — and it is the piece's own mechanic, inverted

E4's verb is *watch*: you have been made obsolete. That is the era's despair, and TRANSCENDANCE has
to be the one thing you can do that is not work — **without becoming a game.** No score, no rhythm
mini-game, no achievement; the frame never plays.

**Proposal: the only thing you do is look. And that is the entire point.**

The piece's single bodily ask has always been **the turn**. At the ball, **everyone else is turning
too — toward one person, on purpose.** You are inside a room where being looked at is the gift.

> **Surveillance and a ball are the same act: a room full of attention pointed at one person.**
> The difference is consent, and who is doing the looking, and whether they are cheering.

The piece spends four eras teaching you that being watched is what happens to you. Then it puts you
in a room where being watched is what you came for, **and asks you to do the same thing with your
body that you have done all along.** No new mechanic. No instruction. That is the strongest use of
the turn the work has found.

**And nothing asks you to leave.** You stay as long as you like.

## 1.4 · TransJesus is the bridge, and the canon already says how

> *TransJesus is respite, but it is also evidence… the corruption should read as system violence /

That reconciles perfectly with the ball: **the ball is the room; the stream is how the apparatus

# 2 · ⚑ THE AI PHOTO EDITOR — and it should not be an app

Sérgio: *"don't forget about the AI editor of the photos."* Canon: E3's presets applied by hand to
one person become E4's *"image changer, trans→cis, gay→straight,"* applied to anyone, instantly, by
nobody. Ethics #13 already pins the claim: **the "your straight version" filter stays `speculative`
`[DO NOT CITE YET]`.**

**The mistake would be building it as a tool Maya opens.** An editor you choose to use is a choice,
and E4's thesis is that the choice is gone.

## The design: it is a *memories* feature
The system resurfaces her own photographs. *Two years ago today.* And the photo has been **enhanced**
— nobody asked, nothing announced it, and there is no before unless you go looking.

**⚑ The cruelty is that it is a good photo.** Well lit. Flattering. Cleaner than the original. The
system is *pleased*, and it thinks it did her a favour. It is not vandalism; it is a service.

- **Undo exists and works** — the dismissal law holds, as it has since E1. And the next memory is
  already enhanced. *Nothing you decline is ever un-offered.*
- **No camera, no file input, ever.** Same law as the Restoration Filter: every photograph is a
  pre-authored sprite and the piece never requests a permission.
- **It rhymes with the deadname beat exactly:** the archive of you, corrected. One in pictures, one
  in speech, neither asking, both filed as accurate.

## ⚑ And here is where it meets the ball
**The ball photographs cannot be enhanced.** L tries — the same automatic pass it ran on everything
else — and returns them **unchanged**, with something bland: *no enhancement available.*

Not because it refuses. **Because it cannot tell what it is looking at.**
`NO CATEGORY FOUND`, in the image pipeline, silently.

**Two mechanisms, one inversion, no commentary.** The editor and the respite are the same system
succeeding and failing at the same task, twenty minutes apart.

# 3 · THE OTHER IDEAS THE SEARCH TURNED UP

**3.1 · ⚑ The ball is the only room in the piece with no record.** Everything else files: the cork
board, the dashboard, the feed, the ledger, the witness wall. **Nothing about the ball enters the
record** — same doctrine as Tape C and Malta: *the apparatus did not ask for this.* A player who
turns to the witness wall afterwards finds it blank for the first time in thirty years. Free to
build, and it is the thread's quietest payoff.

**3.2 · Silence is L's raw material.** The design already says *"the quiet is what L fills."* At the
ball there is no quiet at all — so **L has nothing to do, and stops talking.** Not silenced,
not defeated: simply unnecessary. The only minutes in the era without its voice.

**3.3 · The room's captions run out.** L captions every object in Maya's room, gently and
diagnostically. Bring one ball object home — a trophy, a number, a fan — and **L captions it wrong,
twice, then offers a third caption, then stops.** The instrument visibly reaching.

**3.4 · The export, staged rather than explained** (from the Byline source): a webinar Maya is
recommended is *"available in your region"* — a phrase that is only ever said by something that
checked. **One line does the whole transnational argument.**

**4.2 · ⚑ THE NAME — the high-value, high-risk one, and it is yours to decide.**
The name/file thread already reads: E1 *"they already know your name"* → E2 `subject migrated — file
retained` → E4 *"the deadname the record won't release."*

**The piece takes exactly one typed input in its entire runtime: the player's name, in 1997.** It
lives in the in-memory ledger and is wiped on exit.

**So the record could still have it in 2026 — and L could speak it.**

That closes the piece's central thread perfectly and it is the most dangerous thing in the work:
- it makes the player the subject of a deadnaming beat, in a headset, with a name they chose;
- Maya is a character, not the player — so the beat has to be built so the *record* is what carries
  the name forward, not a claim about who the player is;
- **it requires the trans reader pass as a gate**, an advisory, and an opt-out that keeps the meaning.

**My honest read: it is the strongest ending the name thread can have, and I would not build it
without you saying yes in writing.** The safe alternative is that the record retains *Daniel's* 1997
file and reads it back — same argument, no player implicated, most of the force.

**4.3 · E3, small:** the correction list's `Household` should never be glossed. If a player reaches
the ball's houses without ever being told they are the same word, the piece has done its job.

# 5 · ⚑ ETHICS — ballroom specifically, and it needs naming before a line is written

**Ballroom culture is Black and Latinx queer and trans culture**, built in Harlem by people who were
being killed for it, and it is not a general-purpose symbol of queer joy. A Norwegian university
project can pay homage to it. It has to do so as **credit, not extraction**:

1. **Invented houses, invented names, no likenesses** — the same law the rest of the piece obeys, and
   it matters more here, not less.
2. **Credit it explicitly** in the attributions and on a Dossier card: what ballroom is, whose it is,
   and that this is homage. **The Close is a room full of sources; this belongs among them.**
3. **⚑ Ethics #16 already requires a reader protocol for TransJesus content.** My recommendation:
   that reader should include **someone from ballroom or Black queer community specifically** — a
   general queer reader pass is not the same competence, and this is the one scene where getting the
   texture wrong would read as costume.
4. **No AAVE or ballroom vernacular written by me.** The MC's language is the most specific writing in
   the piece and the least mine to draft. ⚑ **This is the one place I would not follow the
   drafting-then-reviewing norm** — I can build the scene, the sound design, the turn, the captions
   and the `NO CATEGORY FOUND`; **the MC's actual words should come from you or from the reader, not
   from a placeholder of mine to be corrected.**

**None of this is a blocker.** It is the difference between an homage and a borrowing, and it is
cheaper to decide now than after fifty lines exist.
