## CONTEXT (short)
A university research-creative project (SurvivingSOGICE, University of Bergen) is preparing to engage a
paid trans-masculine reader/consultant to review design material before it's built — material depicting
family rejection, institutional misgendering, and detransition used as a political tactic. We already have
a ranked list of sourcing routes (GATE Trans Consultants roster, Lex Academic, Transforming Film, Rowan
Pierce, Gendered Intelligence, Salt & Sage Books). What we don't have: a real understanding of how this
kind of engagement actually works once contact is made.

## THE SINGLE QUESTION
**How does a paid sensitivity/cultural-consultant engagement for research-creative work actually function
in practice, end to end?** Answer with as much concrete, sourced detail as you can find — treat this as
the deliverable, not a warm-up to something else. Cover, in whatever order the evidence supports:

- **First contact and scoping**: what does a client (here, a university research project) typically send a
  prospective consultant before terms are agreed — a synopsis, full script, specific scenes? How is scope
  usually defined (a fixed number of pages/scenes, a time budget, an ongoing relationship)?
- **Compensation**: what are documented rate ranges or rate structures (per-hour, per-page, flat project
  fee, day rate) — flag clearly which region/field any figure comes from, since US publishing norms may
  not transfer to European academic/interactive-media work. Is there any documented Nordic/European rate
  data specifically, even approximate?
- **What the consultant actually delivers**: margin notes, a written report, a call/discussion, a
  pass/fail verdict, iterative back-and-forth on revisions — what's typical, and does it vary by whether
  the client is publishing, games/interactive, film, or academic?
- **Creative control**: how much authority does a consultant typically have — advisory-only (the creator
  decides what to change), or something closer to approval/veto over specific content? Is this usually
  negotiated explicitly in advance, and if so how?
- **Distress/duty-of-care for the reader**: since this material specifically covers family rejection and
  detransition-as-tactic, is there documented practice for protecting a reader's wellbeing (advance content
  warnings, right to decline specific sections, no penalty for stepping back)?
- **Written agreements**: what does a typical scope-of-work or consent document cover for this kind of
  engagement? Cite academic-ethics-board guidance on community/cultural consultation for trauma-adjacent
  research if it exists, not just industry/publishing norms — this project sits in a university research
  context, not a trade-publishing one.
- **Timeline**: how long does this kind of engagement typically take, from first contact to a usable
  verdict — days, weeks, months?

## OUTPUT FORMAT
One continuous, thorough answer covering the above — status-tag each claim
(`documentary | contested | speculative`) and cite sources. End with a short practical checklist: what
this project should prepare BEFORE first contacting a route from the ranked list, based on what you found.
