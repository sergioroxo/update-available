## 0. What a provotype is (Sérgio's framework, as given)

A **provotype** = **provocation** + **prototype**: a provocative artifact meant to spark discussion, not
move toward final production (Casnati et al., 2024). Playful, experimental, exploring a topic past its
usual limits, with no aim at a "right answer" (To et al., 2022). The lineage: **Critical Play** (Flanagan,
2009), **Persuasive Games / Procedural Rhetoric** (Bogost, 2007), **Critical and Speculative Design**
(Malpass, 2013; Dunne & Raby, 2013). Specific mechanic-level precedent: **"I Lose, Therefore I Think"**
(Lee, 2003) and Bogost's **Rhetoric of Failure** — a game's *designed impossibility of winning* is itself
the argument. Here, applied directly: **you cannot win a provotype about changing someone's sexual
orientation or gender identity, because you can't do that in reality either — the unwinnability is the
demonstration, not a difficulty setting.** A further layer Sérgio names: generative AI's own permissiveness
in producing SOGICE-adjacent content is itself a documented research finding, and using generative AI in
production is therefore already a small act of Speculative Design, not just a production shortcut.

## 1. Why this is being introduced now

Two pressures converge:

- **The witness panel is dissolving** (per the four-rooms redesign, the turn-around gesture is folding into
  an ambient reactive system-panel). That removes one of the piece's clearest devices for making an
  uninformed audience *feel* the weight of what's happening — a real risk given most players don't know
  what SOGICE is.
- **The museum-panel/didactic register was already flagged as the single riskiest move in the whole
  redesign** (`REINTERPRETATION_FOUR_ROOMS_ANALYSIS_2026-07-01.md` §7) — leaning further into explanatory
  text risks exactly the "script teaching what mechanics should" problem this whole reinterpretation exists
  to fix.

Provotypes answer both at once: small, pervasive, **actually interactive** vignettes, grounded in real
documented technique, that teach through *doing* rather than reading — while being explicitly light and
controlled (short, optional-to-find, not a full arc) so they don't reintroduce the backstory-load problem
either. This is a genuine "revert a bit of the gameplay" move, and a correct one.

## 2. The pillow, re-understood

What was drafted in the prior notes doc as a generic comfort-object → brochure trigger is now something
more specific and stronger: a **provotype built on a real, documented technique** — hold a racket overhead,
address "Dad" on a sustained exhale, strike the pillow, repeat "until deeper feelings/thoughts emerge." This
is Era-2 material specifically (the father-wound frame already anchors "Daniel," the ex-gay man,
1997→2003's self-surveillance era) — not a floating prop usable in any room.

**Two hard rails already apply and must hold here without exception:**
- The **named practitioner stays dossier-only** — citable, `[VERIFY SOURCE]`/status-tagged, never a
  playable character or a likeness in the scene. The player enacts *the technique*, never meets *the man*.
- The **shown reference photo is real, copyrighted documentary photography** (credited to a named
  photographer, depicting a real demonstrated method) — it is reference material for understanding the
  pose and motion, not an asset. Any in-game version must be an invented, stylized rendering (consistent
  with the piece's whole-project low-poly/flat-shaded art doctrine), never a reproduction of this image or
  anything that reads as documentary footage of a real person.

**What this does to the prior "pillow → brochure" idea:** it isn't deleted, it's upgraded and re-sequenced.
The provotype (the short unwinnable exercise itself) comes first; the brochure/documentary citation becomes
the **after-panel** — same "short frame → scene → short debrief" pairing already validated for this
project's museum-panel question (§9 of the four-rooms analysis doc). The provotype does the *feeling*; the
brochure afterward does the *sourcing*.

## 3. Open framing question — "played as the system"

Sérgio's note: "frame them as a connection with the narrative as when we play as the system." Two readings
exist in the piece's own precedent, and this provotype needs to pick one deliberately rather than default
into it:

- **(a) Self-inflicted, under system direction** — the player *is* the person the technique is prescribed
  to, performing it on their own instruction from the apparatus (Era 2's whole register — self-policing,
  "Daniel" doing this to himself because the system told him to). Keeps the player in the sympathetic seat.
- **(b) Operating the tool on someone else** — the player enacts the technique *at* a composite character,
  the way Era 3's triage mechanic makes the player *do* the sorting to someone else. Implicates the player
  more directly, echoes "you polish another woman with your own hands."

Both are already precedented elsewhere in the piece; neither is obviously correct for *this* provotype.
This is a question for Fable to reason through with actual dramaturgical weight, not resolve by default —
see the companion prompt, ask 3.

## 4. Rhetoric-of-failure, reconciled with the hard rails

The theory calls for **designed unwinnability as the argument**. The project's hard rails ban score/streak/
timer/win-or-lose UI. These are not actually in tension — **Era 3 already proved the pattern**: the
"unflag your own card" dead-toggle (Three-Era Roadmap **A7**) is a button that visibly refuses to work, no
fail-buzzer, no penalty framing — the refusal itself is the narrative point. The pillow provotype should use
the same house style: the player can perform the exercise correctly, exactly as instructed, as many times as
they like — nothing resolves, nothing changes, no "you lose" state ever appears. The absence of resolution
*is* delivered as the point, not as a punishment.

## 5. Scope note — this does not generalize for free

Because the pillow provotype's grounding is specifically gay-men/father-wound material, it cannot be
reskinned into the lesbian, trans-man, or 1997-teen contexts without an equivalent real documented technique
for each. This is exactly the research Sérgio is asking for (see the companion ChatGPT prompt) — "pervasive"
means *several* provotypes, each separately grounded, not one mechanic copy-pasted across rooms.

## 7. Correction to §4 — what the "impossibility of winning" actually demonstrates

Sérgio's refinement (2026-07-02): the unwinnability isn't only "the technique's premise is false" — it's
that **even played as the enforcer, the queer/trans side always wins in the end**, the same way the piece's
own ending already says it: **"you are never the problem."** This is the glitch doctrine (the system failing
to overwrite a person) *as a mechanic*, not a separate idea — a provotype is a small, playable enactment of
the same failure the whole piece is built on. Every provotype's design should be read against that standard
first, before any technique-specific framing.

## 8. Provotypes are an umbrella category, not a new isolated feature

Correction (2026-07-02): provotypes are not exclusively the *new* technique-grounded vignettes drafted here.
**ZAP! already qualifies** — a short, self-contained, playfully-framed interactive piece (listen to the
jingle, play the zapper) that sits pervasively in the world rather than carrying the main arc. This reframes
the design task: alongside authoring new provotypes, an audit of what's already built should identify which
existing pieces already function as provotypes (and could be sharpened toward the failure-as-argument
structure) versus which are genuinely new. This affects how the implementation plan (see the companion
master-plan Fable prompt) should be scoped — it is partly new content, partly reclassification.
