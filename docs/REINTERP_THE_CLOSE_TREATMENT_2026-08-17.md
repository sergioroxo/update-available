# THE CLOSE — a treatment
**⚑ PLACEHOLDER-draft, Claude, for Sérgio to react to and rewrite.** Written because the Close has
never been specced, it is the last thing a visitor sees, and *a piece that stops instead of ending
will sink an exhibition no matter how good its middle is.* Every line below is a proposal.

## What exists, and what the piece has already promised
`close_network.json` holds **23 labels** and nothing else; `enterClose()` disables the rooms and shows
a procedural constellation. Meanwhile `s4_offers.json`'s finale doc lists what it deliberately did NOT
spend, because all of it is the Close's: **the survivors · `Your update has failed.` · the dossier
reframe · `Restart as you are.`** Four debts, and the ending is where they come due.

---

# 1 · THE ONE IDEA — the title has never been said, and it is not a lament
For three eras the machine has been confident, competent and gentle, and it has updated on schedule
every time. The piece is named after an error message that **has not appeared once.**

> ## `Your update has failed.`
> **It should arrive in the machine's own flat administrative voice — and it is the happiest sentence
> in the piece.** Not a lament. A *report of defeat*, filed by the thing that was defeating you.

⚑ **That is the whole reversal and the Close only has to land it.** The title reads as despair for
ninety minutes and then, in one beat, reads as survival — the same words, re-pointed by everything
that came before. Nothing needs to explain it. **Do not explain it.**

The glitch doctrine already established this: *the system cannot overwrite a person.* Era 1's diary
could not be deleted. The Close is that same failure, stated once, at the scale of the whole work.

# 2 · THE IMAGE — the building, from outside, in the grammar the piece already has
**Do not invent a second visual idea for the ending.** `REINTERP_THE_BUILDING` and the finale's
cyclorama already give the piece one grammar for *many rooms*: **vertical lit slots in a dark field,
countless rooms seen edge-on.** The Close is that image, pulled back far enough to see it whole.

You have been in four rooms. **The Close is where you learn there were always thousands**, and that
the four were never special — only the ones you happened to sit in.

## ⚑ 3 · THE ETHICS, RENDERED AS A VISUAL RULE — this is the part I would fight for
The piece's aesthetic law is *the witness side is the sharp side; surveillance is high-definition,
life is soft.* The Close should obey it literally:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The apparatus's own nodes** — the instruments, the campaigns, the curricula, the sources | **sharp, lit, labelled, readable.** They are documented, and the dossier names them |
| **The rooms where people are** | **soft, warm, unlabelled — and they cannot be read.** No names. No stories. No count |

**The system documents people. The piece refuses to.** ⚑ That refusal, made visible at the exact
moment the work could most easily cash in its subject for pathos, *is* the dossier reframe the finale
promised — and it argues the ethics position instead of asserting it in a caption.

The lines between nodes are the network: how a 1997 questionnaire reaches a 2016 moderation queue
reaches a 2026 model. **The player can trace the apparatus end to end and cannot trace a single
person.** That asymmetry is the piece's thesis in one picture.

# 4 · THE SURVIVORS — the debt I am least sure how to pay, and would not pay with text
The finale reserved "the survivors" for here. ⚑ **My strong recommendation: not a memorial, not a
count, and not a line of prose.** This work's law is *no verbatim survivor testimony*, and a
respectful paragraph at the end would be the one moment the piece speaks *for* people instead of
about a system.

**Proposal: the soft rooms are simply, visibly, still lit.** The apparatus nodes can be traced,
counted and read; the warm ones cannot — and there are more of them than of anything else on screen.
**Survival rendered as unreadable, numerous and on.** *(Sérgio: this is the beat I most want you to
overrule if you have a better answer. It is the one place a draft feels presumptuous.)*

# 5 · THE LAST PRESS — the person's restart, not the system's
`updates.json`'s `close` key is already built and already correct: no terms, no changelog, no
progress bar — **bare, in a piece where every previous transition made you agree to something.**

> ## `Restart as you are.`
> The system's own restart vocabulary, turned against the premise it spent thirty years selling.
> **Not corrected. Not restored. As you are.**

⚑ **And the button must not restart the piece.** Every other update in the work took control of you at
the moment you pressed it. This one should give it back: the constellation holds, the room does not
return, and nothing further is asked. **A loop back to 1997 would say the machine won.**

*(Practically, for an exhibition, the reset to an attract state has to happen — but it should happen
on IDLE, invisibly, after the visitor has left. The visitor's own last act must be an ending.)*

---

# What this costs to build
Most of it exists. `pointCloud.ts` already draws a node graph and already reads
`close_network.json`; the cyclorama grammar is built for the finale; the `close` update is built.
**The work is: split the node set into apparatus/person tiers with two visual treatments, author the
three lines above, and make the last press terminal rather than looping.**

⚑ **Nothing here needs new technology, and that is deliberate** — the ending should be the cheapest
thing in the piece to build and the most expensive to have gotten wrong.
