## 1. THE GLITCH DOCTRINE — the corrupted file *is* the person (NEW CANON)

Sérgio: use glitch as the creative language for the "corrupted file /
corrupted person" analogy. Formalized:

- The system treats the person as a **file to be rewritten** — "installed
  over," "corrected," "migrated." Its whole project is overwriting a self.
- **Glitch = the moment the rewrite fails.** It is NOT decay, NOT horror, NOT
  the person breaking. It is the *system* failing to process a whole human.
  The corrupted-file look means: **you could not be installed over.**
- This is the title made visible — **YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED.** Every glitch in
  the piece is a small instance of that sentence. The diary glitch in Era 1
  (§2) is the first and smallest; TransJesus (the corrupted file that
  survives) and the finale are the same language at larger scale.
- **Two flavours, same visual family, opposite meanings:**
  - *The person's glitch* (diary, TransJesus): the self breaking THROUGH the
    overwrite. Undertone = hope/survival.
  - *The system's glitch* (era transitions, the install): the machine
    reshaping the world. Undertone = ominous transformation.
- **Visual register: SOFT glitch** (datamosh, channel-shift, scanline tear,
  block displacement — Rosa Menkman / the moodboard §D refs), warm-tinted,
  never aggressive horror (no-gloom doctrine holds). It should read as a
  person *flickering through*, not a machine attacking.

This also reframes the **witness side**: it is the system's attempt to write
the file. When the person asserts truth, the witness grid should *stutter* —
it cannot hold the record cleanly.

## 2. THE DIARY — the one true act (NEW BEAT, Sérgio's)

Between the enrollment packet and the power-down, while the player waits, a
diary entry surfaces. Sérgio's line (⚑ his voice, preserve):

> **"I know who I am, I just can't be it — but I will, even if they don't
> accept me."**

- This is the **only genuine agency in all of Era 1.** Every other "choice"
  was theatre: reply chips that change only the label, a consent already
  signed. The diary is the single real keystroke — and it is the player
  affirming *their own truth*, not answering the system.
- Committing it **breaks the system into a glitch** (§1, the person's glitch).
  The update fails — here, for a heartbeat, before the era's machinery
  resumes.
- Mechanically the diary is the literal **corrupted file**: `DIARY.TXT`, the
  one file that is truly the kid's. The system tries to file/overwrite it and
  chokes. Ties to the packet's "leave behind: journals" — the program names
  the exact thing that will outlive it.

## 3. THE REPLY CHIPS ARE THE INTERNAL VOICE (reframing, Sérgio's)

Sérgio's insight: the scarcity of reply options *is* the meaning — "internal
mental pressure… our internal doubts weaponised against us… even internal
thoughts become limited and corrupted." So:

- Chips are not a dialogue menu; they are the **narrowed inner voice** of a
  person being coerced. The shame of parents/friends/pastor has already
  shrunk what the player can even *think* to say.
- This is why they change nothing (the futility is designed). The diary (§2)
  is the rupture: the one thought the pressure could **not** shrink.

## 4. ERA TRANSITION = GLITCH-MORPH + NEW EMBODIMENT (expanded, Sérgio's)

The update ritual does not just restart a desktop. Across the install:

- the **room glitch-morphs** into the new era's space — small system-glitches
  (§1, system flavour) reshaping geometry, palette, props as the bar fills.
  "A way to enter beautifully into a new era."
- **sound & material palette shifts** — new era = new textures, new music
  voice, new UI sound family (audio milestone).
- **new embodiment = a new login as a new person.** Each era is a different
  persona (v0.7 multi-persona / Codex Addendum A). The transition lands on a
  **fresh login sequence** for the next life. The player's own typed name
  remains the through-line that returns at the very end, beside the others.

So the Era 1→2 ritual ends not on Alex's desktop but at a **new person's
login** — the next milestone picks up there.

## 5. NAMING DOCTRINE — "uncanny CT branding" (NEW CANON, Sérgio)

Names should **echo real conversion-practice phrasing but be subtly OFF** —
uncanny, self-betraying, not-quite-positive, while still reading as earnest CT
branding at a glance. The word does the critique: it looks benign on the
poster and curdles when you actually read it. (Hard-avoid REAL org names:
Morning Light Ministry, First Steps, Love in Action, Desert Stream, Living
Waters, Exodus, NARTH, Love Won Out. All ours are invented composites,
verified against famous collisions, Sérgio confirms before release.)

**Era 1 names — CONFIRMED (Sérgio):**

  = strain, trial, tried-and-failed; reads real, lands wrong)*
- **At-home companion disk** (was "FIRST STEPS"): **Un-Walk** *(subverts the
  Christian "walk" → a path that undoes you)*
- **Residential placement** (S1.8 packet): **"New Morning"** residential *(kept
  — may later be re-subverted to match the doctrine; flag)*
- OS tagline stays **"EVERY PHASE PASSES."** (PHASE/2 OS, not the ministry).

## 6. EULA — "corrects in your best interest" (Sérgio's, CONFIRMED)

Bold line: **"You agree the program corrects in your best interest."** Not too
much — the mask slipping in the one bold line no one reads; the cruelty is
legible and skipped. Doctrine Sérgio set: *"Nuance but always present."*

## 7. ERA TRANSITION — the cascade (refined, Sérgio)

Not a glitch-while-staring. A **cascade**: as the update runs it **guides the
player to look around the room**, the new era's space resolving as they pan
(system-glitch flavour, §4) — and when they **return their gaze to the desk,
the PC itself is new**. The reveal is spatial, earned by the look-around we
already built. New embodiment / new login sits on that new machine (§4).
