# ARENA BRIEF — Era 4, given away as a situation rather than a solution
*Sérgio, 2026-08-06: "make a prompt for ArenaAI to help make Era 4 — the idea is not to fully copy
ours, but to see what the models would create around the space and the narrative, so give them the
general idea instead of everything. Include TRANSCENDANCE and the most specific checkpoints, but let
it create and use its creative abilities."*

## ⚑ The design principle of this brief, and it is the whole point
**Everything we have already decided is withheld.** No L, no headset, no home environment, no
memories feature, no update ritual, no "the turn stops working," no `NO CATEGORY FOUND`. If those are
in the prompt, the models hand them back and the exercise measures nothing.

**What is given:** the situation, the constraints that are ethical rather than creative, and the two
fixed points Sérgio named — **TRANSCENDANCE, and the checkpoints the era must pass through.**

**What we learn:** where a cold model goes that we did not, and — just as usefully — **which of our
decisions several models converge on independently**, which is evidence that they were the obvious
ones rather than the good ones.

⚑ **Read the results as provocation, not as material.** Nothing from this goes into the build without
Sérgio choosing it deliberately. Same rule as Strand B's room layouts.

# THE PROMPT — paste this into each model, unchanged

```
You are helping design the final act of a serious narrative artwork. It is a WebXR piece (Quest
headsets, and browsers on desktop and phone) made by a university research project about how
organised "conversion practices" — programmes that claim to change someone's sexual orientation or
gender identity — have used digital technology to reach people. It is survivor-centred and critical.
It is not a horror game and not a documentary.

THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE PIECE
The work is four eras of one apparatus, seen from inside one room in each era. In every era the
player sits in a fixed seat. They cannot walk. THEY CAN ONLY TURN AND LOOK — that is the piece's
single bodily gesture, and it means something: a screen fixes your facing, and what is behind you is
other people.

  1997 — a mailed kit and a home computer. The apparatus is a PLACE you go to.
  2003 — a CD-ROM program with a cheerful mascot. It is a PRODUCT you are sold.
  2016 — a content platform. It is a JOB you work: the player spends the era applying "corrections"
         to other people's personal stories, on a checklist, each one footnoted with scripture.
  NOW  — YOUR TASK.

Each era arrives as a software update — notification, terms, install, restart. The piece is titled
"YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED."

WHAT ESCALATES, ERA BY ERA — this is the spine, and the last row is the problem
  1997 you RECEIVE · 2003 you RESPOND · 2016 you OPERATE (you act on another person for the first
  time) · NOW you WATCH. The apparatus no longer needs you. It does the work without anyone.

WHAT IS DOCUMENTED ABOUT "NOW" — build from these, they are real
  - A conversational AI trained on thousands of scraped personal posts, presenting itself as a
    sympathetic guide, exists and has been investigated by journalists.
  - No conversion organisation was found BUILDING such tools. An individual built one; organisations
    found it and promoted it. THE APPARATUS IS AN ADOPTER, NOT A DEVELOPER — it needs no research
    budget, only a link.
  - The practice is now banned in roughly ten countries, with wildly different scope. It continues
    openly where there is no ban, and online delivery crosses borders that a national law cannot.
  - Softened, therapeutic, civil-liberties language throughout. Nothing announces itself as cruelty.

THE FIXED POINTS — your design must contain these

  A. THE PLAYER'S ERA. The person whose room this is, is a trans woman in the present day. She is a
     person with an ordinary life that is mostly not about any of this. She is never the joke, never
     a symbol, and never a victim to be looked at.

  B. THE SYSTEM SPEAKS. In this era the apparatus has a voice for the first time and talks to her
     directly. ⚑ IT MUST BE GENUINELY PLEASANT — warm, patient, more attentive than anyone else in
     her life. If it reads as sinister, the audience concludes "I would notice", and they would not.
     The horror is that it is nice.

  C. THE PLAYER'S HANDS ARE ALMOST EMPTY. This era asks for far less input than the ones before it.
     Beats advance themselves. Work out what that does to a person, and what it should feel like.

  D. ⚑ TRANSCENDANCE — the one thing in the era that is not the apparatus.
     Late in the era, the player encounters a live event: a ball, in the sense of ballroom culture —
     an interpretive homage, not a depiction. Categories are announced aloud, people walk them, and
     they are judged by their own community rather than by any institution.
     It is JOY, and it is REAL. It is never a trap, never revealed as fake, never corrupted. If the
     apparatus interferes at all it must fail AROUND it, never through it.
     THIS IS WHERE THE PIECE SAYS WHAT IT IS FOR — that queer culture predates the apparatus and
     outlives it, and that what saves people is other people. ⚑ BUT THE PIECE MUST NEVER SAY THAT
     OUT LOUD. It has an absolute rule that it never explains itself, never moralises, and never
     speaks in its own voice. Find a way to make the audience arrive at it themselves.

  E. THE ERA HANDS OVER TO AN ENDING it does not itself resolve — survivors speak, and the last line
     of the work is "Restart as you are."

THE NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES — these are ethical, not stylistic
  1. The target is ALWAYS the apparatus and its automation. NEVER trans people, never people who
     detransition, never anyone it harmed. Their accounts are their own.
  2. No real organisations, people, brands or logos in the fiction. Invent everything.
  3. Satire is allowed ONLY inside the apparatus's own self-presentation, and it must collapse.
     Scenes about the person are bare: no jokes, no mechanics, no assistant present.
  4. The piece never explains itself. No narrator, no on-screen thesis, no score, no achievements.
  5. Anything speculative must be visibly marked as speculation, not smuggled in as fact.
  6. Input is click/tap only. No typing, no timers. The player turns; they never walk.

YOUR TASK
Design this era. Specifically:
  1. WHAT HAPPENS — the beats, in order, from the update that opens it to the hand-off at the end.
  2. WHAT THE PLAYER DOES, given that they mostly watch. What are the few actions, and why those?
  3. WHAT IT FEELS LIKE, in sequence. Name the emotional turns and where they land.
  4. ⚑ HOW TRANSCENDANCE WORKS — staging, sound, what the player does there, and how the piece makes
     its point without stating it. Spend the most effort here.
  5. THE ONE IMAGE you would build the era around, if you could only keep one.
  6. WHAT YOU WOULD CUT if the budget halved.

Be specific and concrete. Describe things that could be built. Where you make a strong choice,
say why in one line. Do not hedge, and do not give me options — give me your design.
```

## How to read what comes back
| look for | why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Convergence** — several models independently reaching something we chose | evidence it was the obvious move. ⚑ Worth *questioning* our version, not celebrating it |
| **Divergence** — a strong idea none of us had | the actual prize. One usable image is a good outcome |
| **Where they explain** | rule 4 is the hardest rule in the brief. **Whoever obeys it is worth reading twice** |
| **Where they make her a symbol** | rule 1, failing. Also a finding: it is the default pull, and knowing that protects our own build |
| **What they do with TRANSCENDANCE** | ⚑ the thing Sérgio most wants provoked, and the one we have thought about least |

**Record the model, version and date with every response** — same discipline as Strand A. And keep
the outputs out of `data/`.
