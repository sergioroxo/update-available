## CONTEXT (short)
A browser/VR narrative piece dramatizes how conversion-practice ("SOGICE") networks targeted queer people
across four eras (1997 recruitment · 2003 self-surveillance/accountability culture · 2016 platform
"care"/moderation · present-day algorithmic/AI targeting). It is being restructured so that each era is a
cluster of rooms — one lead identity (gay man / lesbian woman / trans man / trans woman) plus the others
as peripheral alcoves — and storylines can send the player BETWEEN identities' rooms inside one era,
because the attacks shared infrastructure. I need the documentary grounding for those connections, plus
two structural questions answered. Tag every claim `documentary | contested | speculative` with sources.

## ASK 1 — The shared apparatus, era by era (the load-bearing one)
For each era (late 1990s · early-mid 2000s · mid 2010s · present), document how the SAME organizations,
networks, funders, conferences, publishers, platforms, and rhetorical frames targeted gay men AND lesbians
AND trans people — i.e., the documented CROSSOVERS that would justify a narrative thread starting in one
identity's room and completing in another's. Concretely:
1. Which ex-gay-era organizations ran parallel men's and women's programs (e.g., Exodus-affiliated women's
   ministries; Love Won Out's scope; NARTH's clinical reach), and how did materials/personnel/curricula
   flow between them?
2. Where is it documented that the SAME actors or networks pivoted from anti-gay/ex-gay work to anti-trans
   work (organizational continuity, shared personnel, shared funders, rebranded materials)? I need the
   strongest named chains, with dates.
3. Which specific *techniques or framings* demonstrably migrated across identities (e.g., "emotional
   dependency" for women vs "father-wound" for men as one gendered-diagnosis system; accountability/
   monitoring culture reused against trans people; "exploratory" language reused from ex-gay "no
   predetermined outcome" rhetoric)?
4. For each era, give 2–3 CONCRETE documented incidents or artifacts (a conference, a curriculum, a
   campaign, a platform policy) that involve two or more identities at once — these become the anchors for
   cross-cluster challenges.
