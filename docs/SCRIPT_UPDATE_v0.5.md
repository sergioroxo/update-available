## 1. WHY THE UPDATE HAPPENS — the trigger logic (Sérgio's question, answered)

Sérgio asked: is the update caused by player failure? a societal shift? the
breakage of an organization? The answer with the strongest rhetorical and
historical footing:

> **The system updates because it failed — and it never says so.**
> The player never causes an update. Each update is triggered by a
> **documented breakage** of the practice itself, and the "update" is how
> failure is rebranded as improvement.

### The trigger table (era → documented breakage → the patch)

| Update | The breakage (witness side / in-world news, just before) | The patch language (the changelog) |
|---|---|---|
| → Era 2 | The flagship "change" ministry **collapses**; its own leader concedes change didn't happen and apologizes (the Exodus-shaped event, abstracted per no-real-persons rule; org names where documented per canon — RESEARCH_BASE §12 timeline) `[VERIFY SOURCE]` exact wording before ship | *"'Cure' is deprecated. Introducing: support for unwanted attractions."* |
| → Era 3 | **Bans begin** (the Malta-first wave, 2016 →); courts and health bodies condemn; "reparative therapy" becomes legally radioactive | *"We've heard you. Nothing needs fixing — introducing Flourishing™. Not therapy. Results may vary."* |
| → Era 4 | The movement's **own framing collapses culturally** (Pride-era visibility; survivor testimony; the apology genre) — and the **anti-trans pivot** opens a new market | *"A new conversation is available. Slower. More thoughtful. No outcomes favoured."* (No satire inside the clinical-debate content itself — ethics hard line; the satire is that the *changelog format* carries it.) |

**Why this beats "player failure":** if the player's behavior triggered
updates, the piece would say *you bring this on yourself* — the
victim-blaming trap, mechanized. The historical-failure trigger says the
opposite and true thing: **the practice never worked, was repeatedly caught
not working, and survived every exposure by renaming itself.** "The system
does not stop" gets its causal engine: it doesn't stop *because failure is
its growth strategy*.

**Staging:** each breakage is witnessed, not narrated — on the witness side
(or a news ticker crossing the victim desktop) the player sees the collapse
*before* the update notification arrives. The sequence of feeling: *they got
caught → relief → "An update is available."* That gut-drop is the rebranding
thesis, felt in order.

**Boot screens carry the history (Sérgio's note):** each post-update boot
shows one line of period-grounded context in BIOS voice (*"v2.0 · est. after
the closure of [the ministry] · serving families since…"*) — the time-travel
logic surfaced in the machine's own self-description.

**Agent check (as requested):** before EULA/changelog texts are finalized, run
one focused deep-research pass: *"For each rebranding era, identify the
documented failure/scandal/ban that preceded the new language, with primary
sources and exact dates"* — extends PROMPTS §A1 item 7; most material already
in `Sources/Deep Research/`; the gap is precise dates + quotable patch-style
phrasings. → log in PROCESS_REGISTER when run.

## 3. AESTHETIC CORRECTION — Soft Lo-Fi doctrine (replaces "dark rooms"; fixes U6)

Sérgio's correction, adopted as law: **the rooms are not horror-dark.** Gloom
is itself a mood-manipulation tactic, and this piece doesn't get to use the
tools it critiques. Revised doctrine:

1. **Soft lo-fi space.** Low-poly, flat-shaded, *gentle* light: dusk, lamp
   warmth, window daylight by era. Cozy is allowed — coziness is honest
   (these things happened in ordinary warm bedrooms, not in bunkers).
2. **Underdefinition as material.** Away from the desk, meshes get **less
   defined — blurred, low-res, memory-soft** (vertex-fade / dithered
   softness, not GPU blur). The room is remembered, not surveilled. Cheap on
   Quest and distinctive.
3. **The inversion (load-bearing):** the **witness side is the sharp side** —
   crisp edges, full definition, administrative clarity. Surveillance is
   high-definition; life is soft. The flip's two facings now differ in
   *focus*, not in darkness — a kinder image and a harder argument.
4. **Darkness stays earned and local:** Stage 4 may go genuinely dark
   *because the era's own UX is dark-mode* — the gloom is diegetic, the
   room around it can stay soft. The 3am Pastor.AI scene reads *against* a
   soft room, not inside a haunted one.

**Equirect pack (U6 fix) — revised base string:**
> *Equirectangular 360-degree panorama, 2:1, of a small cozy bedroom-office,
> soft lo-fi low-poly game-art style, gentle dusk/lamp lighting, pastel-leaning
> quantized palette, furniture near the desk fully modeled while edges of the
> room dissolve into soft underdefined blurred low-poly forms, calm and
> intimate, NOT dark, NOT horror; behind the camera, a slightly crisper,
> cooler, more defined administrative space. No people, no logos, no text. —*
(Per-era variants from v0.4 §6 still apply; drop their "dark/near-black room"
phrasings except the diegetic era-4 screen glow.)

## 4. RESPITE — the rationale, recorded (Sérgio's framing, kept verbatim-close)

> Respite is *"a method of embracing the cognitive process of the user in
> retrieving the information — a sympathetic system to help produce more
> social awareness and less empathic rejection."*

Academic anchor (for the kappa): Imagine-Self perspective-taking raises
**personal distress**, and over-aroused distress produces withdrawal —
*empathic rejection* — rather than concern (Batson; RESEARCH_BASE §9). Respite
beats down-regulate arousal so the material can be *integrated* instead of
defended against. The dossier is the cognitive release valve; **respite is the
affective one.** Both are care infrastructure, not padding — cite alongside
Nash's immersive witnessing (witness under obligation needs somewhere to
breathe or the obligation collapses into flight).

## 5. TRANSJESUS — the corrupted file that survives (his three options, merged)

Sérgio offered three statuses: corrupted file / something that labels and
targets you / something that survives the oppression. **All three, one
design — with the ethics line intact:**

- **Found, not offered:** in Era 2/3 the player finds `transjesus.str` —
  flagged `corrupted · unclassifiable` by the system. Opening it *works
  anyway*: a glitch-textured but **joyful** dance stream (pixel dance floor,
  chat scrolling warmth, queer-authored voice — *lugar de fala*).
- **It targets you — externally:** watching it writes to the ledger
  (witness side: `subject accessed unclassifiable content · flag applied`).
  True to life: visible queer joy *is* a data point. **The targeting happens
  around the stream, never through it** — the space itself never betrays
  (the non-negotiable from v0.4; the trap reading stays structurally
  impossible).
- **It survives the updates:** every era-update tries to quarantine/delete
  it; every era it's still there — re-skinned by time, more glitch-grain,
  still dancing. The corruption is the *system's* fingerprints on it, not
  decay. (Queer archive logic: what survives deletion carries its scars.)
- **The payoff:** at the Close, it plays **clean** for the first time — the
  one file the restart restores rather than wipes. Dossier card: counter-media
  and survivor-made joy as resistance infrastructure (links to the real
  archive's counter-media strand). ⚑ tone/consultation per ETHICS before
  text ships (U3, with Sérgio).
