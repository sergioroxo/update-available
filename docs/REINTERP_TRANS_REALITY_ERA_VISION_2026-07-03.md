## 1. The shape of the argument across four eras

The research already tells a story if you read the four eras in sequence, not as four separate cells:
**the apparatus's trans category starts as an absence and becomes an increasingly specific, increasingly
differentiated machine.** That arc IS the thing worth staging — not four static rooms, but one room that
visibly learns to see (and mis-see) more precisely as the decades pass. That's the argument for fluidity
over a fixed split: a static tinted room says "these are the three trans experiences"; a *shifting* room
says "watch the apparatus's categories multiply and sharpen" — which is closer to what actually happened
and closer to the piece's thesis (rebrand-not-death, the apparatus recycling and refining itself).

## 2. Era by era

### E1 · 1997 — the room is mostly dark, and that's the content
**What the research supports:** both trans facets are absence-shaped in 1997 — trans-man: "erasure, stage
as absence" (R6-1); trans-woman: sparse, a photocopied Exodus referral list routing trans people into male
mentoring "as severe male deviance, never met as a woman" (R6-1). The one legible thing in 1997 isn't
identity-specific at all — it's structural: **the ministry pathway itself required a mentor of the same
assigned sex before anything else could happen** (R6-3's forced-binarisation motif, '90s instance). That
rule doesn't need a trans-specific artifact to stage; it's baked into how the whole apparatus already
sorts everyone.
**Creative proposal:** E1's fluid room defaults to near-dark, structurally legible only — a hallway/
threshold read rather than a populated room. The absence is not a placeholder for content we haven't
written yet; it *is* the content, and saying so plainly (a caption, or the room's own bare quality) turns
a research gap into a deliberate beat, the same move R6-1 already validated for the trans-man alcove
generally. **Imaginative potential:** the one thing that IS visible in this near-dark room could be the
same-sex-mentor rule rendered almost like a locked door — the player can see there's a room, can't yet see
who's in it, because the apparatus itself couldn't yet either.

### E2 · 2003 — leans trans-feminine, structurally, with a first cross-cluster thread
**What the research supports:** trans-woman '03 anchor is strong and disturbing — a "homosexual
continuum" counselling diagram, folding transfemininity into moral-warning imagery (R6-1: "Leach's Lucifer
imagery, dossier-only"). Trans-man '03 stays erasure (R6-1, unchanged from '97). The forced-binarisation
motif's '00s instance is the paired male/female conference-track structure (R6-3) — you enter only by
translating into one side, which resonates directly with E2's own registration/intake aesthetic (Restorify
onboarding, the iceberg diagram already built).
**Creative proposal:** E2 is the era where the fluid room's DEFAULT should lean trans-feminine — the
continuum diagram is a strong, era-appropriate object (period counseling literature genre, matches E2's
clinical/systematized register). The paired-tracks motif can live as a literal locked binary field inside
an intake-style interface, cheap to build and thematically exact. **Imaginative potential — a genuine
cross-cluster thread (F1), flagged as a proposal, not a call:** Daniel's own accountability-partner web in
E2 plausibly cross-references contemporaneous "female-to-male" case literature (the era's own clinical
habit of cataloguing "presenting problems") — a brief, light send from Daniel's lead room into the
trans-man facet's near-emptiness, which would let the room's absence be *witnessed from inside the story*
rather than only from outside it. Worth testing on paper before any build.

### E3 · 2016 — the era the room can finally hold all three at once, and it's earned
**What the research supports:** this is the strongest cell in the whole grid. Trans-woman '16: the
apparatus's HOPE 2016 audio page runs "What is Gender & How Can One Be Gender Secure?" beside Denise
Shick's family-testimony tracks and circulating Heyer-era regret content — and R6-1's own framing of this
is exact and devastating: **"the apparatus addresses parents/pastors/audiences AROUND trans women, not
trans women as subjects — her alcove can stage material ABOUT her circulating while she is absent from its
address."** That should be the standout beat of E3's trans-feminine facet — a room full of people talking
about someone who was never invited into it. Trans-man '16: the parent/family resource stack "starts
naming him, but still wrongly" (R6-1) — the first naming, still incorrect, a real shift from pure absence.
Non-binary '16: the forced-binarisation motif's '10s instance is ministry **buckets** — male / female /
transgender / parents (R6-3) — note that "transgender" gets its *own bucket* for the first time in this
decade, a real escalation from the pure-binary structure of the earlier eras.
**Creative proposal:** 2016 is exactly when the apparatus's own sorting categories multiply enough to
plausibly hold all three facets in the room at once — E3 is where the "three at once" convergence state
is dramaturgically *earned*, not just permitted. **A genuine gift worth naming:** 2016 is also when the
full Side A/B/X/Y chart debuts complete (Sides coined March 2016, R4-1) inside Vera's own lead room. The
trans room's facets multiplying in the SAME year the chart multiplies its own categories is not a
coincidence worth wasting — the two should visibly rhyme (the same instinct to sort into more boxes,
staged twice, once as Vera's chart and once as the room's architecture). If the geometry can afford it,
this is the era to prototype the "all three at once" state first.

### E4 · present — does this era even need a separate trans-woman facet?
**What the research supports:** present-day trans-man material is the recovery/detrans/parent-support
ecosystem (R6-1) — real, but entirely inside G1's zone (c), not buildable without the reader consult.
Present-day non-binary: the forced-binarisation motif is a genuinely sharp irony — **separate on-demand
courses still exist for "males" and "women"** even now, in a culture with much broader non-binary
visibility than any prior era (R6-3) — the apparatus's binary insistence has outlasted its own audience's
categories. Trans-woman present-day is Maya — the piece's LEAD for this era, already richly developed
canon, not a peripheral alcove.
**Creative proposal, flagged explicitly as a question rather than a call:** does E4's fluid trans room need
a trans-feminine facet at all, structurally? Maya's own full room may already BE that facet — R8-1's
existing note that "Era 4 converges" (the cluster tightens around Maya) already gestures at this. The
economical, thematically apt answer might be: **E4's alcove holds only trans-man (gated) and non-binary
(buildable now) orbiting Maya's already-full room**, with the convergence expressed as the alcove's
lighting/state visibly syncing toward Maya's room rather than needing its own separate trans-woman content.
This resolves the "does Maya's centrality compete with the alcove" question for free, if it holds up once
the geometry is sketched.

## 3. A proposed switching mechanism (the open Q2 from Round 12, answered as a creative proposal)

Not locked — offered because you asked for imaginative follow-through, not just organized research:

- **Each era carries a DEFAULT facet-weighting**, set by which pressure the documentary record makes
  clearest for that year (per §2 above): E1 mostly dark/structural, E2 leans trans-feminine, E3 capable of
  holding all three, E4 leans trans-man+non-binary orbiting Maya's room.
- **The default can be PULLED toward a non-default facet** by two mechanisms already in canon: a
  cross-cluster storyline send (F1 — an era's lead room sends the player in for a specific beat, as
  sketched for E2 above) or sustained player gaze/dwell (the existing gaze-only spotlight law, ◆2).
- **The "all three at once" state is reserved for earned convergence moments** — E3 primarily (per §2's
  chart-rhyme argument), and possibly recurring once more at the Close, where the point-cloud's warm
  constellation (F6) could visibly gather all three facets' threads together as one of its many light
  points — worth flagging to whoever eventually designs the Close's final beat-by-beat sequence.
- This keeps the room genuinely data-driven (a facet-weighting value per era + pull triggers), which fits
  the piece's existing architecture discipline (tags, not hardcoded splits) and Sérgio's own stated
  preference for flexible, data-driven creative architecture over locked binaries.
