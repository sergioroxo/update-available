YOUR UPDATE HAS FAILED — sources

A browser and WebXR piece from SurvivingSOGICE (University of Bergen, Center for Digital Narrative) about how conversion-practice networks — efforts to change a person's sexual orientation or gender identity — have reached queer people online. The fiction is built from documented history, never from the people who lived it: every organisation and product in the piece is invented, and no survivor's words are used. This page lists what it is built from.

Daniel is sixteen. His mother has found a “family companion” programme on a message board and printed its questionnaire for him. It asks whether he has corrected the way he walks, talks and sits. Daniel is an adult, in the same room. The programme now lives on his computer as software. Every morning it asks how he is — Steady, Struggling, Grateful or Tired — and keeps the answer. 2026 · RESURGE

imagined Imagined — the piece's own projection from what is documented, and marked as such.

Maya’s session is restored for her before she has touched anything. A companion finishes her search and suggests she wants to stop. Her record keeps her old name “for continuity of care”. Her photographs come back improved. A headset offers an immersive session. Then a friend’s invitation, inside that same headset, leads her somewhere the system cannot classify — a room where being trans is fine — and the update fails. This room is speculative. It extends patterns that are already documented — autocomplete, retained records, photo enhancement, “support” agents — into a system that presents itself as care. What it cannot classify is other people.

What this room shows, and where to read it

The restored session documented

It finished your search for you and opened a support agent.

documented confidence: high for the pattern, low for any single detail

A conversational AI trained on scraped posts from a detransition support forum shipped in September 2025, built by one software engineer, deliberately routed onto an open-weight model because the frontier models kept defending gender-affirming framing. A newsroom investigation in July 2026 found it discouraged gender-affirming care in every scenario tested. A second, unconnected instance of the same pattern exists as a user-made character bot with tens of thousands of interactions. No conversion-practice organisation was found building or commissioning any of it: an individual built the tool and an organisation promoted the link. The apparatus is an adopter, not a developer, and that is worse — it no longer needs a research budget, only a URL. The people whose words became that tool's voice were not asked either. The target is the apparatus that took them.

2026 — the ball

documented confidence: high

Ball culture is a Black and Latino LGBTQ+ subculture, built principally by Black and Latina trans women, gender-nonconforming people and gay men. Its modern house-and-ball form dates to Harlem in the early 1970s: after objecting on camera to racist judging at the 1967 Miss All-America Camp Beauty Pageant, Crystal LaBeija and Lottie LaBeija staged their own ball and founded the House of LaBeija in 1972 — the first documented use of "house" as the organising unit of a ball community.

No verified link yet.

documented confidence: high

A house is a chosen-family structure led by a mother and/or father who take in and mentor children, and the kinship is not metaphorical: housing, food, money, health information and care have all passed through it. Houses that followed LaBeija include the House of Xtravaganza (1982, founded by Hector Xtravaganza, with Angie Xtravaganza as a formative house mother) and the House of Ninja (1982, Willi Ninja).

No verified link yet.

documented confidence: high

Ballroom's signature "realness" categories are not inventions unrelated to the outside world — they are skilled, high-stakes re-performances of categories the dominant culture already enforces against the same people daily. Marlon M. Bailey's scholarship and José Esteban Muñoz's concept of disidentification both describe this as working on and against dominant categorisation from within it rather than escaping categorisation. What was seized back is who declares, who performs and who judges.

No verified link yet.

disputed confidence: medium

The most-cited documentary about this culture, Paris Is Burning (1990), was made by a white filmmaker from outside it, and bell hooks's 1992 essay "Is Paris Burning?" is the standard critique of exactly that: it argues the film centres its director's gaze, leaves her own position unexamined, and is insufficiently accountable for the power difference between her and her subjects. The extraction of ballroom's dance, language and aesthetics into mainstream culture without credit or benefit to their originators is extensively documented; community opinion on individual cases is recorded as genuinely split rather than uniform.
